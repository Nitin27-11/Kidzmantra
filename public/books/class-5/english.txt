## The eight parts of speech
Every word in a sentence does a job. These jobs are called **parts of speech**.
1. **Noun** – names a person, place, thing or idea: Asha, Delhi, kite, honesty.
2. **Pronoun** – replaces a noun: she, they, it, mine.
3. **Verb** – shows action or being: run, is, think.
4. **Adjective** – describes a noun: tall, red, five.
5. **Adverb** – describes a verb, adjective or another adverb: quickly, very, never.
6. **Preposition** – shows relation: in, on, under, after.
7. **Conjunction** – joins words or sentences: and, but, because.
8. **Interjection** – shows sudden feeling: Wow! Oh! Alas! Hurray!

> 💡 A word's part of speech depends on how it is used in the sentence.

## Two parts of a sentence
Every sentence has two parts:
- **Subject** – who or what the sentence is about.
- **Predicate** – what the subject does or is; everything else.

SAY: riya-happy | "The dog barked." The dog is the subject!
SAY: buddy-happy | And "barked" is the predicate – what the subject does.

## Examples
- **The old man** | walked slowly to the temple.
- **My little brother** | loves chocolates.
- **The sun** | rises in the east.

## Finding the subject
Ask "**Who?**" or "**What?**" before the verb.
"The birds sang sweetly." – Who sang? **The birds** (subject).

SAY: aarav-think | In "Sit down!", where's the subject?
SAY: buddy-happy | It's hidden – "you" is understood!

## Hidden subject
In commands, the subject "you" is hidden: "(You) Shut the door."

## Inverted sentences
Sometimes the subject comes later: "Into the room ran **the dog**."
> 💡 The subject and the verb must agree: "The boy **plays**" but "The boys **play**".

## The twelve tenses
Each of the three times (present, past, future) has four forms:
- **Simple:** I write. / I wrote. / I will write.
- **Continuous:** I am writing. / I was writing. / I will be writing.
- **Perfect:** I have written. / I had written. / I will have written.
- **Perfect continuous:** I have been writing. / I had been writing. / I will have been writing.

SAY: aarav-think | When do I say "I have eaten"?
SAY: buddy-happy | Use the present perfect for actions just finished or still important now.

## When to use the perfect tense
- **Present perfect:** an action just completed or with an effect now – "I **have finished** my homework."
- **Past perfect:** the earlier of two past actions – "The train **had left** before we **reached**."

SAY: riya-happy | "Since 2020" for a starting point, "for two years" for a length of time!

## Since and for
- **Since** + a point of time: since Monday, since 2020.
- **For** + a length of time: for two hours, for five years.
"She has been studying **for** two hours / **since** 4 o'clock."
> 💡 Present perfect never goes with "yesterday" or "last year" – use simple past instead.

## Active and passive voice
- **Active voice:** the subject **does** the action. "Ravi **wrote** a letter."
- **Passive voice:** the subject **receives** the action. "A letter **was written** by Ravi."

SAY: riya-happy | "Riya wrote a poem" becomes "A poem was written by Riya"!
SAY: buddy-happy | Yes! The object moves to the front in the passive voice.

## How to change active to passive
1. Make the **object** the new subject.
2. Use the correct form of **be** + **past participle** (3rd form) of the verb.
3. Put the old subject after **by**.

## Examples
- The cat **chased** the mouse. → The mouse **was chased** by the cat.
- Mother **cooks** food. → Food **is cooked** by Mother.
- They **will paint** the wall. → The wall **will be painted** by them.

SAY: aarav-think | We use passive when the doer isn't important!

## When we use passive
When the doer is unknown or less important: "My bag **was stolen**." "Rice **is grown** in Punjab."
> 💡 Pronouns change in passive: I → me, he → him, they → them, we → us.

## Let's check
Q: Change to passive: "Sita sings a song."
A: A song is sung by Sita.
Q: Change to passive: "The boys played football."
A: Football was played by the boys.
Q: Is "The window was broken" active or passive?
A: Passive.

## Direct and indirect speech
- **Direct speech:** the speaker's **exact words** in quotation marks. Rohan said, "**I am hungry.**"
- **Indirect (reported) speech:** we **report** what was said, without quotation marks. Rohan said that **he was hungry**.

SAY: aarav-happy | Riya said, "I am happy."
SAY: buddy-happy | Indirect: Riya said that she was happy. The tense and pronoun change!

## Rules for changing
1. Remove the quotation marks; add **that** (for statements).
2. Change pronouns to fit: I → he/she, my → his/her, we → they.
3. If the reporting verb is in the past, move the tense back: am/is → was, are → were, will → would, can → could.
4. Change words of time and place: now → then, today → that day, here → there, tomorrow → the next day.

SAY: riya-think | Questions use "asked if" or "asked what"!

## Questions and commands
- He asked, "Where do you live?" → He asked **where I lived**.
- She said to me, "Close the door." → She **told me to close** the door.
> 💡 If the reporting verb is in the present ("says"), the tense doesn't change.

## Let's check
Q: Change to indirect: Meena said, "I am tired."
A: Meena said that she was tired.
Q: Change to indirect: Teacher said, "Sit down."
A: The teacher told us to sit down.
Q: What does "tomorrow" become in indirect speech?
A: The next day.

## Formal letters
Formal letters are written to officials, like a principal, an editor or a company.

## Format
1. Sender's address and date
2. Receiver's designation and address
3. **Subject** line
4. Salutation: Sir/Madam,
5. Body – short and polite
6. Closing: Yours obediently / Yours faithfully, with name

## Sample: leave application
The Principal
Green Valley School, Lucknow
12 July 2026
**Subject: Application for sick leave**
Respected Ma'am,
I am Aditya Verma of Class 5-B. I have high fever and the doctor has advised me to rest for three days. Kindly grant me leave from 12 to 14 July.
Thanking you,
Yours obediently,
Aditya Verma, Class 5-B

SAY: riya-happy | An essay needs an introduction, body and conclusion!

## Writing an essay
An essay has an **introduction**, a **body** of two or three paragraphs, and a **conclusion**. Topics: "My Favourite Book", "Save Trees", "A Visit to a Zoo".
> 💡 Formal letters must be polite and to the point – no slang.

A: Introduction, body and conclusion.

## Where the Mind is Without Fear (lines by Rabindranath Tagore)
Where the mind is without fear and the head is held high;
Where knowledge is free;
Where the world has not been broken up into fragments
By narrow domestic walls;
Into that heaven of freedom, my Father, let my country awake.

SAY: buddy-happy | He wished for a nation where knowledge is free and people think clearly.

## What the poem means
Tagore wishes for an India where people are **fearless** and proud, where everyone can **learn freely**, and where people are not divided by caste, religion or language ("narrow domestic walls"). He prays that his country wakes up into true freedom.

SAY: aarav-happy | He was the first Asian to win the Nobel Prize!

## About the poet
Rabindranath Tagore wrote India's national anthem, "Jana Gana Mana". He won the **Nobel Prize for Literature** in 1913 – the first Asian to do so.

## Poetic devices
- **Metaphor:** "narrow domestic walls" stands for small-minded divisions.
- **Repetition:** "Where…" begins many lines for effect.
> 💡 Read the poem slowly and think about what freedom means to you.

## Let's check

A: Rabindranath Tagore.
Q: What do "narrow domestic walls" stand for?
A: Divisions among people, like caste, religion or language.
Q: Which prize did Tagore win in 1913?
A: The Nobel Prize for Literature.
