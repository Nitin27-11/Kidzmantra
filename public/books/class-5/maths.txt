- 1,00,000 = one lakh

## Order of operations
When a problem has more than one operation, follow **BODMAS**:
**B**rackets → **O**f → **D**ivision → **M**ultiplication → **A**ddition → **S**ubtraction

## Examples
- 8 + 4 × 3 = 8 + 12 = **20** (multiply first, not 12 × 3!)
- (8 + 4) × 3 = 12 × 3 = **36**
- 50 – 20 ÷ 5 = 50 – 4 = **46**

## Word problems with two steps
A school bought 25 boxes of chalk at ₹48 each and paid with ₹2,000. How much change?
Cost = 25 × 48 = ₹1,200. Change = 2,000 – 1,200 = **₹800**.

## Checking answers
- Addition ↔ subtraction are inverse operations.
- Multiplication ↔ division are inverse operations.
> 💡 Estimate first: 25 × 48 ≈ 25 × 50 = 1,250, so ₹1,200 makes sense.

## Let's check
Q: 15 + 6 × 2 = ?
A: 27.
Q: (15 + 6) × 2 = ?
A: 42.
Q: 100 – 36 ÷ 4 = ?
A: 91.

## HCF – Highest Common Factor
The **HCF** is the biggest number that divides two or more numbers exactly.
Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, **6**, 12
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, **6**, 9, 18
Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6 → **HCF = 6**

## LCM – Lowest Common Multiple
The **LCM** is the smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.
Multiples of 4: 4, 8, **12**, 16, 20, 24…
Multiples of 6: 6, **12**, 18, 24…
**LCM = 12**

## Prime factorisation method
12 = 2 × 2 × 3; 18 = 2 × 3 × 3
- HCF = common primes = 2 × 3 = **6**
- LCM = all primes, as many times as they appear most = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = **36**

## Where we use them
- **HCF:** cutting ribbons of 12 m and 18 m into the longest equal pieces → 6 m pieces.
- **LCM:** two bells ring every 4 and 6 minutes; they ring together every 12 minutes.
> 💡 HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers (for two numbers): 6 × 36 = 12 × 18 = 216.

## Let's check
Q: HCF of 16 and 24?
A: 8.
Q: LCM of 3 and 5?
A: 15.
Q: Two lights blink every 6 and 8 seconds. After how many seconds do they blink together?
A: 24 seconds (LCM).

## Comparing fractions
- Same denominator: bigger numerator is bigger. ⁵⁄₉ > ²⁄₉.
- Same numerator: smaller denominator is bigger. ³⁄₄ > ³⁄₈.
- Different: make the denominators the same using LCM. Compare ⅔ and ¾: ⁸⁄₁₂ and ⁹⁄₁₂ → ¾ is bigger.

## Adding and subtracting unlike fractions
½ + ⅓ = ³⁄₆ + ²⁄₆ = **⁵⁄₆**
¾ – ⅔ = ⁹⁄₁₂ – ⁸⁄₁₂ = **¹⁄₁₂**

## Multiplying fractions
Multiply tops, multiply bottoms: ⅔ × ¾ = ⁶⁄₁₂ = **½**.
Fraction of a number: ¾ of 20 = ¾ × 20 = **15**.

## Simplest form
Divide top and bottom by their HCF: ¹²⁄₁₈ → HCF 6 → **⅔**.

## Mixed numbers
2⅓ = (2 × 3 + 1)⁄3 = ⁷⁄₃.
> 💡 Always give answers in the simplest form.

## Let's check
Q: ⅖ + ⅓ = ?
A: ¹¹⁄₁₅.
Q: Simplify ¹⁵⁄₂₅.
A: ⅗.
Q: What is ⅔ of 27?
A: 18.

## What is percentage?
**Per cent** means "out of 100". The sign is **%**.
- 25% = ²⁵⁄₁₀₀ = ¼
- 50% = ½
- 75% = ¾
- 100% = the whole

## Converting
- Fraction → %: multiply by 100. ⅖ = ⅖ × 100 = **40%**
- Decimal → %: 0.35 = **35%**
- % → decimal: 8% = **0.08**

## Percentage of a number
20% of 150 = ²⁰⁄₁₀₀ × 150 = **30**

## In daily life
- Marks: 45 out of 50 = ⁴⁵⁄₅₀ × 100 = **90%**
- Discount: a ₹500 shirt at 10% off saves ₹50.
> 💡 10% of any number: just move the decimal point one place left. 10% of 340 = 34.

## Let's check
Q: Write ⅕ as a percentage.
A: 20%.
Q: What is 25% of 80?
A: 20.
Q: Asha got 36 out of 40. What is her percentage?
A: 90%.

## Measuring angles
An angle is measured in **degrees (°)** with a **protractor**.

## Types of angles
- **Acute:** less than 90°
- **Right:** exactly 90°
- **Obtuse:** between 90° and 180°
- **Straight:** exactly 180°
- **Reflex:** between 180° and 360°
- **Complete:** 360° (a full turn)

## Angles on a clock
- At 3 o'clock, the hands make a **right angle** (90°).
- At 6 o'clock, a **straight angle** (180°).
- Each hour mark is 30° apart (360° ÷ 12).

## Using a protractor
1. Place the centre point on the vertex.
2. Line up the base line with one arm.
3. Read the scale where the other arm crosses, starting from 0°.
> 💡 A quarter turn = 90°, a half turn = 180°, a full turn = 360°.

## Let's check
Q: What kind of angle is 150°?
A: Obtuse.
Q: What angle do clock hands make at 9 o'clock?
A: 90° (right angle).
Q: How many degrees in a full turn?
A: 360°.

## Triangles
A triangle has 3 sides, 3 angles and 3 vertices. **The angles always add up to 180°.**
By sides:
- **Equilateral:** all 3 sides equal (each angle 60°).
- **Isosceles:** 2 sides equal.
- **Scalene:** no sides equal.
By angles:
- **Acute-angled**, **right-angled** (one 90° angle), **obtuse-angled**.

## Quadrilaterals
Four-sided shapes. **Angles add up to 360°.**
- **Square:** 4 equal sides, 4 right angles.
- **Rectangle:** opposite sides equal, 4 right angles.
- **Parallelogram:** opposite sides equal and parallel.
- **Rhombus:** 4 equal sides; opposite sides parallel.
- **Trapezium:** one pair of parallel sides.

## Example
A triangle has angles 50° and 70°. The third angle = 180° – 50° – 70° = **60°**.
> 💡 Every square is a rectangle and a rhombus, but not every rectangle is a square.

## Let's check
Q: What do the angles of a triangle add up to?
A: 180°.
Q: A triangle with two equal sides is called?
A: Isosceles.
Q: Which quadrilateral has exactly one pair of parallel sides?
A: A trapezium.

## Perimeter and area
- **Rectangle:** perimeter = 2 × (l + b); area = l × b
- **Square:** perimeter = 4 × side; area = side × side
- **Triangle (right-angled):** area = ½ × base × height
Example: a rectangular field 30 m × 20 m: perimeter = 100 m; area = **600 sq m**.

## Volume
**Volume** is the space a solid takes up, measured in **cubic units** (cm³, m³).
- **Cuboid:** volume = length × breadth × height
- **Cube:** volume = side × side × side
Example: a box 5 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm → volume = **60 cm³**.

## Capacity
1 cm³ holds 1 mL. 1,000 cm³ = 1 litre.
> 💡 Area is flat (2-D); volume is solid (3-D).

## Let's check
Q: Area of a square with side 9 cm?
A: 81 sq cm.
Q: Volume of a cube with side 4 cm?
A: 64 cm³.
Q: Area of a right triangle with base 10 cm and height 6 cm?
A: 30 sq cm.

## Time
- 1 hour = 60 minutes; 1 minute = 60 seconds; so 1 hour = 3,600 seconds.
- 24-hour clock: 7:45 p.m. = 19:45.
- Duration: 10:40 to 14:15 → 3 h 35 min.

## Speed
**Speed** tells how fast something moves – the distance covered in one unit of time.
**Speed = Distance ÷ Time**
A car covers 180 km in 3 hours → speed = 180 ÷ 3 = **60 km/h**.

## Finding distance and time
- Distance = Speed × Time: a cyclist at 12 km/h for 2 hours goes **24 km**.
- Time = Distance ÷ Speed: 240 km at 80 km/h takes **3 hours**.
> 💡 Remember the triangle: D on top, S and T at the bottom. Cover what you need!

## Let's check
Q: A train travels 300 km in 5 hours. What is its speed?
A: 60 km/h.
Q: How far does a bus go in 4 hours at 45 km/h?
A: 180 km.
Q: How many seconds in 5 minutes?
A: 300 seconds.

## Collecting and reading data
We collect data, put it in a **table**, and show it with **graphs**.

## Mean (average)
**Average = total ÷ number of items**
Marks: 70, 80, 90, 60, 100 → total 400 → average = 400 ÷ 5 = **80**.

## Graphs
- **Bar graph:** compares amounts.
- **Line graph:** shows change over time, like temperature through a week.
- **Pie chart:** shows parts of a whole. A full circle = 360° = 100%.

## Reading a pie chart
If ½ of the class likes cricket, that slice is half the circle (180°).
> 💡 Always read the title, labels and scale before answering.

## Let's check
Q: Find the average of 10, 20 and 30.
A: 20.
Q: Which graph is best to show temperature changing over a week?
A: A line graph.
Q: In a pie chart, what does a quarter of the circle stand for?
A: 25% (90°).

## Patterns in numbers
- **Square numbers:** 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36 … (1×1, 2×2, 3×3 …)
- **Triangular numbers:** 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 … (add 2, then 3, then 4 …)
- **Odd numbers add up to squares:** 1 + 3 = 4, 1 + 3 + 5 = 9, 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16.

## Magic patterns
- 1 × 1 = 1; 11 × 11 = 121; 111 × 111 = 12,321
- 9 × 1 + 2 = 11; 9 × 12 + 3 = 111; 9 × 123 + 4 = 1,111

## Shape patterns
Patterns can **grow** (each step adds more), **repeat**, or **turn** (rotate by 90°).

## Find the rule
2, 6, 18, 54, … → multiply by 3 → **162**
> 💡 Patterns are everywhere: in sunflower seeds, honeycombs and rangoli designs.

## Let's check
Q: What is the next square number after 49?
A: 64.
Q: 1, 3, 6, 10, ___?
A: 15.
Q: 5, 10, 20, 40, ___?
A: 80.
