## Five- and six-digit numbers
After 9,999 comes **10,000** (ten thousand). After 99,999 comes **1,00,000** (one lakh).

## Indian place value chart
In 3,45,627:
- 3 lakhs, 4 ten thousands, 5 thousands, 6 hundreds, 2 tens, 7 ones
- Read as: **three lakh forty-five thousand six hundred twenty-seven**

## International system
The same number is written 345,627 and read as **three hundred forty-five thousand six hundred twenty-seven**.
- 1 lakh = 100 thousand
- 10 lakh = 1 million

## Rounding off
Round 3,468 to the nearest hundred: look at the tens digit (6). 6 ≥ 5 → round up → **3,500**.
Round 3,421 to the nearest hundred: tens digit 2 < 5 → round down → **3,400**.
> 💡 In the Indian system, commas come after the first 3 digits from the right, then after every 2 digits.

## Let's check
Q: Write "two lakh five thousand ten" in numbers.
A: 2,05,010.
Q: Round 7,850 to the nearest thousand.
A: 8,000.
Q: What is the place value of 6 in 6,24,315?
A: 6,00,000 (6 lakh).

## Long division
Division by one digit, step by step: **Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down**.

## Example: 756 ÷ 6
- 7 ÷ 6 = 1, 1 × 6 = 6, 7 – 6 = 1. Bring down 5 → 15.
- 15 ÷ 6 = 2, 2 × 6 = 12, 15 – 12 = 3. Bring down 6 → 36.
- 36 ÷ 6 = 6, 6 × 6 = 36, 36 – 36 = 0.
Answer: **126**

## With a remainder
845 ÷ 4 = **211 remainder 1**. Check: 211 × 4 + 1 = 845 ✔

## Dividing by 10 and 100
When a number ends in zeros: 4,500 ÷ 10 = 450; 4,500 ÷ 100 = 45.
> 💡 Remember "Does McDonald's Sell Burgers?" – Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down.

## Let's check
Q: 648 ÷ 8 = ?
A: 81.
Q: 1,000 ÷ 7 = ?
A: 142 remainder 6.
Q: 96 pencils are shared among 8 children. How many each?
A: 12 pencils.

## Factors
**Factors** of a number are numbers that divide it exactly (no remainder).
Factors of 12: **1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12** (because 1×12, 2×6, 3×4).

## Multiples
**Multiples** of a number are what you get when you multiply it by 1, 2, 3, …
Multiples of 4: **4, 8, 12, 16, 20, …** (they go on forever).

## Prime and composite numbers
- **Prime number:** has exactly two factors – 1 and itself. 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19…
- **Composite number:** has more than two factors. 4, 6, 8, 9, 10…
- 1 is neither prime nor composite. 2 is the only even prime.

## Divisibility rules
- By **2**: last digit is even.
- By **5**: last digit is 0 or 5.
- By **10**: last digit is 0.
- By **3**: sum of digits divisible by 3 (e.g. 123 → 1+2+3 = 6 ✔).
> 💡 Every number is a factor and a multiple of itself.

## Let's check
Q: List the factors of 18.
A: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.
Q: Write the first five multiples of 7.
A: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35.
Q: Is 15 prime or composite?
A: Composite (factors 1, 3, 5, 15).

## What is a fraction?
A **fraction** shows a part of a whole. In ¾, **3** is the **numerator** (parts taken) and **4** is the **denominator** (equal parts in all).

## Kinds of fractions
- **Proper fraction:** numerator < denominator – ²⁄₅
- **Improper fraction:** numerator ≥ denominator – ⁷⁄₄
- **Mixed fraction:** a whole and a part – 1¾ (= ⁷⁄₄)
- **Like fractions:** same denominator – ²⁄₇, ⁵⁄₇
- **Unlike fractions:** different denominators – ½, ⅓

## Equivalent fractions
Multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number: ½ = ²⁄₄ = ³⁄₆ = ⁴⁄₈.

## Adding and subtracting like fractions
Add or subtract the numerators; keep the denominator: ²⁄₇ + ³⁄₇ = ⁵⁄₇; ⁵⁄₈ – ²⁄₈ = ³⁄₈.
> 💡 With the same denominator, the bigger numerator is the bigger fraction.

## Let's check
Q: In ⁵⁄₉, what is the denominator?
A: 9.
Q: Write two equivalent fractions of ⅓.
A: ²⁄₆ and ³⁄₉.
Q: ³⁄₁₀ + ⁴⁄₁₀ = ?
A: ⁷⁄₁₀.

## Tenths and hundredths
A **decimal** is another way to write fractions with denominators 10, 100, 1000.
- ¹⁄₁₀ = **0.1** (one tenth)
- ¹⁄₁₀₀ = **0.01** (one hundredth)
- ⁷⁵⁄₁₀₀ = **0.75**

## Decimal point
In 23.45: 23 is the **whole number part**, and .45 is the **decimal part** (4 tenths and 5 hundredths).

## Decimals in daily life
- Money: ₹12.50 = 12 rupees 50 paise.
- Length: 1.5 m = 1 m 50 cm.
- Weight: 2.25 kg = 2 kg 250 g.

## Comparing decimals
Compare whole parts first, then tenths, then hundredths: 3.42 > 3.4 because 3.42 = 3.42 and 3.4 = 3.40.
> 💡 Adding zeros at the end of a decimal doesn't change it: 0.5 = 0.50.

## Let's check
Q: Write ³⁄₁₀ as a decimal.
A: 0.3.
Q: Write 0.07 as a fraction.
A: ⁷⁄₁₀₀.
Q: Which is greater: 0.6 or 0.58?
A: 0.6.

## Lines
- **Line segment:** has two end points. AB.
- **Ray:** has one end point and goes on forever in one direction, like a sunbeam.
- **Line:** goes on forever in both directions.
- **Parallel lines:** never meet, like railway tracks.
- **Intersecting lines:** cross each other at a point.
- **Perpendicular lines:** meet at a right angle, like the corner of a book.

## Angles
An **angle** is made when two rays meet at a point called the **vertex**.
- **Right angle:** exactly 90°, like the corner of a page.
- **Acute angle:** less than 90°.
- **Obtuse angle:** more than 90° but less than 180°.
- **Straight angle:** 180°.
> 💡 We measure angles in degrees (°) using a protractor.

## Let's check
Q: Which lines never meet?
A: Parallel lines.
Q: What is the measure of a right angle?
A: 90°.
Q: Is a 120° angle acute or obtuse?
A: Obtuse.

## Perimeter
**Perimeter** is the total length of the boundary of a shape.
- Rectangle: perimeter = 2 × (length + breadth)
- Square: perimeter = 4 × side
Example: a rectangle 8 cm by 5 cm → perimeter = 2 × (8 + 5) = **26 cm**.

## Area
**Area** is the amount of surface a shape covers. It is measured in **square units** (sq cm, cm²).
- Rectangle: area = length × breadth
- Square: area = side × side
Example: rectangle 8 cm by 5 cm → area = 8 × 5 = **40 sq cm**.

## Perimeter vs area
Fencing a garden needs **perimeter**. Covering a floor with tiles needs **area**.
> 💡 Count squares on squared paper to find area of any shape.

## Let's check
Q: Perimeter of a square with side 6 cm?
A: 24 cm.
Q: Area of a rectangle 7 m long and 4 m wide?
A: 28 sq m.
Q: To put a fence around a park, do we need area or perimeter?
A: Perimeter.

## Units of measurement
- **Length:** 10 mm = 1 cm; 100 cm = 1 m; 1,000 m = 1 km
- **Weight (mass):** 1,000 mg = 1 g; 1,000 g = 1 kg
- **Capacity:** 1,000 mL = 1 L

## Converting
- Big unit → small unit: **multiply**. 4 km = 4 × 1,000 = 4,000 m.
- Small unit → big unit: **divide**. 3,500 g = 3,500 ÷ 1,000 = 3.5 kg.

## Adding measurements
  5 kg 450 g
+ 2 kg 800 g
= 7 kg 1,250 g = **8 kg 250 g**
> 💡 Always convert to the same unit before adding or comparing.

## Let's check
Q: 7 m = ___ cm
A: 700 cm.
Q: 2,500 mL = ___ L
A: 2.5 L.
Q: Which is heavier: 1.5 kg or 1,400 g?
A: 1.5 kg (1,500 g).

## 24-hour clock
Railways and airports use the 24-hour clock so there is no confusion between a.m. and p.m.
- 9:00 a.m. = **09:00**
- 12:00 noon = **12:00**
- 3:30 p.m. = **15:30** (add 12 to p.m. hours)
- 11:45 p.m. = **23:45**
- Midnight = **00:00**

## Elapsed time
A train leaves at 08:45 and reaches at 13:15. Time taken: from 08:45 to 12:45 is 4 hours; 12:45 to 13:15 is 30 minutes → **4 h 30 min**.

## Calendar
- 7 days = 1 week, about 4 weeks = 1 month, 12 months = 1 year.
- Leap years come every 4 years; February then has 29 days. 2024 and 2028 are leap years.
> 💡 A century is 100 years; a decade is 10 years.

## Let's check
Q: Write 6:20 p.m. in 24-hour time.
A: 18:20.
Q: What is 21:00 in 12-hour time?
A: 9:00 p.m.
Q: How many years in a decade?
A: 10.

## Money and bills
Money is written with rupees and paise: ₹45.75 = 45 rupees 75 paise.

## Making a bill
- Notebook: 4 × ₹35 = ₹140
- Pencil box: 1 × ₹85 = ₹85
- Pens: 3 × ₹12.50 = ₹37.50
- **Total: ₹262.50**

## Unit price
If 6 pens cost ₹90, one pen costs ₹90 ÷ 6 = **₹15**.

## Profit and loss (introduction)
If a shopkeeper buys a toy for ₹200 and sells it for ₹250, he makes a **profit** of ₹50. If he sells it for ₹180, he has a **loss** of ₹20.
> 💡 Always check the bill and count your change.

## Collecting and showing data
**Data** is information collected to answer a question. We organise it in tables and graphs.

## Bar graph
A bar graph shows data using bars.
Example – books read in a month:
- Asha: 6
- Ben: 4
- Chitra: 8
- Dev: 5
The tallest bar (Chitra, 8) shows who read the most.

## Pie chart (introduction)
A circle divided into parts shows how a whole is shared – e.g. how you spend your day.

## Reading data
- Total books = 6 + 4 + 8 + 5 = **23**.
- Chitra read 4 more than Ben.
> 💡 Always label the axes and give your graph a title.

## Let's check
Q: Who read the fewest books?
A: Ben.
Q: How many more books did Asha read than Ben?
A: 2.
Q: What does a taller bar show?
A: A larger amount.
