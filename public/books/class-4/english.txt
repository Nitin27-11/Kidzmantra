## What is a noun?
A **noun** names a person, place, animal, thing or idea. Nouns can be sorted into kinds.

## Kinds of nouns
- **Proper noun** – a particular name: Sachin, Kerala, the Taj Mahal, Diwali.
- **Common noun** – a general name: player, state, building, festival.
- **Collective noun** – a group: a **pack** of wolves, a **swarm** of bees, a **fleet** of ships, a **bouquet** of flowers, a **class** of students.
- **Material noun** – what things are made of: gold, wood, cotton, water.
- **Abstract noun** – ideas or feelings we cannot touch: honesty, courage, joy, childhood.

## Countable and uncountable
- **Countable:** can be counted – one apple, two apples.
- **Uncountable:** cannot be counted – milk, sugar, advice. We say "some milk", not "two milks".
> 💡 Many abstract nouns come from adjectives: brave → bravery, kind → kindness, wise → wisdom.

## Let's check
Q: What kind of noun is "courage"?
A: An abstract noun.
Q: Give the collective noun: a ___ of bees.
A: swarm.
Q: Is "gold" a material noun?
A: Yes.

## Pronouns take the place of nouns
A **pronoun** replaces a noun so that we don't repeat it.

## Kinds of pronouns
- **Personal:** I, we, you, he, she, it, they (and me, us, him, her, them).
- **Possessive:** mine, ours, yours, his, hers, theirs. "The red cap is **mine**."
- **Reflexive:** myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves. "She hurt **herself**."
- **Demonstrative:** this, that, these, those. "**This** is my house."
- **Interrogative:** who, whom, whose, which, what. "**Who** is at the door?"

## Subject or object?
- Subject (does the action): **I, we, he, she, they** – "**She** called."
- Object (receives the action): **me, us, him, her, them** – "Mother called **her**."
> 💡 "This/these" for things near; "that/those" for things far away.

## Let's check
Q: Fill in: "We made the cake ___." (ourselves/us)
A: ourselves.
Q: Which pronoun asks about a person: who or which?
A: who.
Q: Choose: "The teacher praised (we/us)."
A: us.

## Tenses tell time
**Tense** shows when an action happens: present, past or future.

## Simple tenses
- **Simple present** – habits and facts: "Birds **fly**." "He **drinks** milk every day."
- **Simple past** – finished actions: "We **visited** Agra last year."
- **Simple future** – actions to come: "I **will meet** you tomorrow."

## Continuous tenses
These show actions going on:
- Present continuous: "She **is reading** now."
- Past continuous: "They **were playing** when it rained."
- Future continuous: "I **will be travelling** at 6 o'clock."

## Irregular past forms
begin–began, bring–brought, buy–bought, catch–caught, drink–drank, fly–flew, keep–kept, sing–sang, take–took, teach–taught.
> 💡 Continuous tenses use am/is/are/was/were/will be + verb-ing.

## Let's check
Q: Change to past: "She sings a song."
A: She sang a song.
Q: Which tense: "They were playing."?
A: Past continuous tense.
Q: Past form of "catch"?
A: caught.

## What is an adverb?
An **adverb** tells us more about a **verb** – **how**, **when**, **where** or **how often** something happens.

## Kinds of adverbs
- **How (manner):** slowly, quickly, carefully, happily – "The tortoise walked **slowly**."
- **When (time):** now, today, yesterday, soon – "We will leave **soon**."
- **Where (place):** here, there, inside, outside – "The children are playing **outside**."
- **How often (frequency):** always, often, never, sometimes – "She **always** smiles."

## Making adverbs
Many adverbs are made by adding **-ly** to adjectives: quick → quickly, soft → softly, happy → happily, careful → carefully.
> 💡 Not all -ly words are adverbs: "friendly" and "lovely" are adjectives.

## Let's check
Q: Find the adverb: "He spoke loudly."
A: loudly.
Q: What does "yesterday" tell us – how, when or where?
A: When.
Q: Make an adverb from "brave".
A: bravely.

## Prepositions show position
A **preposition** shows the relation of a noun to another word – often **where** or **when**.

## Prepositions of place
- **in** – inside: The fish is **in** the bowl.
- **on** – touching the top: The book is **on** the table.
- **under** – below: The cat is **under** the bed.
- **between** – in the middle of two: Sit **between** Raj and Mira.
- **behind / in front of / near / beside / above / below**.

## Prepositions of time
- **at** for exact times: **at** 6 o'clock.
- **on** for days and dates: **on** Monday, **on** 15 August.
- **in** for months, years, seasons and parts of the day: **in** May, **in** 2026, **in** the morning.

## Prepositions of movement
into, out of, across, through, over, along. "The train went **through** the tunnel."
> 💡 "Among" is for more than two; "between" is for two.

## Let's check
Q: Fill in: "My birthday is ___ June." (on/in)
A: in.
Q: Fill in: "The ball rolled ___ the road." (across/at)
A: across.
Q: Fill in: "Share the sweets ___ all the children." (between/among)
A: among.

@@ 6
## Conjunctions join
A **conjunction** is a joining word. It joins words or sentences.

## Common conjunctions
- **and** – adds: "I like tea **and** biscuits."
- **but** – shows contrast: "He is poor **but** honest."
- **or** – gives a choice: "Would you like tea **or** coffee?"
- **because** – gives a reason: "She stayed home **because** she was ill."
- **so** – shows a result: "It was raining, **so** we took umbrellas."
- **when, while, until, although, if**.

## Joining sentences
"Ravi is tall. Ravi is strong." → "Ravi is tall **and** strong."
"I was hungry. I ate an apple." → "I was hungry, **so** I ate an apple."
> 💡 "Because" answers "why?"; "so" tells what happened next.

## Let's check
Q: Join: "She ran fast. She missed the bus." (but)
A: She ran fast but missed the bus.
Q: Choose: "Take an umbrella ___ it may rain." (because/but)
A: because.
Q: Which conjunction gives a choice?
A: or.

## Reading carefully
Comprehension means understanding a passage and answering questions about it.

## Read the passage: Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam
Abdul Kalam was born in 1931 in Rameswaram, a small town in Tamil Nadu. As a boy, he sold newspapers to help his family. He loved watching birds fly and dreamt of flying himself.
Kalam studied hard and became a scientist. He helped India make rockets and missiles, so he is called the **Missile Man of India**. In 2002, he became the **President of India**. He loved children and often told them, "Dream, dream, dream!"

## Word meanings
- **missile** – a weapon that flies through the air towards a target
- **President** – the head of our country

## Answering tips
Look for key words from the question in the passage. Write answers in complete sentences.
> 💡 Kalam's birthday, 15 October, is celebrated as World Students' Day.

## Let's check
Q: Where was Abdul Kalam born?
A: He was born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.
Q: Why is he called the Missile Man of India?
A: Because he helped India make rockets and missiles.
Q: What did he tell children?
A: "Dream, dream, dream!"

## A good paragraph
A paragraph is about **one main idea**.
1. **Topic sentence** – tells the main idea.
2. **Supporting sentences** – give details and examples.
3. **Concluding sentence** – sums up.

Example – **My School**: My school is a big, beautiful building. It has a library, a science lab and a large playground. Our teachers are kind and help us learn. I love my school.

## Writing a story
- **Beginning:** who, where, when.
- **Middle:** a problem.
- **End:** how the problem is solved, and a moral.

## Story outline to try
A boy finds a lost puppy → he looks for its owner → he puts up posters → a little girl comes, crying with joy → moral: kindness brings happiness.
> 💡 Use describing words and feelings to make your story come alive.

## Let's check
Q: What does a topic sentence do?
A: It tells the main idea of the paragraph.
Q: Name the three parts of a story.
A: Beginning, middle and end.

## The Brook (adapted lines by Alfred Tennyson)
I chatter over stony ways,
In little sharps and trebles,
I bubble into eddying bays,
I babble on the pebbles.
For men may come and men may go,
But I go on for ever.

## What the poem means
A **brook** (small stream) is speaking. It makes happy sounds as it flows over stones and pebbles. People are born and die, but the brook keeps flowing forever.

## Poetic devices
- **Personification:** giving human qualities to non-living things – the brook "chatters" and "babbles".
- **Sound words:** bubble, babble, chatter.
- **Rhyme:** trebles – pebbles; go – (for) ever.
> 💡 Read the poem aloud slowly. Can you hear the water?

## Let's check
Q: Who is speaking in the poem?
A: The brook (a small stream).
Q: What is personification?
A: Giving human qualities to non-living things.
Q: What does the brook mean by "I go on for ever"?
A: It keeps flowing forever, while people come and go.
