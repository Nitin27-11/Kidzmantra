## Our five senses
Our **sense organs** tell our brain what is happening around us.
- **Eyes** – sight. Light enters the eye, and the brain makes a picture.
- **Ears** – hearing. Sound travels into the ear and makes the eardrum vibrate.
- **Nose** – smell. It also cleans the air we breathe with tiny hairs.
- **Tongue** – taste. Taste buds sense sweet, salty, sour and bitter.
- **Skin** – touch. It feels heat, cold, pain, pressure and texture. It is the largest organ of the body.

## Senses in animals
- Eagles can see a rabbit from very high up.
- Dogs smell thousands of times better than we do.
- Bats use echoes of sound to find their way in the dark.
- Snakes feel vibrations from the ground.

## Caring for sense organs
Don't read in dim light, don't put sharp things in your ears, wash your face daily, and protect your skin from strong sun.
> 💡 People who cannot see read using **Braille** – raised dots felt with fingertips.

## Let's check
Q: Which is the largest organ of the body?
A: The skin.
Q: What do taste buds sense?
A: Sweet, salty, sour and bitter tastes.
Q: How do bats find their way in the dark?
A: By using echoes of sounds they make.

## The journey of food
**Digestion** is the process of breaking food into very small parts that the body can use.

## Parts of the digestive system
1. **Mouth** – teeth chew food; **saliva** makes it wet and starts digestion.
2. **Food pipe (oesophagus)** – carries food to the stomach.
3. **Stomach** – churns food and mixes it with digestive juices.
4. **Small intestine** – digested food is absorbed into the blood here. It is about 6–7 metres long!
5. **Large intestine** – absorbs water; waste leaves the body.

## Healthy digestion
- Chew food well – about 30 times!
- Eat fibre-rich food: fruits, vegetables, whole grains.
- Drink enough water.
- Don't run or swim right after a heavy meal.
> 💡 It takes many hours for a meal to pass completely through the digestive system.

## Let's check
Q: Where does digestion begin?
A: In the mouth.
Q: Where is digested food absorbed into the blood?
A: In the small intestine.
Q: What is the job of the large intestine?
A: To absorb water and remove waste.

## Our teeth
Teeth help us **bite**, **chew** and **speak** clearly.

## Two sets of teeth
- **Milk teeth:** 20 teeth that come in childhood and start falling around age 6.
- **Permanent teeth:** 32 teeth that replace milk teeth and stay for life.

## Kinds of teeth
- **Incisors** (8) – sharp, flat front teeth for **cutting**.
- **Canines** (4) – pointed teeth for **tearing**.
- **Premolars** (8) and **molars** (12) – broad teeth at the back for **grinding** and **chewing**.

## Caring for teeth
- Brush twice a day and rinse after every meal.
- Eat less sticky sweets and chocolates.
- Visit a dentist regularly.
- Tooth decay is caused by germs feeding on food left on teeth.
> 💡 Enamel, the outer layer of the tooth, is the hardest substance in the body.

## Let's check
Q: How many permanent teeth do adults have?
A: 32.
Q: Which teeth are used for tearing?
A: Canines.
Q: What causes tooth decay?
A: Germs that feed on food left on teeth.

## Seeds and how they grow
A seed has a tiny baby plant inside it, with stored food and a protective **seed coat**.

## Germination
When a seed gets **water**, **air** and **warmth**, it starts to grow. This is called **germination**.
1. The seed soaks water and swells.
2. A tiny root comes out first and grows downward.
3. A shoot grows upward towards light.
4. Leaves open and start making food.

## How seeds travel (dispersal)
Plants spread their seeds far away so new plants have space:
- **Wind:** light, winged or fluffy seeds – dandelion, cotton, maple.
- **Water:** seeds that float – coconut, lotus.
- **Animals:** sticky or hooked seeds stick to fur; birds eat fruits and drop seeds.
- **Bursting:** pods burst open – peas, balsam.

## Experiment
Put moong seeds on wet cotton. In 2–3 days you'll see them sprout!
> 💡 Some seeds stay alive for hundreds of years before germinating.

## Let's check
Q: What three things does a seed need to germinate?
A: Water, air and warmth.
Q: Which part comes out of the seed first?
A: The root.
Q: How are coconut seeds dispersed?
A: By water.

## Habitats
A **habitat** is the natural home of a plant or animal, where it gets food, water and shelter.

## Kinds of habitats
- **Forests:** tigers, monkeys, deer, many birds.
- **Deserts:** camels, lizards, cacti. Camels store fat in their humps and can go without water for days.
- **Polar regions:** polar bears and penguins. Thick fur and fat keep them warm.
- **Mountains:** yaks and snow leopards with thick fur.
- **Water:** fish breathe through **gills**; ducks have webbed feet for swimming.
- **Grasslands:** zebras, deer, lions.

## Adaptation
Special features that help animals live in their habitat are called **adaptations**.
- Fish have streamlined bodies to swim.
- Woodpeckers have strong beaks to make holes in trees.
- Frogs live on land and in water (they are **amphibians**).
> 💡 Cutting forests destroys habitats. Many animals are now endangered.

## Let's check
Q: What is a habitat?
A: The natural home of a plant or animal.
Q: How do fish breathe?
A: Through gills.
Q: How is a camel adapted to the desert?
A: It stores fat in its hump and can go without water for days; it has long legs and thick eyelashes against sand.

## Uses of water
Drinking, cooking, bathing, cleaning, farming (irrigation), making electricity, factories, transport by boats and ships.

## Water around us
About three-fourths of the Earth is covered with water, but most of it is salty sea water. Only a tiny part is fresh water we can use.

## Clean and dirty water
Dirty water can carry germs that cause diseases like cholera, typhoid and diarrhoea.
Ways to clean water:
- **Sedimentation:** let mud settle at the bottom.
- **Filtration:** pass water through cloth or a filter.
- **Boiling:** kills germs.
- **Chlorine tablets:** kill germs.

## Saving water
Fix leaking taps, collect rainwater, water plants in the evening, reuse water.
> 💡 Irrigation means supplying water to crops by canals, wells or sprinklers.

## Let's check
Q: Name two diseases caused by dirty water.
A: Cholera, typhoid or diarrhoea.
Q: What does boiling water do?
A: It kills germs.
Q: Why is little of Earth's water fit to drink?
A: Most of it is salty sea water.

## Air is all around us
Air is a mixture of gases: mainly **nitrogen** (about 78%), **oxygen** (about 21%), and small amounts of carbon dioxide, water vapour and other gases.

## Air has weight and takes up space
- A blown-up balloon is heavier than an empty one.
- An "empty" bottle pushed upside down into water doesn't fill completely – air takes up space.

## Why air is important
- We breathe in **oxygen** and breathe out **carbon dioxide**.
- Plants use carbon dioxide to make food and give out oxygen.
- Fire needs oxygen to burn.
- Wind turns windmills, dries clothes and moves sailboats.

## Air pollution
Smoke from vehicles, factories and burning garbage pollutes air and causes breathing problems.
> 💡 Air quality is measured with an AQI – Air Quality Index. Lower is better!

## Let's check
Q: Which gas is the largest part of air?
A: Nitrogen.
Q: Which gas do we breathe in to live?
A: Oxygen.
Q: Give one cause of air pollution.
A: Smoke from vehicles, factories or burning garbage.

## Why we wear clothes
Clothes protect us from heat, cold, rain, dust and insects. They also show culture and occasions.

## Clothes for seasons
- **Summer:** light-coloured cotton clothes – they absorb sweat and keep us cool.
- **Winter:** woollen clothes – they trap warm air and keep us warm.
- **Rainy season:** raincoats and gumboots made of waterproof material.

## Where fabric comes from
- **Natural fibres:** cotton (cotton plant), jute (jute plant), wool (sheep, goats), silk (silkworm).
- **Man-made fibres:** nylon, polyester, rayon – made in factories.

## Special clothes
Doctors wear white coats, firefighters wear fire-proof suits, soldiers wear uniforms, and players wear sports kits.

## Traditional clothes of India
Saree, dhoti, kurta-pyjama, salwar-kameez, lehenga, mundu, phiran (Kashmir).
> 💡 Dark colours absorb more heat; light colours reflect heat.

## Let's check
Q: Why do we wear cotton in summer?
A: It absorbs sweat and keeps us cool.
Q: Where does silk come from?
A: From the silkworm.
Q: Name one man-made fibre.
A: Nylon, polyester or rayon.

## Reading maps
A **map** shows places on a flat surface, as seen from above.

## Parts of a map
- **Title:** what the map shows.
- **Directions:** usually shown by an arrow pointing **North** (N).
- **Key or legend:** explains symbols and colours.
- **Scale:** tells how distance on the map compares with real distance (e.g. 1 cm = 10 km).

## Colours on maps
- **Blue:** water – seas, rivers, lakes.
- **Green:** plains and forests.
- **Brown/yellow:** mountains and plateaus.

## Globe
A **globe** is a round model of the Earth. It shows continents and oceans correctly but is hard to carry. Maps are easier to carry and can show more detail.
> 💡 There are 7 continents and 5 oceans on Earth.

## Let's check
Q: What does the blue colour show on a map?
A: Water bodies like seas, rivers and lakes.
Q: What does the scale of a map tell us?
A: How map distance compares with real distance.
Q: What is a globe?
A: A round model of the Earth.

## Our country, India
India is a large country in **South Asia**. Its capital is **New Delhi**.

## States and union territories
India has **28 states** and **8 union territories**. Each state has its own capital, languages and culture.

## Physical features
- **The Himalayas** in the north – the highest mountains in the world.
- **The Northern Plains** – fertile land made by rivers Ganga, Yamuna and Brahmaputra.
- **The Thar Desert** in Rajasthan.
- **The Deccan Plateau** in the south.
- **Coastal plains** along the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal.
- **Islands:** Andaman and Nicobar, Lakshadweep.

## National symbols
- National flag: Tiranga · National animal: tiger · National bird: peacock
- National flower: lotus · National fruit: mango · National anthem: Jana Gana Mana
> 💡 India is called a land of "unity in diversity" – many languages, foods and festivals, one nation.

## Let's check
Q: How many states does India have?
A: 28.
Q: Which mountains are in the north of India?
A: The Himalayas.
Q: What is the national flower of India?
A: The lotus.

## Families and work
People do different kinds of work to earn a living and support their families.

## Kinds of work
- **Farmers** grow crops.
- **Weavers** make cloth; **potters** make clay pots.
- **Fishermen** catch fish.
- **Shopkeepers** sell things.
- **Doctors, teachers, engineers, police officers, drivers** offer services.
- **Homemakers** take care of the home and family – very important work, even though it is unpaid.

## Sharing work at home
Everyone in a family can share household work: cooking, cleaning, shopping, caring for elders. Boys and girls can do all kinds of work.

## Child labour
Children should study and play, not do paid work. Child labour is against the law in India.
> 💡 Many skills like pottery and weaving are passed from parents to children.

## Let's check
Q: Who makes clay pots?
A: A potter.
Q: Is housework only for women?
A: No, everyone in the family should share housework.
Q: Is child labour allowed in India?
A: No, it is against the law.
