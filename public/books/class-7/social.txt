## The medieval period
The period between roughly **700 and 1750 CE** is called the **medieval period** in Indian history.

SAY: riya-happy | Coins, inscriptions and manuscripts tell us about this period!

## Sources
- **Inscriptions, coins, buildings and paintings**.
- **Manuscripts** written by hand – often in Persian, Sanskrit and regional languages. Copyists sometimes made errors, so historians compare different copies.
- Travellers' accounts: **Al-Biruni**, **Ibn Battuta** and others.
- Paper became cheaper and more common after the 13th century.

## New groups and ideas
- New social and political groups appeared: **Rajputs**, **Marathas**, **Sikhs**, **Ahoms**, **Kayasthas**.
- Technology spread: the **Persian wheel** for irrigation, the **spinning wheel**, firearms.
- New foods: potatoes, chillies, corn, tea and coffee came from the Americas and elsewhere.
- Religions: Hinduism changed with the worship of new deities, building of temples and **Bhakti**. Islam came with traders and settlers. Sikhism began with **Guru Nanak** (born 1469).

## Region and empire
Big empires like the **Delhi Sultanate** and the **Mughals** tried to control many regions. Regions kept their own languages and traditions.

## Periodisation
British historians divided Indian history into "Hindu", "Muslim" and "British" periods – this is now considered wrong, because rulers' religion was not the only important thing. Today we use "ancient", "medieval" and "modern".
> 💡 The word "Hindustan" was used by Minhaj-i-Siraj in the 13th century for areas of Punjab, Haryana and the Ganga–Yamuna lands.

## Delhi becomes a capital
Delhi became important under the **Tomara Rajputs** and the **Chauhans** (Prithviraj Chauhan). In the 13th century it became the capital of the **Delhi Sultanate**.

SAY: buddy-happy | Then the Sultans ruled from 1206 to 1526, across five dynasties.

## Dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)
1. **Mamluk/Slave dynasty:** **Qutbuddin Aibak** (began the Qutub Minar), **Iltutmish** (completed it), **Raziyya Sultan** (the only woman ruler of the Sultanate; removed by nobles who didn't accept a queen), **Balban**.
2. **Khalji dynasty:** **Alauddin Khalji** – defended against Mongol invasions; built a large army; controlled market prices; built the city of Siri.
3. **Tughluq dynasty:** **Muhammad bin Tughluq** – shifted the capital to **Daulatabad** and back; introduced **token currency** (copper coins) that failed. **Firuz Shah Tughluq** built canals.
4. **Sayyid dynasty**
5. **Lodi dynasty:** **Ibrahim Lodi** was defeated by Babur at the **First Battle of Panipat (1526)**.

## Administration
- The empire was divided into **iqtas** given to **iqtadars/muqtis**, who collected taxes and kept armies.
- Taxes: on cultivation (**kharaj** – about 50% of the produce), on cattle and on houses.

## Mosques
Rulers built mosques to show their power and as places of worship – e.g. **Quwwat-ul-Islam** mosque near the Qutub Minar.
> 💡 Ibn Battuta, a traveller from Morocco, visited Delhi during Muhammad bin Tughluq's rule.

## Founding the empire
**Babur** defeated Ibrahim Lodi at the **First Battle of Panipat (1526)** and founded the Mughal Empire.

## Great Mughals
- **Humayun** (1530–1540, 1555–56): lost to **Sher Shah Suri**, who built the Grand Trunk Road and introduced the rupiya; Humayun regained the throne.
- **Akbar** (1556–1605): expanded the empire; **Second Battle of Panipat (1556)** against Hemu.
- **Jahangir** (1605–1627): known for justice (a chain of justice) and art; his wife **Nur Jahan** was very influential.
- **Shah Jahan** (1627–1658): built the **Taj Mahal**, Red Fort and Jama Masjid in Delhi.
- **Aurangzeb** (1658–1707): expanded the empire to its largest extent but faced revolts by Marathas, Sikhs, Jats and others.

SAY: buddy-happy | Akbar's policy of sulh-i kul meant peace with all religions.

## Akbar's policies
- **Mansabdari system:** officers (**mansabdars**) got ranks (**zat**) and had to maintain cavalry (**sawar**); they were paid through land revenue assignments (**jagirs**).
- Land revenue system by **Todar Mal**: careful survey of land and crops; tax fixed on average produce (**zabt**).
- **Sulh-i-kul** – "universal peace": tolerance for all religions. He held discussions with scholars of all faiths at **Ibadat Khana** in Fatehpur Sikri.
- **Akbar Nama** written by **Abul Fazl**.

## Decline
After Aurangzeb, weak rulers, regional powers and later the British weakened the empire.
> 💡 The Mughals were descended from Genghis Khan (mother's side) and Timur (father's side).

## Building in the medieval period
Rulers built forts, palaces, temples, mosques, tombs, step-wells and gardens to show power, wealth and devotion.

## Engineering skills
- **Trabeate (corbelled)** style: horizontal beams across vertical columns (used in temples).
- **Arcuate** style: weight of the roof carried by **arches**, introduced in the 12th century; the **keystone** at the centre of the arch.
- Use of **limestone cement**, domes and minarets.

## Temples
- **Kandariya Mahadeva temple** (Khajuraho, built by Chandela kings) – detailed carvings.
- **Rajarajeshvara temple** (Thanjavur, built by Chola king Rajaraja I) – very tall shikhara.
- Temples were centres of wealth and were sometimes attacked by rival kings (both Hindu and Muslim rulers did this).

SAY: buddy-happy | The Mughals loved gardens – the charbagh style divides a garden into four parts.

## Mughal architecture
- **Chahar bagh:** gardens divided into four parts (Humayun's Tomb, Taj Mahal).
- **Hasht bihisht** (eight paradises) plan with a central hall.
- **Pietra dura:** inlay of coloured stones in marble (Taj Mahal).
- **Red Fort** (Delhi) and **Agra Fort**; **Fatehpur Sikri** with Buland Darwaza.

SAY: aarav-happy | Stepwells and tanks stored precious water!

## Water works
**Hauz-i-Sultani** (tank built by Iltutmish), step-wells and canals.
> 💡 The Taj Mahal was built on the bank of the Yamuna so it could be reached by boat.

- **Administrative centres:** capitals of kingdoms.
- **Port towns and commercial centres:** Surat, Masulipatnam, Calicut.

A magnificent city in Karnataka, founded in 1336. Famous for its temples (Virupaksha), bazaars and fortifications. Destroyed after the Battle of Talikota (1565).

Cotton textiles of Bengal and Gujarat, Kashmiri shawls, bronze sculptures of the Cholas (using the lost-wax technique), metal work.

## Traders and guilds
Traders formed **guilds** to protect their interests: **Manigramam** and **Nanadesi** in South India. Merchants travelled in **caravans**.

The Portuguese (Vasco da Gama, 1498), the Dutch, and the English and French East India Companies came for spices and cloth.
> 💡 Bidar and Golconda in the Deccan were famous for diamonds and metalwork.

## Our environment
**Environment** means everything around us – natural and human-made.

## Components
- **Natural:** land (**lithosphere**), water (**hydrosphere**), air (**atmosphere**) and living things (**biosphere**).
- **Human-made:** buildings, roads, bridges, parks, industries.
- **Human:** individuals, families, communities, religion, economy.

SAY: aarav-happy | Lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere – Earth's four domains!

## Domains of the Earth
- **Lithosphere:** solid crust of rocks and soil.
- **Hydrosphere:** oceans, rivers, lakes, ice, groundwater.
- **Atmosphere:** blanket of air around the Earth.
- **Biosphere:** the narrow zone where land, water and air meet to support life.

## Ecosystem
A system formed by living organisms interacting with each other and their surroundings – e.g. a pond, forest or desert.

## Human and environment
Early humans adapted to nature. Later, they changed it – farming, settlements, industries. Today, we must balance development with conservation.
> 💡 5 June is celebrated as World Environment Day.

## Layers of the Earth
1. **Crust:** the thin outermost layer. **Continental crust** is made mainly of silica and alumina (**sial**); **oceanic crust** of silica and magnesium (**sima**). Thickness: about 35 km (continents) to 5 km (oceans).
2. **Mantle:** below the crust, about 2,900 km thick.
3. **Core:** the innermost layer, made of **nickel and iron (nife)**. Very hot and under great pressure.

SAY: buddy-happy | Rocks are igneous, sedimentary or metamorphic.

## Rocks
Any natural mass of mineral matter in the crust.
- **Igneous rocks:** formed from cooled **magma/lava** – granite (intrusive, cools slowly inside), basalt (extrusive, cools quickly on the surface). Called primary rocks.
- **Sedimentary rocks:** formed from layers of sediments pressed together – sandstone, limestone. May contain **fossils**.
- **Metamorphic rocks:** formed when igneous or sedimentary rocks change under heat and pressure – clay → slate; limestone → **marble**.

SAY: riya-think | Rocks change from one type to another – the rock cycle!

## The rock cycle
Rocks keep changing from one type to another over millions of years.

## Minerals
Naturally occurring substances with certain physical and chemical properties – e.g. iron ore, mica, bauxite, coal. Used to make tools, fuels and many products.
> 💡 The Taj Mahal is made of marble, a metamorphic rock.

## Let's check

Q: How are igneous rocks formed?
A: From cooled magma or lava.
Q: Into which rock does limestone change under heat and pressure?
A: Marble.

## The atmosphere
The blanket of air around the Earth, held by gravity. Mainly **nitrogen (78%)** and **oxygen (21%)**, plus carbon dioxide, argon and others.

SAY: buddy-happy | The atmosphere has layers. We live in the lowest one, the troposphere.

## Layers of the atmosphere
1. **Troposphere:** nearest; about 13 km; all weather happens here.
2. **Stratosphere:** up to 50 km; has the **ozone layer**, which protects us from harmful UV rays; good for flying planes.
3. **Mesosphere:** up to 80 km; meteorites burn up here.
4. **Thermosphere:** temperature rises; has the **ionosphere**, which reflects radio waves.
5. **Exosphere:** the outermost, very thin layer.

## Weather elements
Temperature, air pressure (falls with height), wind, humidity and rainfall.
**Insolation** is the incoming solar energy received by the Earth.
Types of wind: **permanent** (trade winds, westerlies), **seasonal** (monsoon), **local** (loo).

SAY: aarav-wow | Ocean waves, tides and currents keep water moving!

## Water
- **Water cycle:** evaporation → condensation → precipitation.
- **Ocean movements:** **waves** (from wind), **tides** (rise and fall of sea water twice a day due to the Moon's gravity), **ocean currents** (warm and cold streams of water).
- A **tsunami** is a huge wave caused by an earthquake under the sea.
> 💡 High tides help ships enter harbours; tidal energy can produce electricity.

## Let's check
Q: In which layer does weather occur?
A: The troposphere.
Q: Why is the ozone layer important?
A: It protects us from harmful ultraviolet rays of the Sun.
Q: What causes tides?
A: The gravitational pull of the Moon (and the Sun).

## Natural vegetation
Plants that grow naturally depend on **temperature and rainfall**.
- **Forests:** where rainfall is high.
- **Grasslands:** moderate rainfall.
- **Thorny shrubs/scrubs:** low rainfall.

## Types of forests
- **Tropical evergreen (rainforests):** near the equator; heavy rain; dense; trees don't shed leaves together – rosewood, ebony, mahogany. Animals: elephants, monkeys, lemurs.
- **Tropical deciduous (monsoon forests):** shed leaves in the dry season – sal, teak, neem, shisham. Animals: tigers, lions, elephants, langurs.
- **Temperate evergreen:** mid-latitude coasts – oak, pine, eucalyptus.
- **Temperate deciduous:** oak, ash, beech; deer, foxes, wolves.
- **Mediterranean vegetation:** dry summers, rainy winters – citrus fruits, olives, grapes ("orchards of the world").
- **Coniferous forests (taiga):** higher latitudes; tall, cone-shaped softwood trees – chir, pine, cedar; used for paper and matchboxes. Animals: silver fox, mink, polar bear.

SAY: riya-happy | Grasslands are home to zebras and giraffes!

## Grasslands
- **Tropical:** savannah (Africa) – lions, zebras, giraffes.
- **Temperate:** prairies (North America), steppes (Asia), pampas (South America), velds (Africa), downs (Australia).

## Thorny bushes and tundra
Deserts have thorny bushes and scrubs. In polar **tundra**, only mosses, lichens and small shrubs grow.
> 💡 Forests are being cleared rapidly; conservation and planting trees are urgent.

## Human environment
**Settlements** are places where people build homes. They can be **permanent** or **temporary** (nomads, herders).
- **Rural settlements:** villages; people farm, fish, rear animals.

Sending messages from one person to another – letters, telephones, radio, TV, satellites, internet, mobile phones. The **internet** has made the world a "global village".

- **Ladakh (cold desert):** yaks, Pashmina goats; Buddhist monasteries.
> 💡 Roads built in high mountains, like the Manali–Leh highway, are among the highest in the world.

## Equality in a democracy
In a democracy, **all persons are equal before the law**. Every adult has **one vote**, whatever their religion, caste, gender, wealth or education. This is **universal adult franchise**.

## Inequality in daily life
People may still be treated unfairly because of their caste, religion, gender, disability or economic status. **Dignity** means respect; inequality hurts a person's dignity.

SAY: riya-happy | Our Constitution says all are equal before the law!

## The Constitution on equality
- Every person is **equal before the law**.
- No one can be discriminated against on the basis of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth.
- Everyone can use public places like parks, wells, roads and shops.
- **Untouchability** has been abolished.

## Government efforts
- **Laws** against discrimination.
- **Schemes:** the **Midday Meal Scheme** (1995) gives cooked lunch in government schools – more children, especially poor and girl children, come to school; children of different castes eat together.

## Challenges
Equality on paper doesn't automatically end unfair behaviour. Social attitudes also need to change.
> 💡 Dr B.R. Ambedkar, chairperson of the Drafting Committee of the Constitution, fought against caste discrimination.

## State government
India has a government at the **state** level, in addition to the central government.

SAY: riya-think | Who makes laws for our state?
SAY: buddy-happy | MLAs in the Legislative Assembly, elected by the people.

## The Legislative Assembly
- Each state is divided into **constituencies**. People elect one **MLA (Member of Legislative Assembly)** from each.
- The party (or group of parties) with a **majority** of MLAs forms the government (**ruling party**); the rest form the **opposition**.
- MLAs discuss problems, ask questions and make **laws** for the state.

SAY: aarav-happy | The Chief Minister leads the state government!

## The Executive
- The leader of the majority party becomes the **Chief Minister**.
- The Chief Minister chooses other **ministers**, who head departments like health, education and public works.
- The **Governor**, appointed by the President, is the formal head of the state.

## How government works
- Ministers answer questions in the Assembly and take decisions.
- Government departments carry out decisions.
- People raise issues through **press conferences**, media, petitions and protests.

## Example
In a water shortage, MLAs raise the issue in the Assembly; the health/water minister announces new tube wells and water tankers.
> 💡 Elections to the state assembly are held every 5 years.

## Let's check
Q: Who heads the state government?
A: The Chief Minister.
Q: Who appoints the Governor?

Q: What is a constituency?
A: An area from which people elect one representative.

## Mass media
**Media** are the ways of communicating with many people – TV, radio, newspapers, magazines, the internet and social media. **Mass media** reach millions at once.

## Technology and money
Media use costly technology, so they need money. They earn from **advertisements**, which can affect what they show.

## Media and democracy
- Media inform citizens about the government's work, so people can form opinions.
- **Balanced reporting:** a story should show all points of view.
- Media are expected to be **independent** – free from government or business control.
- Media **set the agenda** – what they focus on, people discuss.

SAY: buddy-happy | Ads try to make us buy things. Ask: do I really need it?

## Advertising
Advertisements tell us about products and try to persuade us to buy them.
- They create a **brand** – a special name and image for a product.
- They use emotions, celebrities and **values** like happiness and being modern.
- **Social advertisements** are made by the government or organisations to spread messages – e.g. about health, safety, saving water.

## Being a smart viewer
Ask: who made this message? What is left out? Is it true? Advertisements can make people feel bad if they can't afford things.
> 💡 Fake news spreads fast on social media – always check the source before sharing.

## Markets around us
A **market** is any place where buying and selling happens.
- **Weekly markets (haats):** held on a fixed day; low prices because sellers don't pay rent; many sellers sell the same things, so buyers can bargain.
- **Neighbourhood shops:** daily needs; credit for regular customers.
- **Shopping complexes and malls:** large, air-conditioned buildings with branded goods at higher prices.

SAY: buddy-happy | Goods pass through a chain of markets – from farmer to wholesaler to shop.

## Chain of markets
Goods travel from producers through **wholesalers** (who buy in bulk) to **retailers** (who sell to consumers). Each takes a profit, so prices rise along the chain.
Example: a farmer sells tomatoes to a wholesale trader in a mandi → vegetable vendors buy and sell them in the neighbourhood.

SAY: aarav-wow | Now we can shop online too!

## Online shopping
Buying through the internet; goods are delivered home.

## Markets and equality
Big shop owners earn much more than small vendors. Poor people often face problems like low incomes and debt. Markets offer opportunities but not equally to everyone.
> 💡 Not all markets are places you can see – shares and many goods are traded by phone and internet.

## Let's check
Q: Why are things cheaper in weekly markets?
A: Sellers don't pay rent or electricity bills, and there is competition among many sellers.
Q: Who is a wholesaler?
A: A trader who buys goods in large quantities and sells them to retailers.
Q: Give one advantage of a neighbourhood shop.
A: It's nearby and may give credit to regular customers.
