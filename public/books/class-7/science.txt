## Modes of nutrition
**Nutrition** is the way organisms get and use food.
- **Autotrophic:** organisms make their own food – green plants.
- **Heterotrophic:** organisms depend on others for food – animals, fungi, most bacteria.

SAY: aarav-wow | Leaves use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make food – photosynthesis!

## Photosynthesis
Green plants make food (glucose) in their leaves using **carbon dioxide** (from air through **stomata**), **water** (from roots), **sunlight** and **chlorophyll** (green pigment). Oxygen is released.
**Carbon dioxide + water → (sunlight, chlorophyll) → glucose + oxygen**
Glucose is stored as **starch**. Testing a leaf with iodine shows a blue-black colour where starch is present.

SAY: riya-think | The insect-eating pitcher plant – is it a plant or an animal?

## Other modes in plants
- **Parasitic:** Cuscuta (amarbel) – a yellow, leafless climber that takes food from its host plant.
- **Insectivorous:** Pitcher plant – traps insects in its pitcher-shaped leaf and digests them for nitrogen.
- **Saprotrophic:** Fungi (mushrooms, bread mould) – absorb nutrients from dead and decaying matter.
- **Symbiotic:** Lichens – an alga makes food and a fungus gives shelter and water. **Rhizobium** bacteria in roots of pulses fix nitrogen for the plant.

## Replenishing soil
Farmers add **manure and fertilisers**, or grow pulses, to restore nitrogen in soil.
> 💡 Some plants with red, brown or purple leaves also photosynthesise – they still have chlorophyll, hidden by other pigments.

## Let's check

A: Carbon dioxide and water, with sunlight and chlorophyll.
Q: Give an example of a parasitic plant.
A: Cuscuta (amarbel).
Q: Why does the pitcher plant trap insects?
A: To get nitrogen, which is lacking in the soil it grows in.

## Steps of nutrition in animals
**Ingestion** (taking food in) → **Digestion** (breaking it down) → **Absorption** (into blood) → **Assimilation** (using it to build the body and give energy) → **Egestion** (removing undigested waste).

SAY: aarav-think | How long is our small intestine?
SAY: buddy-happy | About 7.5 metres – coiled up inside us!

## Human digestive system
- **Mouth (buccal cavity):** teeth chew; **saliva** turns starch into sugar; the tongue mixes food and helps us taste.
- **Oesophagus (food pipe):** moves food down by wave-like movements.
- **Stomach:** releases **hydrochloric acid** (kills bacteria) and **digestive juices** that digest proteins; mucus protects the lining.
- **Small intestine:** about 7.5 m long; receives **bile** from the **liver** (digests fats) and **pancreatic juice** from the pancreas. Digestion completes here. Finger-like **villi** absorb digested food into the blood.
- **Large intestine:** absorbs water and salts; waste leaves through the **anus**.

## Teeth
Incisors (cutting), canines (tearing), premolars and molars (grinding). Milk teeth (20) are replaced by permanent teeth (32).

SAY: riya-wow | Cows chew the cud – they bring food back up to chew again!

## Digestion in grass-eaters
**Ruminants** (cow, buffalo, goat) swallow grass quickly and store it in the **rumen**. Later they bring it back to the mouth in small lumps (**cud**) and chew again – this is called **rumination**. Bacteria help digest **cellulose**.

## Amoeba
A single-celled organism that captures food using finger-like **pseudopodia** and digests it in a **food vacuole**.
> 💡 Glucose from food gives instant energy – that's why ORS (salt + sugar) helps during diarrhoea.

## Let's check
Q: Which organ produces bile?
A: The liver.
Q: What are villi?
A: Finger-like projections in the small intestine that absorb digested food.
Q: What is rumination?
A: Chewing the cud – bringing back swallowed grass to the mouth and chewing it again.

## Hot and cold
**Temperature** tells how hot or cold something is. Our touch can mislead us, so we use a **thermometer**.
- **Clinical thermometer:** measures body temperature, range **35°C to 42°C**. Normal body temperature is about **37°C**. It has a kink to stop mercury from falling back.
- **Laboratory thermometer:** range about **–10°C to 110°C**.
Digital thermometers are now preferred, since mercury is toxic.

SAY: riya-think | Why does a steel spoon in hot tea get hot?
SAY: buddy-happy | Heat travels through it by conduction. Metals are good conductors.

## Transfer of heat
Heat flows from a **hotter** object to a **colder** one.
1. **Conduction:** through solids, from particle to particle – a steel spoon in hot tea gets hot.
2. **Convection:** in liquids and gases; hot fluid rises and cold fluid sinks – boiling water, sea breeze and land breeze.
3. **Radiation:** without any medium – heat from the Sun and a fire reaches us this way.

## Conductors and insulators
- **Conductors:** let heat pass easily – metals (copper, aluminium, iron).
- **Insulators:** don't – wood, plastic, cloth, air.

SAY: aarav-happy | We wear dark clothes in winter because they absorb more heat!

## Clothes and heat
- Dark colours absorb more heat → wear dark clothes in winter.
- Light colours reflect heat → wear light colours in summer.
- Wool traps air (an insulator), keeping us warm. Layers of clothes keep us warmer than one thick layer.
> 💡 Sea breeze (day): wind from sea to land. Land breeze (night): from land to sea.

## Let's check
Q: What is the normal human body temperature?
A: About 37°C.
Q: How does heat reach us from the Sun?
A: By radiation.
Q: Why are cooking pan handles made of plastic or wood?
A: They are insulators and don't get hot.

## Acids and bases
- **Acids** taste sour and turn **blue litmus red**. Examples: citric acid (lemon, orange), lactic acid (curd), acetic acid (vinegar), tartaric acid (tamarind), hydrochloric acid (stomach), formic acid (ant sting).
- **Bases** taste bitter, feel soapy and turn **red litmus blue**. Examples: sodium hydroxide, calcium hydroxide (lime water), magnesium hydroxide (milk of magnesia), ammonium hydroxide.
⚠️ Never taste or touch lab chemicals.

SAY: buddy-happy | Indicators like litmus show us: acids turn blue litmus red, bases turn red litmus blue.

## Indicators
Substances that change colour in acids and bases:
- **Litmus:** from lichens. Acid → red; base → blue.
- **Turmeric:** stays yellow in acid; turns **red** in a base (soap).
- **China rose (hibiscus):** turns **dark pink** in acid and **green** in base.
- **Phenolphthalein:** colourless in acid; pink in base.

SAY: riya-happy | An antacid neutralises extra acid in the stomach!

## Neutralisation
**Acid + base → salt + water + heat**. Examples:
- **Indigestion:** too much stomach acid; an **antacid** (milk of magnesia) neutralises it.
- **Ant sting:** formic acid; rub **baking soda** (a base).
- **Acidic soil:** farmers add quicklime or slaked lime (bases); **basic soil** gets organic matter.
- **Factory waste:** neutralised before being released into rivers.
> 💡 Distilled water is neutral – it doesn't change litmus colour.

## Let's check
Q: Which acid is found in curd?
A: Lactic acid.
Q: What colour does turmeric turn in soap solution?
A: Red.
Q: What is neutralisation?
A: The reaction of an acid with a base to form salt and water, with heat released.

## Physical changes
Changes in **shape, size, colour or state** where **no new substance** forms. Usually reversible.
Examples: melting ice, dissolving salt, cutting paper, folding cloth, boiling water.

## Chemical changes
**New substances** with different properties form. Usually not reversible. Signs: heat or light given out, gas produced, colour change, sound, smell.
- **Burning:** magnesium ribbon burns with a bright white flame → magnesium oxide (white ash).
- **Rusting of iron:** iron + oxygen + water → **iron oxide (rust)**.
- **Baking soda + vinegar:** fizzing; carbon dioxide gas forms. CO₂ turns **lime water milky**.
- Ripening of fruits, cooking food, digestion, photosynthesis.

SAY: aarav-happy | Painting iron stops rust from forming!

## Preventing rust
Rusting needs both **oxygen** and **water**. Prevent it by painting, greasing, or **galvanisation** (coating iron with zinc).

## Crystallisation
Getting pure, large crystals from a solution – e.g. copper sulphate crystals; salt from sea water by evaporation gives impure salt.
> 💡 Cut apples turn brown – a chemical change caused by oxygen in the air.

India's climate: hot and humid on the coasts and in the north-east (Kerala), hot and dry in the desert (Rajasthan), cold in Jammu & Kashmir.

Very cold; snow covered for six months; **sun doesn't set for six months** and doesn't rise for the next six.
**Adaptations:**
- **Polar bear:** white fur (camouflage), thick fat layer, wide paws for walking and swimming, strong sense of smell.
- **Penguin:** white and black body, thick skin and fat, huddles together for warmth, webbed feet for swimming, streamlined body.

Hot and humid with heavy rain all year. Dense forests with many plants and animals.
**Adaptations:**
- **Red-eyed frog:** sticky pads to climb trees.
- **Lion-tailed macaque** (Western Ghats): lives on trees, feeds on fruits.
- **Toucan:** long, large beak to reach fruits on thin branches.
- **Elephant:** trunk as a nose and hand; large ears to hear and to stay cool.
> 💡 Animals living in hot, wet rainforests often have bright colours and loud calls.

SAY: riya-think | Can air really push things?
SAY: buddy-happy | Yes! Air exerts pressure. Wind is air moving from high to low pressure.

## Air exerts pressure
Air pushes on everything around it – this is **air pressure**. A balloon swells; a tin can crushes when air inside it is removed.

## Wind
Moving air is **wind**. It blows from a region of **high pressure** to **low pressure**. The greater the pressure difference, the faster the wind.

## How winds form
- **Uneven heating of land and water:** land heats faster than water by day → sea breeze; the opposite at night → land breeze.
- **Uneven heating of Earth:** warm air near the equator rises; cool air from the poles moves in.
- **Monsoon winds:** in summer, land is hotter than the ocean; moist winds blow from the ocean to land, bringing rain.

## Thunderstorms and cyclones
- **Thunderstorms:** warm, moist air rises fast, cools and forms tall clouds with lightning and rain.
- **Cyclones:** huge low-pressure systems; warm, moist air spirals upward with very strong winds. The calm centre is the **eye**. They form over warm seas; the east coast of India (Odisha, Andhra Pradesh) is often hit.
- Called **hurricanes** in America and **typhoons** in the Philippines and Japan.

SAY: aarav-happy | During a storm, stay indoors and away from trees and poles!

## Safety
Follow weather warnings, move to safe shelters, keep emergency kits, don't go to the sea. Satellites and radar help forecast cyclones.
> 💡 An anemometer measures wind speed.

## Let's check

A: From high pressure to low pressure.
Q: What is the calm centre of a cyclone called?
A: The eye.
Q: Why do monsoon winds bring rain?
A: They carry moisture from the ocean to the land.

## Soil and its layers
**Soil** forms when rocks break down by wind, water and climate (**weathering**), mixed with **humus** (decayed plants and animals).
A vertical section showing layers is called a **soil profile**:
- **A horizon (topsoil):** dark, rich in humus and minerals; soft and holds water.
- **B horizon (subsoil):** less humus, more minerals; harder.
- **C horizon:** small lumps of rock with cracks.
- **Bedrock:** hard rock below.

SAY: buddy-happy | And types: sandy, clayey and loamy. Loamy soil is best for most crops.

## Types of soil
- **Sandy:** large particles, loose, drains water fast, holds little water and air spaces are big.
- **Clayey:** fine particles, sticky, holds lots of water, less air.
- **Loamy:** a mix of sand, clay and silt with humus – **best for plants**.

## Soil and crops
Clayey and loamy soils are good for wheat and gram; clayey soil holding water suits **paddy (rice)**; sandy loam suits lentils and pulses; cotton grows in loamy sand.

SAY: riya-think | Tree roots hold the soil and stop erosion!

## Soil erosion
Removal of topsoil by wind and water, especially where there are no plants. Prevent it by planting trees, grass, and terrace farming on slopes.
> 💡 Percolation rate = amount of water ÷ time taken. It's highest in sandy soil.

## Let's check

Q: What is humus?

Q: How can we prevent soil erosion?

## Why we respire
**Respiration** breaks down food (glucose) in cells to release **energy**. It happens in every living cell.

SAY: riya-think | Why do my legs hurt after running fast?
SAY: buddy-happy | Without enough oxygen, muscles respire anaerobically and make lactic acid.

## Aerobic and anaerobic respiration
- **Aerobic (with oxygen):** glucose → carbon dioxide + water + **energy**. Most plants and animals.
- **Anaerobic (without oxygen):** glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide + energy (in **yeast** – used to make bread and idli rise). In our **muscles** during heavy exercise, glucose → **lactic acid** + energy; lactic acid causes **muscle cramps**.

## Breathing
Taking in oxygen-rich air (**inhalation**) and giving out carbon-dioxide-rich air (**exhalation**).
- Air goes: nostrils → nasal cavity → windpipe → **lungs**.
- The **diaphragm** moves down and the ribs move up and out during inhalation; the chest expands.
- An adult breathes about **15–18 times** a minute at rest.
- Lime water turns milky with exhaled air – it has more carbon dioxide.

SAY: aarav-wow | Fish breathe through gills!

## Breathing in other organisms
- **Cockroach:** small openings called **spiracles** and air tubes (**tracheae**).
- **Earthworm:** through its moist **skin**.
- **Fish:** **gills** take oxygen dissolved in water.
- **Plants:** leaves through **stomata**; roots take air from spaces in the soil.
> 💡 We sneeze to push out dust and harmful particles from the nose.

## Let's check
Q: What is produced in muscles during anaerobic respiration?
A: Lactic acid.
Q: How does an earthworm breathe?
A: Through its moist skin.
Q: Why does lime water turn milky with exhaled air?
A: Because exhaled air has more carbon dioxide.

## Circulatory system
- **Blood** carries oxygen, food, waste and hormones. It has a fluid called **plasma**, **red blood cells** (contain **haemoglobin**, which carries oxygen), **white blood cells** (fight germs) and **platelets** (help clotting).
- **Arteries** carry oxygen-rich blood **away** from the heart (thick, elastic walls). The **pulmonary artery** is an exception – it carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs.
- **Veins** carry blood **to** the heart (thin walls, valves).
- **Capillaries** are very thin tubes connecting arteries and veins.
- **Heart:** has **four chambers** – two **atria** (upper) and two **ventricles** (lower). It beats about **72 times a minute**. A **stethoscope** helps doctors hear heartbeats. The **pulse** in the wrist shows the heartbeat.

SAY: buddy-happy | Blood carries oxygen and food. Kidneys clean waste from the blood.

## Excretion
Removing waste from the body. **Kidneys** filter blood and make **urine**, which goes through **ureters** to the **urinary bladder** and out. Sweat also removes water and salts.
**Dialysis** is used when kidneys fail.

SAY: riya-wow | Xylem carries water up a tree, even 100 metres high!

## Transport in plants
- **Xylem:** carries water and minerals **up** from roots to leaves.
- **Phloem:** carries food made in leaves to all parts.
- **Transpiration** (evaporation from leaves) creates a pull that helps water rise, and cools the plant.
> 💡 Sponges and hydra have no circulatory system – water moving through their bodies carries food and oxygen.

## Let's check
Q: Which blood cells fight germs?
A: White blood cells.
Q: How many chambers does the human heart have?
A: Four.
Q: What does xylem carry?
A: Water and minerals from roots to leaves.

SAY: riya-think | Can a plant grow without a seed?
SAY: buddy-happy | Yes! A potato grows from its eyes, a rose from a cutting – asexual reproduction.

## Asexual reproduction
New plants grow from **one parent** without seeds.
- **Vegetative propagation:** from roots, stems or leaves – rose and money plant (stem cuttings), potato (eyes/buds on tubers), bryophyllum (buds on leaf edges), sweet potato and dahlia (roots).
- **Budding:** yeast forms small buds that become new cells.
- **Fragmentation:** algae like Spirogyra break into pieces that grow into new algae.
- **Spore formation:** fungi (bread mould), mosses and ferns make spores that grow in good conditions.

## Sexual reproduction
Needs male and female parts – in the **flower**.
- **Stamen** (male): anther makes **pollen grains**.
- **Pistil** (female): stigma, style, ovary (contains **ovules**).
- **Unisexual flowers** have only stamens or only pistils (papaya, corn); **bisexual flowers** have both (mustard, rose).

SAY: aarav-happy | Bees carry pollen from flower to flower – pollination!

## Pollination and fertilisation
- **Pollination:** transfer of pollen to the stigma, by wind, water or insects. **Self-pollination:** same flower/plant; **cross-pollination:** another plant.
- **Fertilisation:** the male cell joins the egg in the ovule → **zygote** → **embryo**.
- The ovule becomes a **seed**; the ovary becomes the **fruit**.

## Seed dispersal
Wind (drumstick, maple – winged; cotton – hairy), water (coconut – fibrous, floats), animals (xanthium – spiny), explosion (castor, balsam).
> 💡 Dispersal prevents overcrowding and helps plants spread to new places.

Q: What does the ovary become after fertilisation?
A: The fruit.
Q: Give an example of a unisexual flower.
A: Papaya or corn.

## Slow or fast?
We compare motion by **speed** – the distance covered per unit time.
**Speed = Distance ÷ Time**
SI unit: **metre per second (m/s)**; we also use km/h.
- **Uniform motion:** equal distances in equal time intervals (a car at a steady speed on a highway).
- **Non-uniform motion:** speed keeps changing (a car in traffic).

## Measuring time
- Earlier: sundials, water clocks, sand clocks.
- **Simple pendulum:** a small metal ball (**bob**) hanging from a thread. The time for one complete swing is its **time period**; it stays the same for a given length.
- SI unit of time: **second (s)**.

## Distance–time graph
Distance on the y-axis, time on the x-axis. A **straight line** means uniform motion; the steeper the line, the faster the motion.

SAY: riya-happy | A speedometer shows speed, an odometer shows distance!

## Instruments in vehicles
- **Speedometer:** shows speed in km/h.
- **Odometer:** shows total distance travelled.
> 💡 A train at 72 km/h moves 20 m every second (72 × 1000 ÷ 3600).

## Circuit symbols
Diagrams use symbols: cell (long line + and short line –), battery, bulb, switch (open/closed), wire. A **battery** is two or more cells joined + to –.

SAY: riya-wow | A heater gets hot because of electric current!

## Heating effect
When current flows through a wire, it gets **hot**. Used in electric heaters, irons, kettles, geysers and toasters, which have a coil called an **element** (made of **nichrome**).
- Bulbs glow because their **filament** gets very hot.
- **CFLs and LEDs** waste less energy as heat and save electricity.

SAY: buddy-happy | That's the heating effect. A fuse melts and breaks the circuit if too much current flows.

## Electric fuse and MCB
A **fuse** has a wire that melts and breaks the circuit when too much current flows, preventing fires. Modern homes use **MCBs** (miniature circuit breakers), which switch off automatically.
⚠️ Use only ISI-marked fuses.

SAY: aarav-happy | A current-carrying wire acts like a magnet – electromagnet!

## Magnetic effect
A wire carrying current behaves like a magnet – it deflects a compass needle (discovered by **Hans Christian Oersted**).
- **Electromagnet:** a coil of wire around an iron core that becomes a magnet when current flows. Its magnetism can be switched on and off.
- Uses: cranes lifting scrap iron, **electric bells**, loudspeakers, motors, MRI machines.
> 💡 An electric bell works because an electromagnet pulls a hammer to strike the gong repeatedly.

SAY: buddy-happy | Because light travels in straight lines!

## Light travels in a straight line
That's why we see shadows and can't see around corners.

## Reflection
Light bouncing back from a surface.
**Laws of reflection:** (1) the angle of incidence = the angle of reflection; (2) the incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.

## Plane mirror image
- **Virtual** (can't be caught on a screen) and **erect** (upright)
- **Same size** as the object
- **As far behind** the mirror as the object is in front
- **Laterally inverted** (left–right swapped) – that's why "AMBULANCE" is written in reverse on ambulances.

## Spherical mirrors
- **Concave mirror** (curved inward): can form real, inverted images or enlarged virtual images. Used by dentists, in torches and car headlights.
- **Convex mirror** (bulging outward): always forms smaller, upright, virtual images and gives a wide view. Used as **rear-view mirrors** in vehicles.

## Lenses
- **Convex lens** (thicker in the middle): converges light; used in magnifying glasses, microscopes, spectacles for long-sightedness.
- **Concave lens** (thinner in the middle): diverges light; used for short-sightedness.

SAY: riya-wow | White sunlight splits into seven colours – that's a rainbow!

## Sunlight is made of seven colours
A **prism** splits white light into **VIBGYOR** (violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red). A rainbow forms in a similar way. **Newton's disc** with seven colours appears white when spun fast.
> 💡 Never look at the Sun through a lens – it can damage your eyes.

A: The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
Q: Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror?
A: A convex mirror.
Q: What does VIBGYOR stand for?
A: Violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red.

## Water on Earth
About **71%** of Earth's surface is water, but only about **3%** is fresh water, and most of that is frozen. Water exists in three forms: **solid** (ice, snow), **liquid** (rivers, seas), **gas** (vapour).

## Groundwater
Rainwater seeps into the ground (**infiltration**) and fills spaces between rocks. The upper level of this water is the **water table**. Water stored between layers of hard rock is an **aquifer**. We draw it out using wells, tube wells and hand pumps.

## Depletion of the water table
- Growing population and cities
- More farming and industries
- Less rain soaking in because of concrete and cut forests

## Distribution of water
Water is unevenly distributed. Some places get floods while others face drought. In many places, people – often women and girls – walk long distances for water.

SAY: aarav-happy | Drip irrigation saves water on farms!

## Managing water
- **Rainwater harvesting:** rooftop collection; recharging groundwater.
- **Bawris** (stepwells) – traditional water storage in Rajasthan and Gujarat.
- **Drip irrigation** in farms.
- Fixing leaks and avoiding waste.
> 💡 22 March is World Water Day.

A: The upper level of groundwater beneath the surface.
Q: What is an aquifer?
A: Groundwater stored between layers of hard rock.

## Forests are an ecosystem
A **forest** is a large area with many trees and other plants, animals and microbes, all depending on one another.
- **Layers:** the **canopy** (top branches), understorey, shrubs and herbs, and the forest floor.
- The **forest floor** has dead leaves that are broken down by **decomposers** (bacteria, fungi) into **humus**.

SAY: buddy-happy | Grass → deer → tiger. That's a food chain!

## Food chains and food webs
Grass → deer → lion. Plants are **producers**; herbivores are **primary consumers**; carnivores are **secondary consumers**. Many food chains linked together form a **food web**.

## Why forests matter
- Give oxygen and absorb carbon dioxide; help bring rain.
- Prevent floods and soil erosion; recharge groundwater.
- Provide wood, honey, gum, medicines, fruits, and homes for wildlife and tribal people.

## Deforestation
Cutting forests leads to floods, droughts, soil erosion, loss of wildlife and global warming. We must protect forests through afforestation and wise use.

## Wastewater
Water that has been used in homes, industries and farms is **wastewater** or **sewage**. It contains:
- **Organic impurities:** human waste, food scraps, oil
- **Inorganic impurities:** chemicals, metals
- **Germs:** bacteria and viruses that cause cholera, typhoid and dysentery
Sewage flows through underground pipes (**sewers**).

SAY: buddy-happy | It's cleaned at a wastewater treatment plant before going back to rivers.

## Wastewater treatment plant (WWTP)
1. **Bar screens:** remove large objects (rags, sticks, plastic).
2. **Grit and sand removal:** sand and pebbles settle.
3. **Sedimentation:** solids (sludge) settle at the bottom; oil and grease (scum) are skimmed off.
4. **Aeration:** air is pumped in to help helpful bacteria eat the remaining waste.
5. **Disinfection:** with chlorine or ozone before releasing clean water into rivers.
**Sludge** is used to make **biogas** and **manure**.

## Good habits
Don't throw oil, fat, plastics, medicines or chemicals down the drain. Use dustbins.

## Sanitation
Open defecation spreads disease. Toilets, **septic tanks**, **vermi-processing toilets** and **bio-toilets** help keep surroundings clean.
> 💡 Clean water and sanitation are needed for good health – that's why Swachh Bharat focuses on toilets.
