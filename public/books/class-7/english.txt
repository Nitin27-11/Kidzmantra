## The tense system at a glance
- **Simple present:** habits, facts – "The Earth revolves around the Sun."
- **Present continuous:** now, or a fixed future plan – "We are leaving for Goa tomorrow."
- **Present perfect:** past action, present result – "She has broken her arm."
- **Present perfect continuous:** started in the past, still going – "I have been waiting for an hour."
- **Simple past:** finished at a known time – "He visited Mysuru in 2024."
- **Past continuous:** in progress at a past moment – "I was sleeping when the phone rang."
- **Past perfect:** the earlier of two past actions – "The film had started before we arrived."
- **Future forms:** will (decision/prediction), going to (plan/evidence), present continuous (arrangement).

SAY: riya-happy | He said that he was tired – the reported verb moves back in time!

## Sequence of tenses
If the main verb is in the past, the verb in the dependent clause is usually past too: "He said that he **was** tired." But universal truths stay present: "The teacher said that light **travels** faster than sound."

## Common mistakes
- ✗ I didn't went. ✓ I didn't **go**. (after did, use the base form)
- ✗ He has came. ✓ He has **come**.
- ✗ Since two years. ✓ **For** two years / **since** 2024.
> 💡 Make a timeline: put events in order to choose the right tense.

## What is a clause?
A **clause** is a group of words with a **subject and a verb**.
- **Independent (main) clause:** makes complete sense alone – "I stayed home."
- **Dependent (subordinate) clause:** can't stand alone – "because it was raining."

## Kinds of dependent clauses
- **Noun clause** – does the job of a noun: "**What she said** surprised me." "I know **that he is honest**."
- **Adjective (relative) clause** – describes a noun, often begins with who, which, that, whose, where: "The boy **who won the race** is my cousin."
- **Adverb clause** – tells when, where, why, how, or on what condition: "**When the bell rang**, we left." "I will come **if you invite me**."

## Phrase vs clause
A **phrase** has no subject–verb pair: "in the morning", "a very tall tree". A clause always has one.

SAY: aarav-happy | "I stayed home because it was raining." Two clauses joined!

## Joining sentences with clauses
"I met a girl. She speaks five languages." → "I met a girl **who speaks five languages**."
> 💡 Use "who" for people, "which" for things, "whose" for possession, "where" for places.

## Let's check

Q: What kind of clause is "who lives next door" in "The man who lives next door is a doctor"?
A: An adjective (relative) clause.
Q: Is "under the old bridge" a phrase or a clause?
A: A phrase.

## Active to passive – all patterns
**Formula:** object → subject + be (tense) + past participle + by + agent
- Present simple: They **build** houses. → Houses **are built** (by them).
- Present continuous: He **is painting** the gate. → The gate **is being painted**.
- Present perfect: She **has written** a poem. → A poem **has been written** by her.
- Past simple: The dog **bit** the boy. → The boy **was bitten** by the dog.
- Past continuous: They **were repairing** the road. → The road **was being repaired**.
- Past perfect: He **had sold** the car. → The car **had been sold**.
- Future: We **will announce** the results. → The results **will be announced**.
- Modals: You **must finish** the work. → The work **must be finished**.

SAY: buddy-happy | Even questions and commands can be passive: "Let the door be closed."

## Questions and imperatives
- **Who** wrote this? → **By whom** was this written?
- **Did** she cook dinner? → **Was** dinner cooked by her?
- Bring the book. → **Let** the book **be brought**. / You **are asked to** bring the book.

SAY: riya-think | "He gave me a pen" has two objects – so two passives are possible!

## Verbs with two objects
"The teacher gave **me** a **prize**." → "**I** was given a prize." or "A prize was given to me."
> 💡 Intransitive verbs (no object) like "sleep, arrive, go" cannot be changed into passive.

## Let's check
Q: Change to passive: "Who broke the vase?"
A: By whom was the vase broken?
Q: Change to passive: "They will have finished the project."
A: The project will have been finished by them.
Q: Can "The baby sleeps" be changed to passive?
A: No, "sleeps" has no object.

## Reporting statements, questions and commands
**Reporting verbs:** said, told, asked, enquired, ordered, requested, advised, exclaimed.

## Statements
"I have finished my work," said Anu. → Anu said that she **had finished** her work.

SAY: buddy-happy | When the reporting verb is in the past, tenses move one step back.

## Tense backshift table
- is/am → was · are → were · has/have → had
- did/past → had + V3 · will → would · can → could · may → might · must → had to

## Questions
- "Where are you going?" asked Mother. → Mother **asked** me **where I was going**. (no question mark; statement word order)
- "Do you like music?" he said to me. → He **asked** me **if/whether** I liked music.

SAY: aarav-think | Commands become "told me to…" – "Sit down" → She told me to sit down!

## Commands and requests
- "Stand up," said the teacher. → The teacher **ordered** us **to stand** up.
- "Please lend me your pen," she said. → She **requested** me **to lend** her my pen.
- "Don't touch the wire," he said. → He **warned** us **not to touch** the wire.

## Exclamations
"What a lovely day!" she said. → She **exclaimed** that it was a very lovely day.
> 💡 Indirect questions have no question mark and use statement word order: "where he lived", not "where did he live".

## Let's check
Q: Report: "I can't come today," said Rohit.
A: Rohit said that he couldn't come that day.
Q: Report: "Are you hungry?" she asked me.
A: She asked me if I was hungry.
Q: Report: "Please help me," said the old man.
A: The old man requested me to help him.

## Diary entry
A **diary** records personal experiences, thoughts and feelings.
Format:
- Day, date and time (top left)
- Dear Diary,
- Body – first person, informal, expressive; events and feelings
- Name/signature at the end

## Sample diary entry
**Saturday, 14 November 2026, 9 p.m.**
Dear Diary,
Today was Children's Day and I'm still smiling! Our class performed a skit on saving water, and I played the role of a thirsty river. I was so nervous before going on stage that my hands were shaking, but when the audience laughed at my lines, I relaxed. Our principal praised us and said we should perform it for the whole district! I've learned that practice really does beat fear.
Kavya

## Informal letter
Written to friends and family. Address, date, "Dear …", body, "Yours lovingly/With love", name. The tone is warm and personal.
> 💡 A diary is written for yourself – be honest about your feelings.

## Elements of a story
- **Characters:** who the story is about.
- **Setting:** where and when it happens.
- **Plot:** the events – beginning, rising action, **climax** (turning point), falling action, resolution.
- **Conflict:** the problem the main character faces.
- **Theme/moral:** the message.

## Writing a story from an outline
Outline: a girl finds a wallet → it has a lot of money and an address → tempted, she decides to return it → the owner, an old man, is a retired teacher who had lost his pension → he blesses her; her school honours her honesty.

SAY: riya-happy | Show, don't tell – "her hands shook" instead of "she was scared"!

## Tips
- Begin with an interesting line: "It was the last thing Meera expected to find under the bus seat."
- Use **dialogue** to bring characters alive.
- Show feelings through actions: instead of "She was scared", write "Her heart thumped and her palms grew cold."
- Give the story a title.
> 💡 Past tense is usually used for story writing.

## Let's check
Q: What is the climax of a story?
A: The turning point, where the conflict is most intense.
Q: What does "setting" mean?
A: Where and when a story takes place.
Q: Give one tip to make a story lively.
A: Use dialogue, describe feelings through actions, start with an interesting line.

## Poem: The Road Not Taken (lines by Robert Frost)
Two roads diverged in a yellow wood,
And sorry I could not travel both
And be one traveler, long I stood
And looked down one as far as I could…
I shall be telling this with a sigh
Somewhere ages and ages hence:
Two roads diverged in a wood, and I—
I took the one less traveled by,
And that has made all the difference.

SAY: buddy-happy | It's about the choices we make in life, and how they shape who we become.

## Understanding the poem
The traveller must choose between two paths in a forest – a **metaphor** for choices in life. He picks the one "less traveled by" and believes this choice shaped his whole life. The poem reminds us that our decisions matter and we can't go back to try every path.
