## Rational numbers
Numbers of the form **p/q**, where p, q are integers and q ≠ 0. Natural numbers, whole numbers, integers and fractions are all rational.

## Properties
- **Closure:** rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication (and division, except by 0).
- **Commutative** and **associative** for + and ×.
- **Distributive:** a(b + c) = ab + ac.
- **Additive identity:** 0. **Multiplicative identity:** 1.
- **Additive inverse** of ⅗ is –⅗. **Multiplicative inverse (reciprocal)** of –⅔ is –³⁄₂. 0 has no reciprocal.

SAY: buddy-happy | Infinitely many! You can always find another one in between.

## Rational numbers between two numbers
There are infinitely many. Between ¼ and ½: write them as ²⁄₈ and ⁴⁄₈ → ³⁄₈ lies between. Or take the **mean**: (¼ + ½) ÷ 2 = ⅜.

SAY: aarav-happy | Properties like distributivity make calculations quicker!

## Using properties to simplify
²⁄₅ × ³⁄₇ – ¹⁄₁₄ – ³⁄₇ × ³⁄₅ → rearrange: ³⁄₇ × (²⁄₅ – ³⁄₅) – ¹⁄₁₄ = ³⁄₇ × (–⅕) – ¹⁄₁₄ = –³⁄₃₅ – ¹⁄₁₄ = **–¹¹⁄₇₀**
> 💡 On a number line, rationals fill the gaps between integers, but there are still gaps (like √2) that rationals can't fill.

## Let's check
Q: What is the multiplicative inverse of –⁵⁄₉?
A: –⁹⁄₅.
Q: Find a rational number between ⅓ and ½.
A: ⁵⁄₁₂ (their mean).
Q: Which rational number has no reciprocal?
A: 0.

## Linear equations in one variable
An equation with one variable whose highest power is 1: **2x + 3 = 11**.

SAY: aarav-happy | 3x − 7 = 2x + 5, so x = 12!
SAY: buddy-happy | Move terms with x to one side and numbers to the other. Keep it balanced!

## Solving
- **Transpose** terms: 5x – 7 = 3x + 9 → 5x – 3x = 9 + 7 → 2x = 16 → **x = 8**.
- **Brackets:** 3(x – 2) = 2(x + 1) → 3x – 6 = 2x + 2 → **x = 8**.
- **Fractions:** multiply by the LCM: x/3 + x/4 = 7 → 4x + 3x = 84 → 7x = 84 → **x = 12**.
- **Cross-multiplication:** (x + 1)/(2x – 3) = ⅗ → 5x + 5 = 6x – 9 → **x = 14**.

SAY: riya-think | Ages, money, digits – so many word problems become easy with equations!

## Word problems
- **Ages:** A father is 3 times as old as his son. In 12 years, he'll be twice as old. Son = x, father = 3x → 3x + 12 = 2(x + 12) → **x = 12**; father is 36.
- **Numbers:** Two numbers differ by 15; one is 4 times the other. 4x – x = 15 → x = 5; numbers **5 and 20**.
- **Perimeter:** A rectangle's length is 4 cm more than its breadth; perimeter 48 cm → 2(b + 4 + b) = 48 → **b = 10**, l = 14.
> 💡 Always check by substituting your answer in the original equation.

## Let's check
Q: Solve: 7x – 5 = 4x + 16.
A: x = 7.
Q: Solve: (x – 3)/5 = 2.
A: x = 13.
Q: The sum of three consecutive integers is 54. Find them.
A: 17, 18 and 19.

## Polygons
A **polygon** is a simple closed figure made of line segments.
- **Convex:** all diagonals inside; **concave:** at least one diagonal outside.
- **Regular:** all sides and angles equal.
- **Sum of interior angles** = (n – 2) × 180°. Quadrilateral: 360°; pentagon: 540°; hexagon: 720°.
- **Sum of exterior angles** of any polygon = **360°**. Each exterior angle of a regular n-gon = 360° ÷ n.

SAY: buddy-happy | Kite, parallelogram, rhombus, rectangle, square – each has special properties.

## Kinds of quadrilaterals
- **Trapezium:** one pair of parallel sides.
- **Kite:** two pairs of equal adjacent sides; diagonals perpendicular.
- **Parallelogram:** opposite sides parallel and equal; opposite angles equal; adjacent angles supplementary; diagonals **bisect each other**.
- **Rhombus:** parallelogram with all sides equal; diagonals are **perpendicular bisectors**.
- **Rectangle:** parallelogram with all right angles; diagonals **equal**.
- **Square:** all sides equal and all angles 90°; diagonals equal and perpendicular bisectors.

SAY: aarav-think | Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square!

## Example
A regular polygon has each exterior angle 45°. Number of sides = 360 ÷ 45 = **8** (octagon).
> 💡 Every square is a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram.

## Let's check
Q: Find the sum of interior angles of a heptagon (7 sides).
A: 900°.
Q: Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 30°. How many sides does it have?
A: 12.
Q: In a parallelogram, one angle is 70°. Find the adjacent angle.
A: 110°.

## Grouping data
Large data is organised into **class intervals** (e.g. 0–10, 10–20) with **frequencies**. The **class size** is the width (10).
A **histogram** shows grouped data with bars touching each other (no gaps).

SAY: aarav-happy | A pie chart shows parts of a whole as slices!

## Pie charts
A **pie chart** shows how a whole is divided. Angle of a sector = (value ÷ total) × 360°.
Example: of 60 students, 15 like football → (15/60) × 360° = **90°**.

SAY: buddy-happy | Probability = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes.
SAY: riya-think | The chance of rolling a 6 on a die is 1/6!

## Probability
- An **experiment** has **outcomes**; an **event** is a set of outcomes.
- **Equally likely outcomes:** each has the same chance (tossing a fair coin).
- **Probability of an event = (number of favourable outcomes) ÷ (total number of outcomes)**
- Tossing a coin: P(head) = ½. Rolling a die: P(even number) = 3/6 = ½. P(getting 7) = 0 (impossible). P(number < 7) = 1 (certain).
Probability is always between **0 and 1**.
> 💡 Weather forecasts ("70% chance of rain") use probability.

## Let's check
Q: In a pie chart of 360°, what angle shows 25% of the data?
A: 90°.
Q: A bag has 3 red and 5 blue balls. What is the probability of picking a red ball?
A: ⅜.
Q: What is the probability of an impossible event?
A: 0.

## Squares
A **square number** (perfect square) is a number multiplied by itself: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, …
Properties:
- Squares end in 0, 1, 4, 5, 6 or 9 – never in 2, 3, 7 or 8.
- The square of an even number is even; of an odd number is odd.
- Sum of the first n odd numbers = n²: 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16 = 4².
- **Pythagorean triplets:** for any m > 1, (2m, m² – 1, m² + 1) – e.g. m = 4 → (8, 15, 17).

SAY: buddy-happy | √144 = 12, because 12 × 12 = 144.

## Square roots
√ is the inverse of squaring: √49 = 7.
**Prime factorisation method:** √324 → 324 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 → pair them → 2 × 3 × 3 = **18**.
**Long division method** is used for big numbers and decimals.

## Making perfect squares
Find the smallest number to multiply 180 by to get a perfect square: 180 = 2² × 3² × 5 → multiply by **5** → 900 = 30².

SAY: aarav-think | √50 is between 7 and 8!

## Estimating
√250 lies between 15 (225) and 16 (256), closer to 16.
> 💡 A number ending in an odd number of zeros (like 1000) is never a perfect square.

## Ratios and percentages review
Percentage increase/decrease = (change ÷ original) × 100.
A population rises from 20,000 to 23,000 → **15% increase**.

SAY: riya-happy | 20% off on ₹500 shoes – I save ₹100!

## Discount
Discount is a reduction on the **marked price (MP)**.
- **Discount = MP – SP**; **Discount % = (Discount ÷ MP) × 100**.
A shirt marked ₹1,200 is sold at 15% off → discount ₹180 → SP = **₹1,020**.

## Profit, loss and overheads
Cost price includes **overhead expenses** (repairs, transport). Profit % and loss % are on the CP.

SAY: buddy-happy | GST is a tax added to the price of most goods and services.

## GST
**Goods and Services Tax (GST)** is added to the selling price. A ₹500 item with 12% GST costs 500 + 60 = **₹560**.

SAY: aarav-think | Compound interest earns interest on the interest too!

## Compound interest
Interest is added to the principal each year, so interest is earned on interest.
**A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ**, CI = A – P.
₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years → A = 10,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = ₹12,100 → **CI = ₹2,100** (vs SI = ₹2,000).
- Half-yearly compounding: rate R/2, time 2n.
- **Growth/depreciation:** population growth or machine value falling use the same formula (with – for depreciation).
> 💡 Compound interest grows faster than simple interest over time.

## Let's check
Q: MP = ₹800, discount 25%. Find SP.
A: ₹600.
Q: Find CI on ₹5,000 at 10% per year for 2 years.
A: ₹1,050.
Q: A ₹2,000 item has 18% GST. What is the total price?
A: ₹2,360.

## Algebraic expressions
Terms, coefficients, like and unlike terms; monomials, binomials, trinomials, polynomials.

## Multiplying
- Monomial × monomial: 3x × 4xy = **12x²y**
- Monomial × polynomial: 2a(3a – 5b) = **6a² – 10ab**
- Binomial × binomial: (x + 3)(x – 5) = x² – 5x + 3x – 15 = **x² – 2x – 15**

SAY: aarav-happy | (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²!

## Standard identities
1. **(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²**
2. **(a – b)² = a² – 2ab + b²**
3. **(a + b)(a – b) = a² – b²**
4. **(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab**

SAY: buddy-happy | Identities make mental maths magic: 103² = (100 + 3)² = 10609!
SAY: riya-wow | That's so much faster!

## Using identities for quick calculations
- 103² = (100 + 3)² = 10,000 + 600 + 9 = **10,609**
- 98² = (100 – 2)² = 10,000 – 400 + 4 = **9,604**
- 51 × 49 = (50 + 1)(50 – 1) = 2,500 – 1 = **2,499**
- (2x + 3y)² = 4x² + 12xy + 9y²
> 💡 An identity is true for all values of the variables; an equation is true only for some.

## Let's check

A: 9m² – 24m + 16.
Q: Find 105 × 95 using an identity.

## Area of plane figures
- **Trapezium:** ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height
- **Rhombus:** ½ × d₁ × d₂ (product of diagonals)
- **General quadrilateral:** split into two triangles using a diagonal: ½ × d × (h₁ + h₂)
- **Polygon:** split into triangles, rectangles and trapeziums.
Example: trapezium with parallel sides 10 cm and 6 cm, height 4 cm → area = ½ × 16 × 4 = **32 cm²**.

SAY: buddy-happy | Find its surface area – the total area of all six faces!

## Surface area of solids
- **Cuboid:** 2(lb + bh + hl); lateral surface area = 2h(l + b)
- **Cube:** 6a²; lateral surface area = 4a²
- **Cylinder:** curved surface = 2πrh; total = 2πr(r + h)

SAY: aarav-happy | And volume tells how much the box can hold!

## Volume
- **Cuboid:** l × b × h
- **Cube:** a³
- **Cylinder:** πr²h
1 m³ = 1,000 L; 1 L = 1,000 cm³.
Example: a cylindrical tank with r = 7 m, h = 10 m → V = ²²⁄₇ × 49 × 10 = **1,540 m³** = 15,40,000 L.
> 💡 Surface area tells how much paint or paper is needed; volume tells how much it can hold.

## Let's check

A: 96 cm².
Q: Total surface area of a cube of side 5 cm?
A: 150 cm².
Q: Volume of a cuboid 8 cm × 5 cm × 3 cm?
A: 120 cm³.

## Direct proportion
Two quantities are in **direct proportion** if, when one increases, the other increases in the **same ratio**. **x/y = k (constant)**.
- Cost and number of items; distance and petrol used.
Example: 5 kg of sugar costs ₹225. 8 kg costs (225 ÷ 5) × 8 = **₹360**.

SAY: buddy-happy | But more workers finish a job in less time – that's inverse proportion.

## Inverse proportion
Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** if, when one increases, the other decreases in the same ratio. **x × y = k (constant)**.
- Number of workers and days needed; speed and time for a fixed distance.
Example: 12 workers build a wall in 10 days. 15 workers take (12 × 10) ÷ 15 = **8 days**.

## Checking the type
Ask: "If one doubles, does the other double (direct) or halve (inverse)?"

SAY: aarav-think | A map scale of 1 : 1000 means 1 cm on the map is 10 m on the ground!

## Map scales
Map scale 1 : 25,000,000 → 3 cm on the map = 75,000,000 cm = **750 km**.
> 💡 Not all relationships are proportional – your age and height aren't!

## What is factorisation?
Writing an expression as a **product of its factors**. It's the reverse of expanding.

SAY: riya-happy | x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3) – using identities!

## Methods
1. **Common factor:** 6x² + 9x = **3x(2x + 3)**
2. **Regrouping:** ax + bx + ay + by = x(a + b) + y(a + b) = **(a + b)(x + y)**
3. **Identities:**
- a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)² → x² + 10x + 25 = **(x + 5)²**
- a² – 2ab + b² = (a – b)² → 4y² – 12y + 9 = **(2y – 3)²**
- a² – b² = (a + b)(a – b) → 49m² – 16 = **(7m + 4)(7m – 4)**
4. **x² + px + q:** find two numbers that **add to p** and **multiply to q**. x² + 7x + 12 → 3 and 4 → **(x + 3)(x + 4)**. x² – x – 20 → –5 and 4 → **(x – 5)(x + 4)**.

## Dividing polynomials
(10x³ – 25x²) ÷ 5x = **2x² – 5x**. (x² + 7x + 12) ÷ (x + 3) = **x + 4** (factorise first).

## Spotting errors
- ✗ 3x + 2x = 5x² ✓ 5x
- ✗ (a + b)² = a² + b² ✓ a² + 2ab + b²
> 💡 Always look for a common factor first.

## Let's check
Q: Factorise: 25a² – 9b².
A: (5a + 3b)(5a – 3b).
Q: Factorise: x² + 9x + 20.
A: (x + 4)(x + 5).
Q: Factorise: 12xy + 8x.
A: 4x(3y + 2).

## Numbers in general form
- A 2-digit number **ab** = 10a + b. A 3-digit number **abc** = 100a + 10b + c.
- **Reversing digits:** (10a + b) + (10b + a) = 11(a + b) → always divisible by 11.
- (10a + b) – (10b + a) = 9(a – b) → always divisible by 9.

## Letters for digits (cryptarithms)
Find A in: 3A + 25 = B2 (two-digit numbers) → A + 5 ends in 2 → A = 7; 37 + 25 = 62 → **B = 6**.

SAY: buddy-happy | Now we can see why divisibility rules work, not just remember them.

## Divisibility – why the rules work
- **10:** last digit 0 (because 100a + 10b + c is divisible by 10 only if c = 0).
- **5:** last digit 0 or 5.
- **2:** last digit even.
- **9 and 3:** 100a + 10b + c = 99a + 9b + (a + b + c) → divisible by 9 (or 3) if the **sum of digits** is.
- **11:** difference between sums of alternate digits is 0 or a multiple of 11 – e.g. 2,728 → (2 + 2) – (7 + 8) = –11 ✔.

SAY: riya-wow | Cryptarithms are puzzles where letters stand for digits!

## Puzzles
Think of a number, double it, add 10, halve it, subtract the original → always **5**! Algebra: (2x + 10)/2 – x = 5.
> 💡 Algebra explains why number tricks always work.

## Let's check
Q: Is 7,425 divisible by 9?
A: Yes; the sum of its digits is 7 + 4 + 2 + 5 = 18, which is divisible by 9.
Q: Why is 47 + 74 divisible by 11?
A: Because (10a + b) + (10b + a) = 11(a + b).
Q: If 31z5 is divisible by 9, find z.
A: z = 0 or 9 (3 + 1 + z + 5 = 9 or 18).
