## Numbers up to 1000
Now we count with **hundreds**, **tens** and **ones**.

## Place value
- 1 hundred = 10 tens = 100 ones
- 345 = **3 hundreds + 4 tens + 5 ones**
- 708 = 7 hundreds + 0 tens + 8 ones
- 1000 = 10 hundreds (one thousand)

## Number names
- 125 – one hundred twenty-five
- 460 – four hundred sixty
- 999 – nine hundred ninety-nine

## Comparing numbers
Compare hundreds first. 452 > 398 because 4 hundreds is more than 3 hundreds.
**>** means greater than, **<** means less than, **=** means equal.
> 💡 The smallest 3-digit number is 100; the greatest is 999.

## Let's check
Q: Write 6 hundreds, 2 tens and 9 ones as a number.
A: 629.
Q: Which is greater: 589 or 598?
A: 598.
Q: What is the place value of 7 in 374?
A: 70 (7 tens).

## Addition with carrying
When the ones add up to **10 or more**, we **carry** 1 ten to the tens place.

## Example
  47
+ 35
Step 1: Ones: 7 + 5 = 12. Write **2**, carry **1** ten.
Step 2: Tens: 4 + 3 + 1 (carried) = **8**.
Answer: **82**

## Another example
  168
+ 254
Ones: 8 + 4 = 12 → write 2, carry 1.
Tens: 6 + 5 + 1 = 12 → write 2, carry 1.
Hundreds: 1 + 2 + 1 = 4.
Answer: **422**
> 💡 Always start adding from the ones place (the right side).

## Let's check
Q: 56 + 27 = ?
A: 83.
Q: 238 + 145 = ?
A: 383.
Q: A shop sold 65 pens on Monday and 48 on Tuesday. How many in all?
A: 113 pens.

## Subtraction with borrowing
When the top digit is **smaller** than the bottom digit, we **borrow** 1 ten from the tens place.

## Example
  52
– 27
Ones: 2 – 7? Not possible. Borrow 1 ten → 12 – 7 = **5**.
Tens: 5 became 4. 4 – 2 = **2**.
Answer: **25**

## Another example
  403
– 156
Ones: 3 – 6? Borrow. The tens is 0, so borrow from hundreds first: 4 hundreds → 3 hundreds, tens become 10, then 9 tens, ones become 13.
13 – 6 = 7, 9 – 5 = 4, 3 – 1 = 2 → **247**
> 💡 Check subtraction by adding: 247 + 156 = 403 ✔

## Let's check
Q: 61 – 34 = ?
A: 27.
Q: 500 – 125 = ?
A: 375.
Q: There were 90 children. 46 went home. How many are left?
A: 44.

## Introduction to multiplication
**Multiplication** is quick, repeated addition of equal groups. We use the sign **×**.

## Equal groups
3 plates with 4 laddoos each: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12
So **3 × 4 = 12** (3 groups of 4).

## Tables of 2, 3, 4 and 5
- 2 × 1 = 2, 2 × 2 = 4, 2 × 3 = 6 … 2 × 10 = 20
- 3 × 1 = 3, 3 × 2 = 6, 3 × 3 = 9 … 3 × 10 = 30
- 5 × 1 = 5, 5 × 2 = 10 … 5 × 10 = 50
- 10 × 1 = 10 … 10 × 10 = 100

## Fun facts
- Any number × 1 = the same number.
- Any number × 0 = 0.
- 2 × 5 = 5 × 2 (order doesn't change the answer).
> 💡 The 5 table always ends in 5 or 0.

## Let's check
Q: 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 written as multiplication?
A: 5 × 4 = 20.
Q: 3 × 6 = ?
A: 18.
Q: A cycle has 2 wheels. How many wheels on 7 cycles?
A: 14.

## Kinds of lines
Lines and shapes are the building blocks of every drawing.
- **Straight line** — like the edge of a ruler.
- **Curved line** — like a rainbow.
- **Standing (vertical) line** — like a pole.
- **Sleeping (horizontal) line** — like the horizon.
- **Slanting line** — like a slide in the park.

## Flat shapes and their parts
- **Triangle:** 3 sides, 3 corners.
- **Square:** 4 equal sides, 4 corners.
- **Rectangle:** 4 sides, opposite sides equal.
- **Circle:** 1 curved line, no corners.

## Solid shapes
Cube, cuboid, sphere, cylinder, cone. A cube has **6 faces**, **12 edges** and **8 corners**.
> 💡 A circle has a centre. Every point on the circle is the same distance from it.

## Let's check
Q: What kind of line is a rainbow?
A: A curved line.
Q: How many faces does a cube have?
A: 6.
Q: Which shape has 4 equal sides?
A: A square.

## Length and weight
We measure **length** to know how long something is, and **weight** to know how heavy it is.

## Length
- Small lengths are measured in **centimetres (cm)** using a ruler.
- Big lengths are measured in **metres (m)**.
- **1 metre = 100 centimetres**
- A pencil is about 15 cm long. A door is about 2 m tall.

## Weight
- We measure weight in **grams (g)** and **kilograms (kg)**.
- **1 kilogram = 1000 grams**
- A packet of salt is 1 kg. A chocolate is about 50 g.
- We use a **weighing balance** to compare weights.
> 💡 Big things are not always heavy: a balloon is big but light!

## Let's check
Q: How many centimetres make 1 metre?
A: 100.
Q: What do we use to measure weight?
A: A weighing balance (scale).
Q: Which is heavier: 1 kg of cotton or 1 kg of iron?
A: Both weigh the same – 1 kg!

## Reading a clock
A clock has a **short hand** (hour hand) and a **long hand** (minute hand).
- When the long hand is at **12**, it is "o'clock". Short hand at 3 → **3 o'clock**.
- When the long hand is at **6**, it is "half past". Short hand between 3 and 4 → **half past 3** (3:30).
- 1 hour = 60 minutes. 1 day = 24 hours.

## The calendar
- A year has **12 months** and **365 days** (366 in a leap year).
- Months: January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November, December.
- February has 28 days (29 in a leap year).
> 💡 Knuckle trick: months on your knuckles have 31 days; months in the dips have 30 (except February).

## Let's check
Q: How many minutes are in an hour?
A: 60.
Q: Which is the first month of the year?
A: January.
Q: The long hand is at 12 and the short hand is at 8. What time is it?
A: 8 o'clock.

## Rupees and paise
- **₹1 = 100 paise**.
- Coins: ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20.
- Notes: ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500.

## Adding money
A notebook costs ₹25 and a pen costs ₹12. Total = ₹25 + ₹12 = **₹37**.

## Giving change
You buy a toy for ₹35 and pay ₹50. Change = ₹50 – ₹35 = **₹15**.

## Making amounts
₹50 = ₹20 + ₹20 + ₹10 = five ₹10 notes = one ₹50 note.
> 💡 Always check the price and count your change before leaving the shop.

## Let's check
Q: How many paise make ₹1?
A: 100.
Q: A banana costs ₹6. What do 4 bananas cost?
A: ₹24.
Q: You pay ₹100 for a book of ₹68. What change do you get?
A: ₹32.

## Data handling
**Data** is information we collect, like the number of children who like each fruit. We can show it with **tally marks** and **pictographs**.

## Tally marks
Each line stands for 1. The fifth line crosses the first four: |||| with a line across = 5.

## Example: Favourite fruits in Class 2
- Mango: 8 children
- Banana: 5 children
- Apple: 3 children

## Pictograph
Mango: 🥭🥭🥭🥭🥭🥭🥭🥭
Banana: 🍌🍌🍌🍌🍌
Apple: 🍎🍎🍎

## Reading the data
- Most liked: **Mango**. Least liked: **Apple**.
- Children who like mango or banana: 8 + 5 = **13**.
> 💡 A pictograph uses pictures to show numbers. It's quick to read!

## Let's check
Q: Which fruit is liked the most?
A: Mango.
Q: How many more children like mango than apple?
A: 5 more (8 – 3).
Q: How many children were asked in total?
A: 16.
