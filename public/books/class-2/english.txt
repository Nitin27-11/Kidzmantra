## Naming words (nouns)
A **noun** is a naming word. It names a **person, animal, place or thing**.

SAY: aarav-think | Is "city" a noun and "Delhi" a noun too?
SAY: buddy-happy | Both! "city" is a common noun, "Delhi" is a proper noun.

## Common and proper nouns
- **Common nouns** are general names: boy, city, river, dog.
- **Proper nouns** are special names: Rohan, Kolkata, Ganga, Tommy.
- Proper nouns always begin with a **capital letter**.

## Examples
- **Priya** (proper) lives in **Jaipur** (proper).
- The **girl** (common) has a **kitten** (common).

SAY: riya-happy | Proper nouns start with a capital letter!

## Let's practise
Underline the nouns: "Arjun went to the zoo and saw a giraffe."
Answer: Arjun, zoo, giraffe.
> 💡 Days and months are proper nouns too: Monday, January.

## Let's check
Q: Is "Ganga" a common noun or a proper noun?
A: A proper noun.
Q: Give a common noun for "Mumbai".
A: City.
Q: Find the nouns: "The cow eats grass."
A: cow, grass.

## Pronouns
A **pronoun** is a word used **in place of a noun**. It saves us from repeating the same name again and again.

SAY: riya-think | Why do we say "he" instead of "Aarav" again and again?
SAY: buddy-happy | Pronouns like he, she, it and they take the place of nouns.

## Common pronouns
I, we, you, he, she, it, they, me, us, him, her, them

## How pronouns help
Without pronouns: **Sita** is my friend. **Sita** sings well. **Sita** is kind.
With pronouns: **Sita** is my friend. **She** sings well. **She** is kind.

SAY: aarav-happy | Riya is my friend. She is very kind!

## Which pronoun?
- For a boy or man → **he**
- For a girl or woman → **she**
- For a thing or animal → **it**
- For many → **they**
> 💡 Always write "I" as a capital letter.

## Let's check
Q: Replace the noun: "Ravi is tall. ___ plays football."
A: He.
Q: Which pronoun do we use for a table?
A: It.
Q: Which pronoun for "Meena and Raj"?
A: They.

## Action words (verbs)
A **verb** is a doing word. It tells what someone or something does.

SAY: aarav-happy | I kick, I paint, I cook – they're all verbs!
SAY: buddy-happy | Yes! Verbs are action words. They tell what someone does.

## Examples
eat, drink, run, write, play, swim, sleep, laugh, cook, draw

## Verbs in sentences
- The dog **barks**.
- Mother **cooks** food.
- We **play** in the park.
- Birds **fly** in the sky.

SAY: riya-think | "I am", "she is", "they are" – is and are are verbs too!

## Is, am, are
These are helping verbs:
- I **am** happy.
- She **is** reading.
- They **are** playing.
> 💡 "I" goes with "am", one person goes with "is", many go with "are".

## Let's check
Q: Find the verb: "The baby sleeps."
A: sleeps.
Q: Fill in: "We ___ friends." (is / are)
A: are.
Q: Fill in: "I ___ seven years old."
A: am.

## Describing words (adjectives)
An **adjective** describes a noun. It tells us **what kind**, **what colour**, **what size** or **how many**.

SAY: riya-wow | A big, grey elephant!
SAY: buddy-happy | Big and grey are adjectives – they describe a noun.

## Examples
- **Colour:** red rose, green parrot
- **Size:** big elephant, small ant
- **Shape:** round ball, square box
- **Feeling/quality:** happy child, kind teacher, sweet mango
- **Number:** three cats, many stars

SAY: aarav-happy | My ice cream is cold, sweet and yummy!

## Making sentences better
"I saw a dog." → "I saw a **small brown** dog."
> 💡 Adjectives make our writing colourful and interesting!

## Let's check
Q: Find the adjective: "She has a blue bag."
A: blue.
Q: Add an adjective: "a ___ elephant".
A: big, huge or grey.
Q: Find the adjective: "It was a hot day."
A: hot.

## Opposites
**Opposites** are words with the opposite meaning.

SAY: aarav-happy | Hot and cold, big and small, up and down!
SAY: buddy-happy | Opposites are words with opposite meanings.

## Common opposites
- big – small
- hot – cold
- up – down
- day – night
- happy – sad
- fast – slow
- open – close
- tall – short
- in – out
- good – bad
- full – empty
- clean – dirty

SAY: riya-wow | The tortoise is slow but the rabbit is fast!

## In sentences
The tea is **hot**. The ice cream is **cold**.
The door is **open**. The window is **closed**.
> 💡 Some opposites are made by adding "un": happy – unhappy, kind – unkind.

## Let's check
Q: What is the opposite of "fast"?
A: slow.
Q: What is the opposite of "full"?
A: empty.
Q: Make the opposite of "lucky" using "un".

## Singular and plural
**Singular** means one. **Plural** means more than one.

SAY: riya-happy | One apple, two apples – just add -s!
SAY: buddy-happy | Right! But words like box become boxes, and baby becomes babies.

## Add -s
book → book**s**, pen → pen**s**, cat → cat**s**

## Add -es (words ending in s, sh, ch, x, o)
bus → bus**es**, dish → dish**es**, box → box**es**, mango → mango**es**

## y → ies
baby → bab**ies**, lady → lad**ies**, city → cit**ies**

SAY: aarav-wow | One child, many children? That's special!

## Special plurals
man → men, woman → women, child → children, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice
> 💡 Some words stay the same: one sheep, two sheep; one deer, many deer.

## Let's check
Q: Plural of "box"?
A: boxes.
Q: Plural of "child"?
A: children.
Q: Plural of "baby"?
A: babies.

## Reading comprehension
**Comprehension** means reading carefully and understanding what we read.

SAY: aarav-think | How do I answer questions about a story?
SAY: buddy-happy | Read the passage carefully, then find the answer in it.

## Read the passage
Mohan lives in a small village near a river. Every morning he walks to school with his sister, Gita. On the way, they see green fields and a big banyan tree. Birds sing on the tree. Mohan loves his village because it is quiet and clean.

SAY: riya-happy | I read the question first, then look for key words!

## Tips for answering
1. Read the passage twice.
2. Read the question and find the line that has the answer.
3. Answer in a full sentence.

## New words
- **village** – a small place in the countryside
- **banyan** – a large tree with roots hanging from its branches
> 💡 Underline names, places and numbers while reading.

## Writing short paragraphs
A **paragraph** is a group of sentences about **one topic**.

SAY: riya-think | How do I write about my pet?
SAY: buddy-happy | Start with one main idea, then add 3 or 4 sentences about it.

## How to write a paragraph
1. Start with a sentence that tells the topic.
2. Write 3–4 sentences with details.
3. End with a closing sentence.

## Example: My Pet
I have a pet dog. His name is Bruno. He is brown and has soft fur. He loves to play with a ball. He barks when strangers come. Bruno is my best friend.

SAY: aarav-happy | I'll use words like "first", "also" and "finally"!

## Useful words
first, then, also, and, because, finally
> 💡 Check your writing: capital letters, full stops and spellings.

## Let's check
Q: What should the first sentence of a paragraph do?
A: Tell what the paragraph is about.
Q: How many topics should one paragraph have?
A: Only one.
