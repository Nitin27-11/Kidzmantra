## Living and non-living things
Everything around us is either **living** or **non-living**.

SAY: riya-think | Is my teddy bear living? It has eyes!
SAY: buddy-happy | No! Living things breathe, eat, grow and move on their own.

## Signs of living things
Living things:
- **breathe** – take in air
- **need food and water**
- **grow** – a puppy becomes a dog
- **move** on their own
- **reproduce** – have young ones or make seeds
- **feel** – respond to touch, heat or light
- **die** after some time

## Examples
- **Living:** humans, animals, birds, plants, insects.
- **Non-living:** stone, chair, book, water, car.

SAY: aarav-wow | Plants don't walk, but they are alive?
SAY: buddy-happy | Yes! They grow, need food and water, and breathe through their leaves.

## Plants are living too!
Plants don't walk, but they grow, need water and food, breathe through tiny holes in their leaves, and turn towards light. The touch-me-not plant (chhui-mui) closes its leaves when touched.
> 💡 A car moves, but it is non-living – it can't move on its own, grow or have babies.

## Let's check
Q: Give three signs of living things.
A: They breathe, grow, need food, move on their own, reproduce.
Q: Is the sun living or non-living?
A: Non-living.
Q: How does the touch-me-not plant show it can feel?
A: Its leaves close when touched.

## Parts of a plant
Every part of a plant has an important job.

SAY: aarav-think | What does the root do underground?
SAY: buddy-happy | It holds the plant in the soil and drinks up water.

## Root
- Holds the plant firmly in the soil.
- Absorbs water and minerals from the soil.
- Some roots store food: carrot, radish, beetroot.

## Stem
- Holds up the leaves, flowers and fruits.
- Carries water from roots to leaves, and food from leaves to other parts.
- Some stems store food: potato, sugarcane, ginger.

SAY: riya-wow | Leaves are the kitchen of the plant – they make food!

## Leaf
- The **kitchen of the plant**. Leaves make food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide. This is called **photosynthesis**.
- Leaves have tiny holes called **stomata** to take in air.

## Flower, fruit and seed
The flower turns into a **fruit**; the fruit protects the **seeds**; seeds grow into new plants.
> 💡 Leaves are green because of chlorophyll, which catches sunlight.

## Let's check
Q: Why is the leaf called the kitchen of the plant?
A: Because it makes food for the plant.
Q: Name a stem that we eat.
A: Potato, sugarcane or ginger.
Q: What are the tiny holes in leaves called?
A: Stomata.

## Animals and their young
All animals have young ones. Some animals are born from their mothers; some come out of eggs.

SAY: riya-happy | A cow's baby is a calf!
SAY: buddy-happy | Yes! Some animals give birth to babies, others lay eggs.

## Animals that give birth
Cow, dog, cat, elephant, human – they feed their babies with **milk**. They are called **mammals**.

## Animals that lay eggs
Hen, duck, snake, crocodile, fish, frog, butterfly, sparrow.

SAY: aarav-wow | A baby frog is a tadpole and a baby dog is a puppy!

## Names of young ones
- Cow → calf, Dog → puppy, Cat → kitten
- Hen → chick, Duck → duckling, Frog → tadpole
- Lion → cub, Horse → foal, Goat → kid, Sheep → lamb

## Care by parents
Birds build nests and feed chicks. A kangaroo carries its baby (joey) in a pouch. A cat carries kittens in its mouth gently.
> 💡 A frog's young (tadpole) lives in water and looks like a tiny fish before it grows legs!

## Let's check
Q: What is the young one of a horse called?
A: A foal.
Q: Name two animals that lay eggs.
A: Hen, duck, snake, fish, frog, etc.
Q: What are mammals?
A: Animals that give birth to young ones and feed them milk.

## Why we need food
Food gives us energy, helps us grow, repairs our body and protects us from diseases.

## Sources of food
- **Plants:** grains, pulses, fruits, vegetables, oil, sugar, spices.
- **Animals:** milk, eggs, meat, fish, honey.

SAY: riya-wow | People in Kerala eat lots of coconut and rice!

## Food in different regions
- North India: roti, paratha, rajma, sarson ka saag.
- South India: rice, idli, dosa, sambar.
- East India: rice, fish, rasgulla.
- West India: dhokla, thepla, bhakri, poha.

## Keeping food safe
Cover food, wash fruits and vegetables, do not eat stale food, keep cooked food in a cool place.
> 💡 Cooking makes many foods soft and easy to digest, and kills germs.

## Let's check

Q: Why should we cover food?
A: To keep flies, dust and germs away.

SAY: riya-think | How does my body work?
SAY: buddy-happy | Many systems work together – bones, muscles, lungs, heart and more!

## Body systems
Our body organs work in teams called **systems**.
- **Skeletal system** – bones give shape and protect organs. The skull protects the brain; ribs protect the heart and lungs.
- **Muscular system** – muscles help us move.
- **Digestive system** – mouth, food pipe, stomach and intestines break down food.
- **Respiratory system** – nose and lungs help us breathe.
- **Circulatory system** – the heart pumps blood through blood vessels.
- **Nervous system** – the brain and nerves control the body.

SAY: aarav-happy | My knees and elbows are joints – they help me bend!

## Joints
Places where bones meet. They let us bend: elbow, knee, neck, shoulder.

## Keep your body fit
Exercise, eat well, sleep 9–10 hours, keep good posture.
> 💡 Your heart beats about 70–100 times a minute, even while you sleep!

## Let's check
Q: Which bone protects the brain?
A: The skull.
Q: Which system helps us breathe?
A: The respiratory system.
Q: Name two joints.
A: Elbow, knee, shoulder or neck.

## Where water comes from
- **Rain** – the main source. Rainwater fills rivers, lakes, ponds and wells.
- **Rivers** – Ganga, Yamuna, Godavari, Narmada, Kaveri.
- **Lakes and ponds** – still water bodies.
- **Groundwater** – water under the ground, taken out by wells, tube wells and hand pumps.
- **Seas and oceans** – salty water.

SAY: aarav-think | Does the rain water come back again and again?
SAY: buddy-happy | Yes! Water goes up as vapour, forms clouds and falls as rain. It's the water cycle!

## The water cycle
1. The sun heats water in rivers and seas; it becomes **water vapour** (evaporation).
2. Vapour rises, cools and forms **clouds** (condensation).
3. Clouds become heavy; water falls as **rain** (precipitation).
4. Rain fills rivers and seas again.

SAY: riya-happy | We can collect rainwater on our roofs and save it!

## Rainwater harvesting
Collecting and storing rainwater for later use. It saves water and fills up groundwater.
> 💡 Stepwells (baolis) in Rajasthan and Gujarat were built long ago to store water.

## Let's check
Q: What is the main source of water?
A: Rain.
Q: What is evaporation?
A: When water turns into water vapour because of heat.
Q: What is rainwater harvesting?
A: Collecting and storing rainwater for later use.

## Houses
A house protects us from heat, cold, rain, wind and animals.

SAY: riya-think | Why do houses in the mountains have sloping roofs?
SAY: buddy-happy | So rain and snow can slide off easily!

## Houses in different places
- **Cold places (Kashmir, Himachal):** sloping roofs so snow slides off; wooden houses.
- **Hot, dry places (Rajasthan):** thick mud walls and flat roofs; keep the inside cool.
- **Rainy places (Assam, Kerala):** houses on bamboo stilts to stay above floodwater; sloping roofs.
- **On water (Kashmir):** houseboats on the Dal Lake.
- **Very cold lands:** igloos made of snow blocks.
- **Moving people:** tents and caravans.

## Materials
Bricks, cement, steel, wood, bamboo, mud, straw, stone.

SAY: aarav-happy | Kutcha houses are made of mud, pucca houses of bricks and cement.

## Kutcha and pucca houses
Kutcha houses are made of mud, straw and wood. Pucca houses are made of bricks, cement and steel – they are stronger.
> 💡 Houses on stilts are called stilt houses.

## Let's check
Q: Why do houses in hills have sloping roofs?
A: So that rain and snow slide off easily.
Q: Where are houseboats found in India?
A: On the Dal Lake in Kashmir.
Q: Why are houses in Assam built on stilts?
A: To stay safe from floodwater.

SAY: aarav-think | Which way is east?
SAY: buddy-happy | The sun rises in the east! Face it, and north is on your left.

## Four main directions
**North, South, East, West.** The sun rises in the **east** and sets in the **west**.
If you face the rising sun: east is in front, west is behind, north is on your left, south is on your right.

## Maps
A **map** is a drawing of a place on paper, seen from above.
- Maps use **symbols** to show things: a cross for a hospital, a blue line for a river.
- Maps have a **key** that explains the symbols.
- North is usually at the top of a map.

SAY: riya-wow | A compass needle always points north!

## Using a compass
A **compass** has a needle that always points north.
> 💡 There are also in-between directions: north-east, north-west, south-east, south-west.

## Let's check
Q: In which direction does the sun rise?
A: The east.
Q: Which direction does a compass needle point to?
A: North.
Q: What explains the symbols on a map?
A: The key.

## Old times
People walked, rode horses, camels and elephants, used bullock carts and boats with oars.

SAY: aarav-wow | Now we have metro trains and aeroplanes!

## Today
- **Land:** bicycle, motorcycle, car, bus, train, metro.
- **Water:** motorboat, ship, ferry.
- **Air:** aeroplane, helicopter.

## Choosing transport
- For short distances: walk or cycle (good for health and the air).
- For long distances: train or aeroplane.
- To carry heavy goods: truck, goods train, ship.

## Travelling safely
Wear seat belts and helmets, follow traffic lights, don't lean out of windows, keep the bus and train clean.
> 💡 Electric vehicles and metro trains create less pollution.

## Communication
**Communication** means sharing messages, ideas and news with others.

SAY: aarav-happy | I call my Nani on the phone every Sunday!
SAY: buddy-happy | Talking, letters, phones and emails – all are ways to communicate.

## Ways of communication
- **Talking** face to face, and **sign language**.
- **Letters and post cards** sent by post.
- **Telephone and mobile phone** – talk to people far away.
- **Email and messaging apps** – send messages in seconds.
- **Video calls** – see and talk to people.

## Mass communication
Reaching many people at once: newspapers, radio, television, the internet.

SAY: riya-wow | Long ago, people sent messages with pigeons!

## Long ago
People used drums, smoke signals and pigeons to send messages!
> 💡 Never share personal details online without a parent's permission.

## Let's check

A: Newspaper, radio, television or the internet.
Q: What did people use long ago to send messages?
A: Drums, smoke signals and pigeons.
Q: What is a video call?
