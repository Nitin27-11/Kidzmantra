## Nouns and articles
A **noun** is the name of a person, place, animal, thing or idea. **Articles** are the small words **a**, **an** and **the** that come before nouns.

## Kinds of nouns
- **Common noun:** a general name – girl, river, city.
- **Proper noun:** a particular name – Kavya, Yamuna, Chennai. It starts with a capital letter.
- **Collective noun:** a name for a group – a **herd** of cows, a **flock** of birds, a **bunch** of grapes, a **team** of players.

## Using a and an
- Use **a** before words that begin with a consonant **sound**: a pen, a dog, a uniform (u sounds like "you").
- Use **an** before words that begin with a vowel **sound**: an apple, an egg, an hour (h is silent).

## Using the
Use **the** for something particular or already known: "I saw a cat. **The** cat was black." Also for unique things: **the** sun, **the** moon.
> 💡 Listen to the first sound, not the first letter!

## Let's check
Q: Fill in: ___ elephant (a/an)
A: an elephant.
Q: What is the collective noun for birds?
A: A flock.
Q: Is "Himalayas" a common or proper noun?
A: A proper noun.

## Pronouns
A **pronoun** takes the place of a noun so we don't repeat it.

## Personal pronouns
- Speaking about yourself: **I, me, we, us**
- Speaking to someone: **you**
- Speaking about others: **he, him, she, her, it, they, them**

## Possessive pronouns
These show ownership: **mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs**.
"This bag is **mine**." "That pen is **hers**."

## Using pronouns correctly
- **I** and **we** do the action: "**I** play."
- **Me** and **us** receive the action: "Mother called **me**."
- When you talk about yourself and others, put yourself last: "Rahul and **I** went home."
> 💡 "It" is used for things, animals and places.

## Let's check
Q: Replace the noun: "The children are playing. ___ are happy."
A: They.
Q: Choose: "Give the book to (I / me)."
A: me.
Q: Make it correct: "Me and Rahul went to the park."
A: Rahul and I went to the park.

## Verbs and tenses
A **verb** shows an action or a state. **Tense** tells us **when** the action happens.

## Three main tenses
- **Present tense** – happening now or every day: "She **walks** to school."
- **Past tense** – already happened: "She **walked** to school yesterday."
- **Future tense** – will happen: "She **will walk** to school tomorrow."

## Making past tense
- Add **-ed**: play → played, jump → jumped
- Add **-d**: dance → danced, smile → smiled
- Irregular verbs change: go → went, eat → ate, see → saw, write → wrote, run → ran

## Present tense with he/she/it
Add **-s** or **-es**: He play**s**. She watch**es**.
> 💡 Words like yesterday, last week and ago point to the past; tomorrow and next week point to the future.

## Let's check
Q: Past tense of "eat"?
A: ate.
Q: Which tense: "We will go to the zoo."?
A: Future tense.
Q: Fill in: "He ___ (go) to school every day."
A: goes.

## Adjectives
An **adjective** describes a noun – it tells us more about it.

## Kinds of adjectives
- **Quality:** what kind? – a **brave** soldier, a **soft** pillow.
- **Quantity:** how much? – **some** milk, **little** water.
- **Number:** how many? – **five** pencils, **many** books.
- **Colour/size/shape:** a **round**, **red** ball.

## Comparing with adjectives
- tall → taller → tallest
- big → bigger → biggest
- good → better → best
"Ravi is **tall**. Sam is **taller**. Mohit is the **tallest**."
> 💡 Use "-er" to compare two things and "-est" for three or more.

## Let's check
Q: Find the adjective: "The clever fox ran away."
A: clever.
Q: Complete: big, bigger, ___
A: biggest.
Q: Complete: good, ___, best
A: better.

## Punctuation
**Punctuation marks** are signs that help us read and understand writing.

## Main punctuation marks
- **Full stop (.)** – ends a telling sentence. "I like books."
- **Question mark (?)** – ends a question. "Where are you going?"
- **Exclamation mark (!)** – shows strong feeling. "Wow! What a goal!"
- **Comma (,)** – separates items in a list or shows a short pause. "I bought apples, bananas and grapes."
- **Apostrophe (')** – shows ownership or shortened words. "Riya's bag", "don't" (do not).
- **Capital letters** – start of a sentence, names, the word "I".

## Without punctuation
"lets eat grandma" vs "Let's eat, Grandma!" – a comma can save a life! 😄
> 💡 Read your sentence aloud. Where you pause, you may need a comma.

## Let's check
Q: Punctuate: "what is your name"
A: What is your name?
Q: What does "can't" stand for?
A: cannot.
Q: Add commas: "I like red blue and green."
A: I like red, blue and green.

## Sentences and questions
A **sentence** is a group of words that gives complete meaning.

## Kinds of sentences
- **Statement** – tells something. "The sun rises in the east."
- **Question** – asks something. "Do you like mangoes?"
- **Command/Request** – gives an order or makes a request. "Close the door." "Please help me."
- **Exclamation** – shows a strong feeling. "What a beautiful garden!"

## Question words
**Who** (person), **What** (thing), **Where** (place), **When** (time), **Why** (reason), **How** (way), **Which** (choice).
"**Where** do you live?" "**Why** are you late?"

## Turning statements into questions
"She is happy." → "**Is** she happy?"
"They can swim." → "**Can** they swim?"
> 💡 Every sentence has a subject (who/what) and a predicate (what it does).

## Let's check
Q: What kind of sentence: "Please sit down."?
A: A request (command).
Q: Which question word asks about a place?
A: Where.
Q: Make a question: "He is your brother."
A: Is he your brother?

## Reading comprehension
Reading carefully and answering questions helps us understand better.

## Read the passage: The Ant and the Grasshopper
It was summer. A little ant was busy carrying grains of wheat to her home. A grasshopper sat on a leaf, singing happily. "Why do you work so hard?" he laughed. "Come and sing with me!"
"I am saving food for winter," said the ant. "You should do the same."
The grasshopper only laughed and kept singing.
Winter came. The fields were covered with snow. The grasshopper had no food and was very hungry. He went to the ant. The kind ant shared some food with him. The grasshopper learnt his lesson.

## Moral
**Work today to be ready for tomorrow.**

## Answering tips
Find the answer in the passage, then write it in a full sentence in your own words.
> 💡 "Moral" means the lesson a story teaches.

## Let's check
Q: What was the ant doing in summer?
A: She was carrying grains of wheat to her home to save for winter.
Q: What did the grasshopper do in summer?
A: He sang and played.
Q: What lesson did the grasshopper learn?
A: We should work hard and save for the future.

## Letter writing
We write **letters** to share news with family and friends. An informal letter is written to people we know well.

## Parts of an informal letter
1. **Address and date** – at the top left.
2. **Greeting** – Dear Grandma,
3. **Body** – what you want to say.
4. **Closing** – With love,
5. **Your name**

## Sample letter
12, Rose Lane, Pune
14 March 2026

Dear Grandma,
I hope you are well. My exams are over and I did well. Next week, we are coming to your house for the holidays. I can't wait to eat your laddoos and hear your stories!
Please give my love to Grandpa.

With love,
Ananya
> 💡 Keep a letter short, friendly and neat.

## Let's check
Q: What comes after the greeting?
A: The body of the letter.
Q: How can you close a letter to your grandmother?
A: With love, followed by your name.

## Poems
Poems use **rhythm**, **rhyme** and **pictures in words** to share feelings.

## The Rain
Pitter-patter falls the rain,
On the roof and window pane.
Puddles grow upon the street,
Splashing under little feet.
Frogs are croaking, trees are green,
Washed and shining, fresh and clean.

## Understanding the poem
- The poet describes a rainy day.
- "Pitter-patter" copies the sound of rain. Words that copy sounds are called **sound words**.
- Rhyming pairs: rain – pane, street – feet, green – clean.

## Reading a poem well
Read slowly, pause at commas, and feel the beat.
> 💡 Other sound words: buzz, splash, tick-tock, hiss.

## Let's check
Q: Which word in the poem copies the sound of rain?
A: Pitter-patter.
Q: Which word rhymes with "street"?
A: feet.
Q: Why are the trees "shining" after the rain?
A: Because the rain washed them clean.
