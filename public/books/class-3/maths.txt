## Numbers up to 10,000
Four-digit numbers have **thousands, hundreds, tens and ones**.

SAY: aarav-wow | A stadium can hold more than 10,000 people!
SAY: buddy-happy | Big numbers need a place value chart: thousands, hundreds, tens and ones.

## Place value chart
In 4,582:
- **4** is in the thousands place → 4,000
- **5** is in the hundreds place → 500
- **8** is in the tens place → 80
- **2** is in the ones place → 2
Expanded form: **4,000 + 500 + 80 + 2**

## Number names
- 3,705 – three thousand seven hundred five
- 9,999 – nine thousand nine hundred ninety-nine
- 10,000 – ten thousand (the smallest 5-digit number)

SAY: riya-think | 4,567 or 4,657 – which is bigger?
SAY: buddy-happy | Same thousands, so compare hundreds: 6 is more than 5. 4,657 wins!

## Comparing and ordering
Compare from the left. 6,421 > 6,398 because the hundreds digit 4 > 3.
Ascending order: smallest to biggest. Descending: biggest to smallest.
> 💡 The comma after the thousands digit makes big numbers easier to read.

## Let's check
Q: Write 7,036 in expanded form.
A: 7,000 + 30 + 6.
Q: Place value of 9 in 2,915?
A: 900.
Q: Smallest 4-digit number?
A: 1,000.

## Adding 4-digit numbers
  3,456
+ 2,789
Ones 6 + 9 = 15 → 5 carry 1. Tens 5 + 8 + 1 = 14 → 4 carry 1.
Hundreds 4 + 7 + 1 = 12 → 2 carry 1. Thousands 3 + 2 + 1 = 6.
Answer: **6,245**

SAY: buddy-happy | Right! In subtraction too, start from the ones and borrow if needed.

## Subtracting 4-digit numbers
  5,000
– 1,368
Borrow across zeros: 5,000 becomes 4 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones.
10 – 8 = 2, 9 – 6 = 3, 9 – 3 = 6, 4 – 1 = 3 → **3,632**

A school has 1,245 boys and 1,180 girls. Total = 1,245 + 1,180 = **2,425** children.
> 💡 Words like "in all", "total", "altogether" mean add. "Left", "fewer", "difference" mean subtract.

## Division
**Division** means sharing equally or making equal groups. We use the sign **÷**.

SAY: riya-happy | 12 cookies shared by 4 friends – 3 each!
SAY: buddy-happy | That's division: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

## Sharing equally
12 sweets shared among 3 children: each gets **4**. So 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

## Parts of division
In 17 ÷ 5 = 3, remainder 2:
- 17 is the **dividend**
- 5 is the **divisor**
- 3 is the **quotient**
- 2 is the **remainder**

SAY: aarav-wow | And 4 × 3 = 12! Division is the opposite of multiplication!

## Division and multiplication are related
3 × 4 = 12, so 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

## Rules
- Any number ÷ 1 = the same number.
- A number ÷ itself = 1.
- 0 ÷ any number = 0.
> 💡 Check: (quotient × divisor) + remainder = dividend. (3 × 5) + 2 = 17 ✔

## Let's check
Q: 36 ÷ 6 = ?
A: 6.
Q: 25 ÷ 4 = ?
A: 6 remainder 1.
Q: 40 pencils are packed 8 in a box. How many boxes?
A: 5 boxes.

## Fractions: halves and quarters
A **fraction** is a part of a whole that is divided into **equal** parts.

SAY: aarav-happy | I cut my roti into two equal parts – two halves!
SAY: buddy-happy | Each part is one half, written ½. The parts must be equal!

## One half (½)
Cut a roti into 2 equal parts. Each part is **one half (½)**.
Two halves make one whole: ½ + ½ = 1.

SAY: riya-wow | Four equal parts are quarters!

## One quarter (¼)
Cut a pizza into 4 equal parts. Each part is **one quarter (¼)**.
Four quarters make a whole. Two quarters = one half.

## Three quarters (¾)
Eat 3 of the 4 pizza pieces → you ate **¾** of the pizza.

## Fraction of a group
½ of 10 apples = 5 apples. ¼ of 12 = 3.
> 💡 The parts must be EQUAL. A roti broken into one big and one small piece is not halves.

## Let's check
Q: What is ½ of 20?
A: 10.
Q: How many quarters make a whole?
A: 4.
Q: A cake is cut into 4 equal parts. Riya eats 1. What fraction did she eat?
A: ¼ (one quarter).

## 2-D shapes
- **Triangle** – 3 sides, 3 corners
- **Square** – 4 equal sides, 4 right-angle corners
- **Rectangle** – opposite sides equal, 4 right-angle corners
- **Circle** – centre, radius (centre to edge) and diameter (edge to edge through the centre). Diameter = 2 × radius.

SAY: buddy-happy | And 3-D shapes like cubes and cones have faces, edges and corners.

## 3-D shapes
- **Cube** – 6 square faces, 12 edges, 8 corners
- **Cuboid** – 6 rectangular faces, 12 edges, 8 corners
- **Cylinder** – 2 circular faces and a curved surface
- **Cone** – 1 circular face, 1 corner (vertex)
- **Sphere** – one curved surface, no corners

SAY: aarav-wow | Floor tiles fit together without gaps – what a design!

## Designs and tiles
Shapes that fit together without gaps make **tiling patterns**, like squares on a floor or hexagons in a honeycomb. 🐝
> 💡 Symmetry: if you fold a shape and both halves match, it is symmetrical.

## Let's check
Q: How many edges does a cube have?
A: 12.
Q: If the radius is 4 cm, what is the diameter?
A: 8 cm.
Q: Which shape do bees use in a honeycomb?
A: Hexagons.

## Length
- 1 centimetre (cm) = 10 millimetres (mm)
- 1 metre (m) = 100 cm
- 1 kilometre (km) = 1,000 m
Short things in cm, a room in m, distance between cities in km.

## Weight
- 1 kilogram (kg) = 1,000 grams (g)
Use g for light things (a pencil) and kg for heavy things (a sack of rice).

SAY: riya-happy | We measure milk in litres – that's capacity!

## Capacity
How much a container can hold.
- 1 litre (L) = 1,000 millilitres (mL)
A spoon holds about 5 mL; a water bottle holds 1 L.

## Converting units
3 m = 300 cm. 2 kg = 2,000 g. 4 L = 4,000 mL.
> 💡 "Kilo" means thousand: kilometre = 1,000 m, kilogram = 1,000 g.

## Let's check
Q: How many grams in 5 kg?
A: 5,000 g.
Q: Which unit to measure the distance from Delhi to Agra?
A: Kilometres.
Q: 2 L = ___ mL
A: 2,000 mL.

## Reading time
- The hour hand is short; the minute hand is long.
- Each small mark is 1 minute. Each number on the clock is 5 minutes for the minute hand.
- 7:15 = **quarter past 7**; 7:30 = **half past 7**; 7:45 = **quarter to 8**.

SAY: buddy-happy | 7:30 a.m. is morning, 7:30 p.m. is evening.

## a.m. and p.m.
- **a.m.** – from midnight to noon (morning).
- **p.m.** – from noon to midnight (afternoon and night).
School starts at 8:00 a.m. Dinner is at 8:00 p.m.

## Units of time
60 seconds = 1 minute, 60 minutes = 1 hour, 24 hours = 1 day, 7 days = 1 week, 12 months = 1 year.

SAY: aarav-think | School starts at 8 and ends at 2 – that's 6 hours!

## Finding time taken
Start 9:00 a.m., finish 11:30 a.m. → **2 hours 30 minutes**.
> 💡 365 days make a year; every 4th year (a leap year) has 366.

## Let's check

A: 48 hours.

## Patterns
Patterns follow a **rule**. Finding the rule helps us predict what comes next.

SAY: riya-happy | 5, 10, 15, 20 – the rule is "add 5"!
SAY: buddy-happy | Find the rule and you can continue any pattern.

## Number patterns
- 3, 6, 9, 12, … → add 3 → **15**
- 100, 90, 80, … → subtract 10 → **70**
- 1, 2, 4, 8, … → double → **16**
- 1, 4, 9, 16, … → 1×1, 2×2, 3×3, 4×4 → **25**

## Shape patterns
Patterns can grow, turn or repeat: ▲▼▲▼, or a shape turning a quarter turn each time.

SAY: aarav-wow | Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8!

## Odd and even
- **Even numbers** end in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 – they can be split into pairs.
- **Odd numbers** end in 1, 3, 5, 7, 9.
- Even + even = even; odd + odd = even; even + odd = odd.
> 💡 Look at the difference between numbers to find the rule.

## Let's check
Q: 5, 10, 20, 40, ___?
A: 80.
Q: Is 37 odd or even?
A: Odd.
Q: 2, 5, 8, 11, ___?
A: 14.
