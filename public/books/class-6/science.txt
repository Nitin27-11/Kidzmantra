## 🎬 Let's begin
Why does the Moon change shape? Why do ice cubes melt? Why do some seeds sprout faster than others? If you have ever asked "**why?**" or "**how?**", congratulations – you already think like a scientist! 🔬

## 🤔 What is science?
> 📌 **Science** is a way of understanding the world around us by **observing**, **asking questions**, **testing ideas** and **finding answers** based on evidence.
Science is not just facts in a book. It is a **process** of discovery that anyone can follow – in a lab, a kitchen, a garden or a playground.

## ⚙️ How scientists work – the scientific method
1. **Observe:** notice something carefully. My plant on the window grows bent towards the light.
2. **Ask a question:** Why does it bend?
3. **Make a guess (hypothesis):** Maybe plants grow towards light.
4. **Test it (experiment):** place one plant in a box with a hole on one side and another in full light. Keep everything else the same – water, soil, pot.
5. **Observe and record results:** the plant in the box bends towards the hole.
6. **Conclude:** plants do grow towards light. ✅
7. **Share and ask more questions:** Does it happen with all plants? With coloured light?
> 💡 In a fair test, change **only one thing** at a time so you know what caused the result.

## 🧩 Branches of science
- **Biology** – study of living things (plants, animals, our body).
- **Physics** – study of forces, motion, energy, light, sound and electricity.
- **Chemistry** – study of materials and how they change.
- **Earth and space science** – rocks, weather, oceans, stars and planets.

## 🌍 Science all around us
- Cooking dal involves heating, dissolving and changes in materials.
- Riding a bicycle uses balance, force and motion.
- A rainbow is light splitting into colours.
- Vaccines, mobile phones and satellites all come from science.

## 🇮🇳 Great Indian scientists
- **C.V. Raman** – discovered the Raman effect about light; Nobel Prize 1930. 28 February is **National Science Day**.
- **Jagadish Chandra Bose** – showed that plants respond to their surroundings.
- **Kalpana Chawla** – the first woman of Indian origin in space.
- **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** – rocket scientist and President of India.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Keep a "wonder journal". Every day, write one question about something you noticed. Pick one and design a fair test to answer it!

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Believing something just because many people say it. Scientists look for **evidence**.
- Changing two things at once in an experiment – you won't know which one mattered.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Science = observing, questioning, testing and concluding.
- A fair test changes only one thing.
- Science is everywhere in daily life.

## 📖 New words
- **Hypothesis** – a sensible guess that can be tested
- **Experiment** – a test to check an idea
- **Evidence** – facts that show whether an idea is right

## 🎬 Let's begin
Go on a walk around your school. You'll see tall neem trees, tiny grass, a rose bush, a money plant climbing a wall, ants, crows, dogs and butterflies. So many different living things! This variety is called **biodiversity**. 🌳🐞

## 🤔 What is diversity in living things?
> 📌 **Diversity** means **variety**. **Biodiversity** is the variety of all plants, animals and other living things in an area.
Scientists group living things by their **similarities and differences** to study them easily.

## 🌿 Grouping plants by their stems
- **Herbs:** small plants with soft, green stems – tulsi, mint, wheat, grass.
- **Shrubs:** medium-sized, with hard woody stems branching near the base – rose, hibiscus, lemon.
- **Trees:** tall, with a thick hard **trunk** that branches high above the ground – mango, neem, banyan.
- **Climbers:** weak stems that climb up a support – money plant, grapevine, pea.
- **Creepers:** weak stems that spread on the ground – pumpkin, watermelon.

## 🍃 Leaves and roots – a secret link
**Leaf venation** (the pattern of veins on a leaf):
- **Reticulate** – a net-like pattern (mango, rose, hibiscus).
- **Parallel** – veins run side by side (grass, banana, maize).
**Root systems:**
- **Taproot** – one main thick root with smaller side roots (mustard, carrot, mango).
- **Fibrous roots** – many thin roots of similar size (wheat, maize, grass).
> 💡 Plants with **reticulate** venation usually have **taproots**. Plants with **parallel** venation usually have **fibrous roots**!

## 🌱 Seeds
- **Dicots:** seeds with **two** parts (cotyledons) – gram, peas, beans.
- **Monocots:** seeds with **one** part – maize, wheat, rice.

## 🐾 Diversity in animals and their habitats
> 📌 A **habitat** is the natural home of a living thing, where it gets food, water and shelter. Special features that help an organism live in its habitat are called **adaptations**.
- **Mountains (cold):** yaks and snow leopards have thick fur; mountain trees like pine are cone-shaped so snow slides off.
- **Deserts (hot, dry):** camels have long legs, humps that store fat, and can go long without water; cacti have spines instead of leaves to save water.
- **Water:** fish have gills to breathe and fins to swim; lotus leaves have a waxy coat and float.
- **Grasslands:** deer have strong legs to run fast and eyes on the sides to watch for danger.

## 🌍 Why biodiversity matters
Every living thing has a role – bees pollinate flowers, earthworms make soil rich, trees give oxygen. Losing even one kind can disturb the balance of nature. We protect biodiversity through **national parks**, **wildlife sanctuaries** and by not wasting resources.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Collect 5 fallen leaves. Sort them by venation. Gently pull out a weed and a grass plant – check if your venation-root rule works!

## 😮 Fun facts
> 😮 India is one of the world's 17 "mega-diverse" countries, with about 8% of all recorded plant and animal species!

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking climbers and creepers are the same – climbers go **up**, creepers spread on the **ground**.
- Calling all small plants "shrubs" – soft green stems mean **herbs**.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Plants: herbs, shrubs, trees, climbers, creepers.
- Reticulate venation ↔ taproot; parallel venation ↔ fibrous roots.
- Habitats and adaptations help living things survive.

## 📖 New words
- **Biodiversity** – variety of living things
- **Venation** – pattern of veins in a leaf
- **Adaptation** – a feature that helps survival in a habitat

## 🎬 Let's begin
In Punjab it's makki di roti and sarson ka saag; in Tamil Nadu it's idli and sambar; in Bengal, rice and fish curry; in Gujarat, dhokla and thepla! India eats in a thousand delicious ways. But what does food actually **do** inside us? 🍛

## 🤔 What are nutrients?
> 📌 **Nutrients** are the substances in food that our body needs to **get energy**, **grow**, **repair itself** and **stay healthy**.
There are **five main nutrients**, plus **dietary fibre** and **water**.

## 🧩 Types of nutrients
- **Carbohydrates** – give **energy**. Rice, wheat, millets, potatoes, sugar.
- **Fats** – give **more energy** than carbohydrates and keep us warm. Ghee, oil, butter, nuts.
- **Proteins** – **body-building** foods; help us grow and repair. Dal, milk, paneer, eggs, fish, soybean.
- **Vitamins** – **protect** us from diseases. Fruits, vegetables, milk. (Vitamins A, B, C, D, E, K.)
- **Minerals** – iron for blood, calcium for bones and teeth, iodine for growth. Green leafy vegetables, milk, iodised salt.
- **Dietary fibre (roughage)** – doesn't give nutrients but helps food move and keeps the stomach healthy. Whole grains, fruits, vegetables.
- **Water** – helps digest food and removes waste through sweat and urine.

## 🔬 Testing food for nutrients
- **Starch (carbohydrate):** add 2 drops of dilute **iodine** → turns **blue-black**.
- **Protein:** add copper sulphate and caustic soda solution → turns **violet**.
- **Fat:** rub food on paper → leaves an **oily patch** that light shines through.
⚠️ Do these tests only with your teacher.

## 🥗 A balanced diet
> 📌 A **balanced diet** has all the nutrients, fibre and water in the **right amounts**.
Fill half your plate with vegetables and fruits, a quarter with grains (roti/rice/millets), and a quarter with protein (dal, curd, paneer, egg).
> 💡 **Millets** like ragi, jowar and bajra – called **Shree Anna** – are rich in fibre, iron and calcium. 2023 was the International Year of Millets!

## ⚠️ Deficiency diseases
Lack of a nutrient for a long time causes **deficiency diseases**:
- Vitamin A → **night blindness**
- Vitamin C → **scurvy** (bleeding gums)
- Vitamin D → **rickets** (soft, bent bones)
- Iron → **anaemia** (tiredness, pale skin)
- Iodine → **goitre** (swelling in the neck)
- Too little protein → **kwashiorkor** (swollen belly, poor growth)

## 🧘 Mindful eating
- Eat slowly and chew well.
- Stop when you feel full; don't overeat.
- Choose fresh, home-cooked food over **junk food** (chips, colas, too much sugar and salt), which can cause obesity and other problems.
- Don't waste food. 🙏

## ✋ Try it yourself
Note everything you eat for one day. Mark each item as energy, body-building or protective. Is your day balanced?

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking fat is always bad. We need some fat – just not too much.
- Thinking vitamins are only in tablets. Fruits and vegetables are full of them!

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Nutrients: carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals (+ fibre, water).
- Iodine test → starch turns blue-black.
- Balanced diet = all nutrients in the right amounts.
- Deficiency diseases come from lacking a nutrient.

## 📖 New words
- **Nutrient** – useful substance in food
- **Deficiency** – shortage of something needed
- **Roughage** – fibre in food

## 🎬 Let's begin
Legend says a shepherd named **Magnes** found that the iron tip of his stick stuck to a strange black rock. That rock – **magnetite** – was a natural magnet! Today magnets hold notes on fridges, power speakers, and guide sailors. 🧲

## 🤔 What is a magnet?
> 📌 A **magnet** is an object that **attracts** (pulls) certain materials like **iron, nickel and cobalt**. Such materials are called **magnetic materials**.
Materials not attracted by magnets – wood, plastic, rubber, paper, glass, aluminium – are **non-magnetic**.

## 🧩 Types of magnets
- **Natural magnets:** magnetite (lodestone), found in nature.
- **Artificial magnets:** made by people – **bar**, **horseshoe**, **ring**, **cylindrical** and **U-shaped** magnets.

## ⚙️ Poles of a magnet
- Sprinkle iron filings on a bar magnet – most stick near its **ends**. These are the **poles**, where attraction is strongest.
- Every magnet has **two poles**: **North (N)** and **South (S)**.
- **Like poles repel** (N–N, S–S push apart). **Unlike poles attract** (N–S pull together).
- Breaking a magnet gives two smaller magnets, each with **both** poles. You can never get a single pole!
> 💡 **Repulsion** is the sure test for a magnet. Attraction can happen between a magnet and plain iron too.

## 🧭 Finding directions
A freely hanging bar magnet always comes to rest pointing **north–south**. Its north-seeking end is its **North pole**.
A **compass** has a small magnetised needle that does the same – travellers and sailors use it to find directions.

## 🛠️ Making your own magnet
Place an iron nail on a table. Stroke it with one pole of a bar magnet **from one end to the other in the same direction**, about 30–40 times. The nail becomes a magnet!

## 🌍 Uses of magnets
Fridge door seals, pencil boxes, speakers and earphones, cranes lifting scrap iron, MRI scanners in hospitals, toys, and **maglev trains** that float above tracks.

## 🧯 Caring for magnets
Magnets lose their magnetism if they are **heated**, **hammered** or **dropped**. Store bar magnets in pairs with **opposite poles side by side**, with pieces of soft iron (keepers) across the ends. Keep them away from phones, cards and computers.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Float a magnetised needle on a cork in a bowl of water. Which way does it point? You've made a compass!

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking all metals are magnetic. Aluminium, copper, gold and silver are **not**.
- Thinking a magnet has only one pole if you break it.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Magnets attract iron, nickel, cobalt.
- Two poles: N and S. Like repel, unlike attract.
- A freely hanging magnet points north–south → compass.

## 📖 New words
- **Magnetic material** – attracted by a magnet
- **Pole** – end of a magnet where attraction is strongest
- **Compass** – a device with a magnetic needle that shows direction

## 🎬 Let's begin
Long ago, people measured length using their **hand span**, **foot**, **cubit** (elbow to fingertip) or **angul** (finger width). But whose hand? A tall farmer's span and a small child's span are different! Imagine buying cloth that way… 😅 That's why we needed **standard units**.

## 🤔 What is measurement?
> 📌 **Measurement** is comparing an unknown quantity with a known fixed quantity called a **unit**. Every measurement has a **number** and a **unit**: 3 **metres**.
**Standard units** are the same everywhere. Scientists worldwide use the **SI system** (International System of Units).
- The SI unit of length is the **metre (m)**.
- 1 m = 100 cm; 1 cm = 10 mm; 1 km = 1,000 m.

## 📏 Measuring correctly with a ruler
1. Place the ruler **along** the object, touching it.
2. Start at **0** (or at a clear mark like 1 cm if the edge is broken, and subtract it).
3. Keep your eye **directly above** the reading – looking from the side gives a wrong reading.
4. To measure a **curved line**, lay a thread along it, mark the ends, and measure the straightened thread.

## 🚗 What is motion?
> 📌 An object is in **motion** if its **position changes with time**. If it doesn't change, it is **at rest**.
Motion is described with respect to something else (a **reference point**). A passenger is at rest compared to the bus seat but in motion compared to a tree outside!

## 🧩 Types of motion
- **Linear (rectilinear) motion:** along a straight line – a train on a straight track, a falling apple, a march-past.
- **Circular motion:** along a circular path – a merry-go-round, the tip of a fan blade, a stone whirled on a string.
- **Oscillatory (periodic) motion:** to-and-fro about a fixed point, repeating – a swing, a pendulum, a plucked sitar string.
> 💡 Many objects show more than one motion. A bicycle wheel spins (circular) while the bicycle moves forward (linear).

## 🌍 In real life
Tailors use measuring tapes, doctors measure height, athletes time their races, and GPS measures distances on maps.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Measure your desk with your hand span, then with a ruler. Ask a friend to do the same. Whose hand-span answer is different? Whose ruler answer?

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Starting from the edge of the ruler instead of the 0 mark.
- Forgetting to write the unit – "5" means nothing; "5 cm" does!

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Standard units make measurement fair; SI unit of length = metre.
- Measure carefully: start at 0, eye directly above.
- Motion types: linear, circular, oscillatory.

## 📖 New words
- **SI unit** – internationally agreed standard unit
- **Reference point** – what we compare position with
- **Oscillatory** – moving to and fro repeatedly

## 🎬 Let's begin
Your water bottle, school bag, pencil and shoes – what are they made of? Plastic, cloth, wood, rubber, metal… These are **materials**. Choosing the right material matters: imagine a paper umbrella or a glass football! ☂️

## 🤔 What are materials?
> 📌 **Objects** are things we use. **Materials** are what objects are **made of**.
- One object can be made of many materials (a pencil: wood + graphite + paint).
- One material can make many objects (wood: chair, door, table, pencil).

## 🧩 Properties of materials
We sort materials by their **properties** (features):
- **Appearance – lustre:** metals like gold, copper and steel are **shiny (lustrous)**. Wood, paper and cloth are dull.
- **Hardness:** **hard** materials are difficult to scratch or press (iron, stone); **soft** ones can be pressed or scratched easily (cotton, sponge).
- **Solubility in water:** sugar, salt and lemon juice **dissolve** (soluble); sand, chalk and sawdust do not (insoluble).
- **Floating or sinking:** wood, cork, dry leaves and plastic balls **float**; stones, coins and iron nails **sink**.
- **Transparency:**
  - **Transparent:** we can see clearly through them – clear glass, clean water.
  - **Translucent:** we can see through them partly – butter paper, frosted glass, oiled paper.
  - **Opaque:** we cannot see through them – wood, metal, cardboard, a brick wall.

## 🧪 Liquids and gases in water
- Some liquids mix completely with water (vinegar, milk); some don't and form a separate layer (oil, kerosene).
- Some gases dissolve in water – oxygen dissolved in water lets fish breathe!

## ⚙️ Why grouping helps
Sorting materials by properties makes it easy to choose the right one:
- Cooking pots → **metal** (good at conducting heat), handles → **plastic/wood** (poor conductors).
- Windows → **transparent glass**.
- Raincoats → **waterproof plastic**.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Collect 10 small objects. Test each: does it float or sink? Can you see through it? Is it shiny? Make a table.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Mixing up "object" and "material": a chair is an object; wood is a material.
- Thinking heavy things always sink – a huge wooden log floats!

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Objects are made of materials.
- Properties: lustre, hardness, solubility, floating/sinking, transparency.
- Transparent, translucent, opaque.

## 📖 New words
- **Lustre** – shine
- **Soluble** – can dissolve in a liquid
- **Translucent** – lets some light through

## 🎬 Let's begin
Touch an ice cube and then a cup of warm milk. One is cold, the other is hot. But how cold? How hot? Our touch can fool us – after holding ice, normal water feels warm! To be exact, we measure **temperature**. 🌡️

## 🤔 What is temperature?
> 📌 **Temperature** tells us **how hot or cold** something is. It is measured with a **thermometer**. The common unit is the **degree Celsius (°C)**.
- Water freezes at **0 °C** and boils at **100 °C** (at normal pressure).
- The SI unit of temperature is the **kelvin (K)**; 0 °C ≈ 273 K.

## 🧩 Types of thermometers
**1. Clinical thermometer** – measures **body temperature**.
- Range: about **35 °C to 42 °C** (our body never goes far outside this).
- Average normal body temperature is about **37 °C** (it can vary a little from person to person and during the day).
- It has a **kink** (a bend) that stops the mercury level from falling back, so you can read it after taking it out.
- Shake it gently before use to bring the level below 35 °C.

**2. Laboratory thermometer** – measures temperatures of other things (water, liquids).
- Range: about **–10 °C to 110 °C**.
- No kink – read it **while it is still in** the liquid.
- Hold it upright; the bulb should not touch the container.

**3. Digital thermometer** – shows the number on a screen. Safe and easy, now widely used. Infrared thermometers measure without touching.

## ⚠️ Mercury safety
Older thermometers contain **mercury**, which is poisonous. If one breaks, don't touch it – tell an adult. Many countries are replacing them with digital ones.

## ⚙️ Reading a thermometer correctly
1. Note the range and what each small division stands for.
2. Keep your eye **level** with the top of the liquid.
3. For a clinical thermometer, keep the bulb under the tongue or in the armpit for about 1 minute.

## 🌍 In real life
Weather reports, fridges and ovens, fever checks, and keeping vaccines and medicines at the right temperature.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Put one hand in cold water and one in warm water for a minute, then both in normal water. Each hand "feels" a different temperature – proof that touch is unreliable!

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Using a clinical thermometer to check hot water – it can break (range only up to 42 °C).
- Taking a laboratory thermometer out of the liquid before reading.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Temperature = how hot or cold; measured in °C with thermometers.
- Clinical: 35–42 °C, has a kink. Lab: –10 to 110 °C, no kink.
- Normal body temperature ≈ 37 °C.

## 📖 New words
- **Thermometer** – instrument to measure temperature
- **Kink** – a bend in a clinical thermometer
- **Celsius** – temperature scale with water freezing at 0°

## 🎬 Let's begin
A puddle on the road disappears after the sun comes out. Ice in your glass turns to water. Tiny drops appear outside a cold bottle. Where does the water go, and where does it come from? Let's follow water on its amazing **journey**! 💧

## 🤔 The three states of water
> 📌 Matter exists in three main **states**: **solid**, **liquid** and **gas**. Water can be in all three!
- **Solid – ice:** has a fixed shape and fixed volume.
- **Liquid – water:** has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container.
- **Gas – water vapour:** has no fixed shape or volume; spreads out to fill any space. It is invisible!
> 💡 The white "smoke" from a kettle is not vapour – it's tiny water droplets formed when vapour cools. Real vapour is invisible.

## 🔄 Changes of state
- **Melting:** solid → liquid (ice melts at **0 °C**).
- **Freezing:** liquid → solid (water freezes at **0 °C**).
- **Evaporation:** liquid → gas, slowly at any temperature, from the surface.
- **Boiling:** liquid → gas, quickly, throughout the liquid (water boils at **100 °C**).
- **Condensation:** gas → liquid on cooling (drops outside a cold glass, dew on grass).

## ⚙️ What makes evaporation faster?
1. **Higher temperature** – clothes dry faster on a sunny day.
2. **More surface area** – spread clothes out; water in a plate dries faster than in a bottle.
3. **Wind** – clothes dry faster on a windy day.
4. **Less humidity** (less moisture in the air) – clothes dry slowly in the rainy season.

## ❄️ Evaporation causes cooling
When water evaporates, it takes heat from the surface. That's why:
- We feel cool when sweat dries.
- Water in an earthen **matka** stays cool – water seeps through tiny pores and evaporates.
- Sprinkling water on a hot roof cools it.

## 🌧️ The water cycle
1. The sun heats water in seas, rivers and lakes → **evaporation**. Plants also give out water vapour (**transpiration**).
2. Vapour rises, cools and **condenses** into tiny droplets → **clouds**.
3. Droplets join and become heavy → **precipitation** (rain, snow, hail).
4. Water flows into rivers and seas or soaks into the ground (**groundwater**) → and the cycle repeats!

## ✋ Try it yourself
Put equal water in a plate and a narrow glass. Keep both in the sun. Check after 2 hours. Which has less water left? Why?

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking evaporation happens only at 100 °C. It happens at all temperatures; boiling happens at 100 °C.
- Calling the visible steam "water vapour".

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Water: solid (ice), liquid (water), gas (vapour).
- Melting, freezing, evaporation, boiling, condensation.
- Evaporation is faster with heat, wind, more surface area, low humidity; it cools.
- The water cycle keeps water moving.

## 📖 New words
- **Evaporation** – liquid slowly turning into gas
- **Condensation** – gas turning into liquid on cooling
- **Humidity** – amount of water vapour in the air

## 🎬 Let's begin
At home, your grandmother picks tiny stones out of rice, your mother strains tea, and in the village, farmers toss grain in the air on a windy day. They are all **separating** mixtures! Let's learn the smart ways people separate things. 🍵

## 🤔 What is separation and why do we need it?
> 📌 A **mixture** has two or more substances mixed together. **Separation** means taking out the parts of a mixture.
We separate to:
- remove **unwanted** or **harmful** parts (stones from rice),
- get **useful** parts (butter from curd),
- get **pure** substances (salt from sea water).

## 🧩 Methods of separation
- **Hand-picking:** removing bigger, visible impurities by hand – stones from rice and dal.
- **Threshing:** beating harvested stalks to separate **grain** from the stalks. Done by hand, by bullocks or by machines.
- **Winnowing:** dropping grain from a height in the wind; the light **husk** blows away while heavy **grain** falls straight down.
- **Sieving:** using a net or sieve to separate particles of **different sizes** – bran from flour, pebbles from sand.
- **Sedimentation:** heavy, insoluble particles **settle at the bottom** of a liquid (mud in water).
- **Decantation:** gently **pouring off** the clear liquid on top after sedimentation.
- **Filtration:** passing a mixture through a **filter** (filter paper, cloth); the solid stays behind, the clear liquid (**filtrate**) passes through. Straining tea!
- **Evaporation:** heating a solution so the water evaporates and the dissolved solid is left – salt from sea water.
- **Churning:** fast stirring that separates **lighter** parts – butter from curd (makhan from dahi).
- **Magnetic separation:** using a magnet to pull out **iron** pieces from a mixture.

## ⚙️ Using more than one method
To separate **sand + salt + water**:
1. **Filter** – sand stays on the filter paper.
2. **Evaporate** the filtrate – salt is left behind.
> 💡 Choose the method based on the **property** that differs: size, weight, solubility, magnetism.

## 🌍 In real life
Water purifiers use filters; dairies churn milk to make butter; salt pans on Gujarat's coast use evaporation; scrap yards use giant magnets.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Mix sand, salt and a few iron pins (careful!). Separate them using a magnet, water, filter paper and sunlight.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Trying to filter out salt from salt water – dissolved salt passes through the filter! Use evaporation.
- Mixing up winnowing (uses wind) and sieving (uses a net).

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Separation removes unwanted parts or gets useful ones.
- Methods: hand-picking, threshing, winnowing, sieving, sedimentation, decantation, filtration, evaporation, churning, magnetic separation.
- Often several methods are used one after another.

## 📖 New words
- **Mixture** – two or more substances mixed together
- **Husk** – the light outer covering of grain
- **Filtrate** – the clear liquid that passes through a filter

## 🎬 Let's begin
A stone, a dog, a mango tree and a toy car. Which are alive? The car moves… but is it living? The tree doesn't walk… but is it living? Let's discover what makes something **alive**! 🌱🐶

## 🤔 Characteristics of living things
> 📌 Living things show certain **life processes**. Non-living things do not show all of them.
1. **Nutrition:** need food for energy. Plants make their own food; animals eat plants or other animals.
2. **Respiration:** take in air (oxygen) to release energy from food. Plants breathe through tiny pores in leaves.
3. **Excretion:** remove waste from the body (urine, sweat; plants give out extra water and gases).
4. **Growth:** become bigger from inside – a seed becomes a tree, a baby becomes an adult.
5. **Movement:** animals move from place to place; plants move parts – leaves turn towards light, flowers open and close.
6. **Reproduction:** produce young ones of their own kind – through eggs, babies or seeds.
7. **Response to stimuli:** react to changes around them – we pull our hand back from a hot pan; the **touch-me-not** (chhui-mui) plant folds its leaves when touched.
8. **Life span:** are born, live for some time and die.
> 💡 A toy car moves, but it can't grow, eat, breathe or reproduce – so it's **non-living**.

## 🌱 Life cycles
> 📌 A **life cycle** is the series of stages a living thing goes through from birth to adulthood.
- **Plants:** seed → **germination** (sprouting) → seedling → adult plant → flowers → fruits and seeds → new plants.
- **Frog:** eggs → **tadpole** (lives in water, breathes with gills) → froglet (legs appear) → adult frog.
- **Butterfly:** egg → caterpillar (larva) → pupa → butterfly.

## 🔬 What do seeds need to germinate?
A fair test with moong seeds shows they need **water**, **air** and **suitable warmth**. Seeds kept dry, under water (no air) or in a fridge don't sprout well.

## 🌍 In real life
Farmers soak seeds before sowing; we see tadpoles in ponds in the rainy season; a sunflower turns to face the sun.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Put moong seeds in three cups: dry cotton, wet cotton, and fully under water. Which sprout in 2–3 days?

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking plants are non-living because they don't walk. They grow, breathe, respond and reproduce!
- Thinking movement alone proves life – the wind, a river and a car move too.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Life processes: nutrition, respiration, excretion, growth, movement, reproduction, response, life span.
- Life cycles: plant (seed → plant), frog (egg → tadpole → frog).
- Seeds need water, air and warmth to germinate.

## 📖 New words
- **Stimulus** – a change that living things respond to
- **Germination** – sprouting of a seed
- **Tadpole** – young stage of a frog

## 🎬 Let's begin
Every breath of air, every glass of water, the sunshine on your face, the soil that grows your food, the wood of your desk – all are **gifts from nature**. They are our **treasures**, and we must use them wisely. 🌍💎

## 🤔 What are natural resources?
> 📌 **Natural resources** are materials and energy we get from **nature** that are useful to us – air, water, sunlight, soil, plants, animals, minerals, coal and petroleum.

## 🧩 Types of natural resources
**1. Renewable resources** – are replaced naturally in a short time, if used wisely.
- **Air, water, sunlight, wind, forests.**
**2. Non-renewable resources** – are limited; they take **millions of years** to form and will run out.
- **Coal, petroleum, natural gas, minerals** (iron, copper).
> 💡 Coal and petroleum are called **fossil fuels** because they formed from remains of plants and animals buried long ago.

## 🌬️ Air
A mixture of gases – mainly **nitrogen (about 78%)** and **oxygen (about 21%)**, plus carbon dioxide and others. We need oxygen to breathe; plants need carbon dioxide to make food.

## 💧 Water
Needed by all living things. Only a tiny part of Earth's water is fresh and usable. Save it!

## ☀️ Sunlight and wind – clean energy
- **Solar energy:** solar panels turn sunlight into electricity; solar cookers cook food.
- **Wind energy:** windmills and wind turbines turn moving air into electricity. Tamil Nadu and Gujarat have many wind farms.
These don't pollute and won't run out!

## 🪨 Soil and forests
Soil grows our food and takes hundreds of years to form just a few centimetres. Forests give oxygen, wood, fruits, medicines and homes to animals, and bring rain.

## ♻️ Using resources wisely
- **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle.**
- Switch off lights and fans when not needed.
- Walk, cycle or use public transport.
- Plant trees; don't waste paper, food or water.
> 😮 Gandhiji said: "The Earth provides enough to satisfy every man's need, but not every man's greed."

## ✋ Try it yourself
Do a "resource audit" at home for one day: count how many times a tap, a light or a fan was left on unnecessarily.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking water is non-renewable – it is renewable through the water cycle, but clean fresh water is limited and must be saved.
- Thinking forests can be cut without limit because they are "renewable". They regrow only if we replant and protect them.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Natural resources come from nature.
- Renewable: air, water, sunlight, wind, forests. Non-renewable: coal, petroleum, natural gas, minerals.
- Solar and wind energy are clean.
- Use resources wisely: reduce, reuse, recycle.

## 📖 New words
- **Renewable** – can be replaced naturally
- **Fossil fuel** – fuel formed from ancient remains (coal, petroleum)
- **Conservation** – careful use and protection

## 🎬 Let's begin
On a clear night far from city lights, look up. Thousands of twinkling stars! The Moon glowing, a bright "star" that doesn't twinkle (a planet!), and maybe a shooting star. What lies **beyond Earth**? Let's go on a space journey! 🚀✨

## 🤔 Stars
> 📌 A **star** is a huge, very hot ball of glowing gases that gives out its **own light and heat**.
- The **Sun** is our nearest star. Other stars look tiny because they are very, very far away.
- Stars seem to **twinkle** because their light passes through moving layers of air.
- Stars appear to move across the night sky from east to west because the **Earth rotates**.

## ✨ Constellations
> 📌 A **constellation** is a group of stars that forms a recognisable pattern in the sky.
- **Saptarishi (Ursa Major / Big Dipper):** seven bright stars shaped like a ladle or a question mark.
- **Orion (Hunter / Mriga):** three bright stars in a row make the hunter's **belt**.
- **Cassiopeia:** looks like a W or M.
- The **Pole Star (Dhruva Tara)** stays almost fixed above the north. The pointer stars of Saptarishi point to it – it helps us find **north**!

## 🪐 Our solar system
> 📌 The **solar system** is the Sun and all the objects that move around it.
- **Eight planets**, in order from the Sun: **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.**
- Trick: "**M**y **V**ery **E**ager **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**oodles."
- **Planets** don't make their own light; they reflect sunlight and don't twinkle.
- Inner planets (Mercury–Mars) are **rocky**; outer planets (Jupiter–Neptune) are **giant** and mostly gas or ice.
- **Venus** is the brightest planet in our sky; **Jupiter** is the largest; **Saturn** has beautiful rings; **Mars** is red.
- **Pluto** is now called a **dwarf planet**.

## 🌙 Other members
- **Moons (satellites):** objects that go around planets. Earth has **one** Moon; it reflects sunlight.
- **Asteroids:** rocky pieces, mostly in the **asteroid belt** between Mars and Jupiter.
- **Comets:** balls of ice and dust with glowing **tails** when near the Sun – Halley's Comet returns about every 76 years.
- **Meteors:** small rocks burning up in our atmosphere – "shooting stars".

## 🇮🇳 India in space
- **Aryabhata** (1975) – India's first satellite, named after the great astronomer.
- **Chandrayaan-3** (2023) – landed near the Moon's south pole; India celebrates **23 August as National Space Day**.
- **Aditya-L1** – India's mission to study the Sun.

## 🌃 Light pollution
Too many bright city lights hide the stars. Using lights wisely helps us – and birds and insects – see the night sky.

## ✋ Try it yourself
On a clear night, find Saptarishi. Follow the two "pointer" stars to locate the Pole Star.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Calling the Moon a star or a planet. It's Earth's natural satellite.
- Thinking a "shooting star" is a falling star – it's a small rock burning in our atmosphere.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Stars make their own light; planets reflect it.
- Constellations are star patterns; the Pole Star shows north.
- Eight planets go around the Sun; Earth has one Moon.
- India's space missions: Aryabhata, Chandrayaan, Aditya-L1.

## 📖 New words
- **Constellation** – a pattern of stars
- **Satellite** – an object that goes around a planet
- **Orbit** – the path of an object around another
