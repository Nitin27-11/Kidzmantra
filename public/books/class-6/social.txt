## 🎬 Let's begin
Your friend is coming to your house for the first time. How do you explain the way? "Turn left at the temple, go straight past the park…" You're making a **map** in words! Now imagine finding any place on the whole Earth – for that we need maps, globes and an invisible grid of lines. 🗺️

## 🤔 What is a map?
> 📌 A **map** is a drawing of the Earth's surface, or part of it, on a **flat** surface, made to **scale**.
A **sketch** is a rough drawing from memory, not to scale.

## 🧩 Three components of a map
1. **Distance (scale):** tells how much real distance one unit on the map stands for – e.g. **1 cm = 10 km**.
2. **Direction:** most maps show an arrow pointing to **North**. The four **cardinal directions** are North, South, East and West. In between are NE, SE, SW and NW.
3. **Symbols:** small signs and colours that stand for real things. A **key (legend)** explains them.
- **Blue** – water bodies; **green** – plains/forests; **brown** – mountains; **yellow** – plateaus.

## 🌐 The globe
> 📌 A **globe** is a small **model of the Earth**. It shows the true shape of the Earth and the correct positions of continents and oceans.
The Earth spins on an imaginary **axis** joining the **North Pole** and **South Pole**. The axis is tilted.

## ➖ Latitudes
> 📌 **Latitudes** (parallels) are imaginary circles running **east–west**, parallel to the Equator.
- The **Equator (0°)** divides Earth into the **Northern** and **Southern Hemispheres**.
- Latitudes go from 0° to **90° N** (North Pole) and **90° S** (South Pole).
- Important ones: **Tropic of Cancer (23½° N)**, **Tropic of Capricorn (23½° S)**, **Arctic Circle (66½° N)**, **Antarctic Circle (66½° S)**.
**Heat zones:**
- **Torrid zone** (between the tropics) – hottest; the Sun is overhead at some time of the year.
- **Temperate zones** – moderate.
- **Frigid zones** (near the poles) – very cold.
> 💡 The Tropic of Cancer passes through the middle of India!

## 🧭 Longitudes
> 📌 **Longitudes** (meridians) are imaginary lines running from the **North Pole to the South Pole**.
- The **Prime Meridian (0°)** passes through **Greenwich** near London. It divides Earth into the **Eastern** and **Western Hemispheres**.
- Longitudes go from 0° to **180° E** and **180° W**.
> 😮 Long before Greenwich, Indian astronomers used a **prime meridian through Ujjain** (the "Madhya Rekha") to calculate time!

## ⏰ Longitudes and time
The Earth turns **360° in 24 hours**, so **15° = 1 hour** (1° = 4 minutes). Places to the east see the Sun first, so their clocks are ahead.
- India uses **Indian Standard Time (IST)**, based on **82½° E**, which passes near Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh).
- IST is **5 hours 30 minutes ahead** of Greenwich time.
- The **International Date Line** (about 180°) is where the date changes.

## ✋ Try it yourself
Draw a map of your classroom with a north arrow, a scale (1 cm = 1 m) and symbols for desks, door and windows. Add a key!

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Mixing up latitude and longitude. **Lat**itudes lie **flat** (east–west); longitudes are **long** (pole to pole).
- Forgetting that maps need a scale, direction and key.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Maps have distance (scale), direction and symbols.
- Latitudes: Equator, tropics, polar circles → heat zones.
- Longitudes: Prime Meridian; 15° = 1 hour; IST = 82½° E.

## 📖 New words
- **Scale** – ratio of map distance to real distance
- **Hemisphere** – half of the Earth
- **Meridian** – a line of longitude

## 🎬 Let's begin
Astronauts call Earth the **"Blue Planet"** – because from space, most of it looks blue! That's water – huge **oceans**. The land is divided into giant pieces called **continents**. Let's take a world tour! 🌏

## 🤔 Land and water on Earth
> 📌 About **71%** of the Earth's surface is covered by **water** and only about **29%** by **land**.
- Large areas of land are **continents**.
- Huge bodies of salty water are **oceans**.
- The Northern Hemisphere has more land; the Southern has more water.

## 🌊 The five oceans (largest to smallest)
1. **Pacific Ocean** – the largest and deepest; the **Mariana Trench** (about 11 km deep) is here.
2. **Atlantic Ocean** – S-shaped; busy shipping routes between Europe and the Americas.
3. **Indian Ocean** – the only ocean named after a country – **India**! It's warm and important for trade.
4. **Southern Ocean** – surrounds Antarctica; very cold.
5. **Arctic Ocean** – the smallest; around the North Pole; mostly frozen.

## 🗺️ The seven continents (largest to smallest)
1. **Asia** – largest and most populated; has India, China; the Himalayas and Mount Everest.
2. **Africa** – has the **Sahara**, the largest hot desert, and the **Nile**, one of the longest rivers. The Equator passes through its middle.
3. **North America** – Canada, USA, Mexico; joined to South America by a narrow strip (isthmus of Panama).
4. **South America** – the **Amazon** rainforest and river; the **Andes** mountains.
5. **Antarctica** – coldest; covered with thick ice; no permanent people, only research stations. India has stations **Maitri** and **Bharati**.
6. **Europe** – many small countries; joined to Asia (together called **Eurasia**).
7. **Australia** – smallest continent; also a country; called an **island continent**.

## 🌊 Why oceans matter
- Give us fish, salt, minerals and oil.
- Control climate and bring rain (the monsoon comes from the Indian Ocean!).
- Help trade by ships.
- Home to coral reefs and whales.
**Threats:** plastic waste, oil spills, overfishing.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Calling Australia only a country – it's a continent too.
- Thinking Antarctica is near the North Pole. It's at the **South** Pole.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- 71% water, 29% land.
- Oceans: Pacific (largest), Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic (smallest).
- Continents: Asia (largest), Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia (smallest).

## 📖 New words
- **Continent** – a very large landmass
- **Isthmus** – a narrow strip of land joining two larger landmasses
- **Trench** – a very deep, narrow valley in the ocean floor

## 🎬 Let's begin
Imagine living high in the Himalayas, on the flat banks of the Ganga, on the rocky Deccan plateau, in the Thar desert or by the sea in Kerala. Your food, clothes, house and job would all be different! The **shape of the land** shapes our **lives**. ⛰️🌾🏜️

## 🤔 What are landforms?
> 📌 **Landforms** are natural features of the Earth's surface, like mountains, plateaus, plains, valleys and deserts.
They are shaped over millions of years by **forces inside the Earth** and by **wind, water and ice** outside.

## ⛰️ Mountains
- Land rising much higher than its surroundings, with steep slopes and peaks.
- **Himalayas** – young, high fold mountains; **Mount Everest (8,849 m)** is the world's highest peak. **Aravallis** – among the oldest mountains.
**Life in mountains:** cold climate, thin air; terrace farming on slopes; people rear yaks, sheep and goats; houses with sloping roofs; tourism and pilgrimages. Rivers like the Ganga and Brahmaputra begin as glaciers here.

## 🟫 Plateaus
- Raised, flat-topped land with steep sides – like a **table**.
- **Deccan Plateau** (South India), **Chota Nagpur Plateau** (Jharkhand).
**Life on plateaus:** rich in **minerals** (iron, coal, mica) → mining; black soil good for cotton; waterfalls (Hundru Falls, Jog Falls) give hydroelectricity.

## 🌾 Plains
- Large stretches of flat, low land, mostly made by rivers depositing fertile soil (**alluvium**).
- **Indo-Gangetic Plains** – among the most fertile in the world.
**Life on plains:** farming (wheat, rice, sugarcane), many villages and big cities, easy to build roads and railways → **most people live in plains**.

## 🏜️ Deserts, 🏝️ coasts and islands
- **Thar Desert (Rajasthan):** little rain, sand dunes; camels, thorny plants; people store rainwater in tankas and wear loose, bright clothes.
- **Coasts:** fishing, ports, salt pans, coconut farming (Kerala, Goa).
- **Islands:** Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep – fishing and tourism.

## 🌍 Caring for landforms
Cutting forests on slopes causes **landslides**; too much mining harms plateaus; dumping waste spoils rivers. We must use land sustainably.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Mixing up plateaus and plains – both can be flat, but plateaus are **raised high** with steep sides.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Landforms: mountains, plateaus, plains, deserts, coasts, islands.
- Mountains: cold, terrace farming. Plateaus: minerals. Plains: fertile, crowded.
- Landforms shape how people live.

## 📖 New words
- **Terrace farming** – farming on steps cut into hill slopes
- **Alluvium** – fertile soil left by rivers
- **Plateau** – raised flat land

## 🎬 Let's begin
How do we know what people ate 5,000 years ago or what games children played? Nobody from that time is alive to tell us! Historians are like **detectives** – they piece together clues from the past. 🔍🏺

## 🤔 What is history?
> 📌 **History** is the study of the **past** – how people lived, what they did, believed and made – using **evidence**.

## ⏳ Timelines and counting time
A **timeline** shows events in order along a line.
- **BCE** (Before Common Era) – years before year 1. Counted **backwards**: 500 BCE is earlier than 200 BCE.
- **CE** (Common Era) – years from year 1 onwards. Counted forwards.
- There is **no year 0**!
- **Decade** = 10 years; **century** = 100 years; **millennium** = 1,000 years.
- The **21st century** = 2001 to 2100.
> 💡 Earlier writers used BC and AD; historians now use BCE and CE.

## 🧩 Sources of history
> 📌 **Sources** are the clues historians use to learn about the past.
**1. Archaeological sources** (found by digging – done by **archaeologists**):
- Tools, pottery, bones, ornaments, toys, buildings, coins, seals.
- **Inscriptions** – writing on stone, metal or pillars (like Ashoka's).
- **Rock paintings** – at **Bhimbetka** (Madhya Pradesh), some over 10,000 years old.
**2. Literary sources** (written):
- **Manuscripts** – handwritten books on **palm leaves** or **birch bark**.
- Religious texts, poems, plays, travellers' accounts.
**3. Oral traditions:**
- Stories, songs and knowledge passed down by **word of mouth** – like the Vedas, recited perfectly for generations.

## 🔬 How historians work
They **compare** different sources, ask who made them and why, date them using science (like carbon dating), and change their ideas when new evidence appears.

## 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Early humans
- Early humans were **hunter-gatherers** – hunted animals and gathered fruits, roots and honey.
- They made **stone tools**, used **fire**, lived in caves and rock shelters, and later began **farming** and settling in villages (like **Mehrgarh**, about 9,000 years ago).

## ✋ Try it yourself

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking 300 BCE comes after 200 BCE. In BCE, bigger numbers are **earlier**.
- Thinking history is only about kings. It's about ordinary people too!

## 🧠 Quick recap
- History studies the past using evidence.
- Timelines: BCE (backwards), CE (forwards); decade, century, millennium.
- Sources: archaeological, literary, oral.

## 📖 New words
- **Archaeologist** – a person who digs and studies remains of the past
- **Inscription** – writing carved on stone or metal
- **Manuscript** – a handwritten book

## 🎬 Let's begin
What's the name of our country? You might say **India** – or **Bharat** – or **Hindustan**! Each name has a fascinating story going back thousands of years. Let's discover them. 🇮🇳

## 🤔 "India, that is Bharat"
> 📌 The very first article of our **Constitution** (Article 1) says: "**India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.**"
So both **India** and **Bharat** are official names of our country.

## 🧩 Names through history
**1. Bharata / Bharatavarsha**
- The **Rig Veda** mentions the **Bharatas**, a people living in the north-west.
- Later texts like the Vishnu Purana describe **Bharatavarsha** as the land north of the ocean and south of the Himalayas.
- Many traditions link the name to King **Bharata**.

**2. Jambudvipa**
- "The island of the jambu (jamun) tree" – an old name used in ancient texts and even in Emperor **Ashoka's** inscriptions.

**3. Hindu / Hind / Hindustan**
- The ancient Persians (Iranians) said "**Hindu**" for the river **Sindhu** (Indus) and the land beyond it. ("S" became "H" in their language.)
- Later, **Hind** and **Hindustan** were used by Persian and Arab writers and the Mughals.

**4. India**
- The Greeks took the word from the Persians and called the river **Indos** and the people **Indoi** – which became **India** in English.

**5. Other names**
- Chinese travellers like **Xuanzang** called it **Tianzhu** or **Yindu**.

## 🌏 Why names matter
Names carry history, memory and identity. Our country's many names show its long and rich past and its connections with the world.

- Bharata (Rig Veda), Jambudvipa (Ashoka), Hindu/Hind (Persian, from Sindhu), India (Greek Indos/Indoi), Tianzhu (Chinese).

## 📖 New words

## 🎬 Let's begin
About 5,000 years ago, while much of the world lived in small villages, people on the banks of the **Sindhu (Indus)** and **Sarasvati** rivers built well-planned cities with drains, bathrooms and wide roads! Welcome to the **Harappan civilisation**. 🏛️

## 🤔 What is a civilisation?
> 📌 A **civilisation** is an advanced society with **cities**, **organised government**, **trade**, **crafts**, **writing** and shared ways of life.
The **Harappan** (or **Sindhu–Sarasvati**) civilisation flourished around **2600–1900 BCE**. It is named after **Harappa**, the first site found (1921, now in Pakistan).

## 🏙️ Major cities
- **Harappa** and **Mohenjo-daro** (Pakistan)
- **Dholavira** and **Lothal** (Gujarat)
- **Rakhigarhi** (Haryana) – one of the largest sites
- **Kalibangan** (Rajasthan)

## ⚙️ Amazing town planning
- Roads in a neat **grid**, crossing at right angles.
- Houses of **baked bricks** of the same proportions; many had wells and bathrooms.
- A covered **drainage system** – among the best in the ancient world.
- **The Great Bath** at Mohenjo-daro – a large waterproof tank.
- **Dholavira** had huge **reservoirs** to store rainwater – smart water management in a dry land!
- **Lothal** had a **dockyard** for boats.

## 🧩 Life and work
- **Farming:** wheat, barley, pulses, cotton; they kept cattle, sheep and goats.
- **Crafts:** beads, pottery, bronze figures (the "**Dancing Girl**"), weights and measures.
- **Seals:** small stone seals with animals (like the **unicorn**) and a **script** that **nobody has been able to read yet**.
- **Trade:** with Mesopotamia (Iraq) by sea and land.
> 😮 Some Harappan practices – like bangles, a game like chess-board dice, and the namaste gesture seen on figurines – still continue in India today!

## 📉 Decline
Around 1900 BCE, cities slowly declined. Likely reasons: the **Sarasvati river dried up**, **climate change** reduced rainfall, floods, and trade fell. People moved to smaller villages.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking the Harappan script has been deciphered. It hasn't – it's still a mystery!

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Harappan / Sindhu–Sarasvati civilisation, about 2600–1900 BCE.
- Cities: Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Lothal, Rakhigarhi, Kalibangan.
- Grid roads, drains, Great Bath, reservoirs, dockyard, seals, trade.
- Decline due to drying rivers and climate change.

## 📖 New words
- **Civilisation** – an advanced society with cities and organised life
- **Seal** – a small carved stone used to stamp marks
- **Reservoir** – a large tank for storing water

## 🎬 Let's begin
Imagine a tree. Its **roots** go deep into the ground and its branches spread wide. India's culture is like that tree – its **roots** are ancient ideas from the Vedas, the Upanishads, Buddhism, Jainism and countless folk and tribal traditions. 🌳

## 📜 The Vedas
> 📌 The **Vedas** are among the world's oldest texts. There are four: **Rig Veda, Sama Veda, Yajur Veda, Atharva Veda**.
- The Rig Veda has over 1,000 hymns praising nature and deities like **Agni** (fire), **Indra**, **Varuna** and **Ushas** (dawn).
- They were passed down **orally** for thousands of years with amazing accuracy, using special memory techniques. UNESCO recognises this **Vedic chanting** as a world heritage.
- Ideas: respect for nature, truth (**satya**), order in the universe (**rita**), and asking big questions about how the world began.

## 🤔 The Upanishads – big questions
- Thinkers asked: Who am I? What is real? What happens after death?
- Key ideas: **Brahman** (the ultimate reality) and **Atman** (the self) are one.
- Famous sayings: "**Asato mā sadgamaya**" – lead me from untruth to truth.

## ☸️ Buddhism
- Founded by **Siddhartha Gautama** (the **Buddha**, "the awakened one"), born in **Lumbini**; attained enlightenment at **Bodh Gaya**; gave his first sermon at **Sarnath**.
- He taught that life has suffering caused by **desire**, and that we can end it by following the **Middle Path** – not too much luxury, not too much hardship.
- Values: **kindness, compassion, non-violence**, right thinking and right action.

## 🕊️ Jainism
- **Vardhamana Mahavira**, the 24th **Tirthankara**, taught it widely.
- Key ideas: **ahimsa** (non-violence to all living beings), **satya** (truth), **aparigraha** (not being greedy), and **anekantavada** – truth has **many sides**, so respect others' views.

## 🎭 Folk and tribal traditions
Tribal and folk communities have deep respect for **nature** – sacred groves, worship of rivers, mountains and trees, and rich songs, dances and art (like **Warli** and **Gond** painting).

## 🌳 Common threads
Despite differences, these traditions share values: **respect for all life, truth, self-control, and harmony with nature**. They influenced each other and still shape Indian life today.

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking the Vedas were first written down. They were **memorised and recited** for centuries before being written.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Vedas (4) – oral tradition, nature and truth.
- Upanishads – big questions about the self and reality.
- Buddha – Middle Path, compassion. Mahavira – ahimsa, anekantavada.
- Folk and tribal traditions – harmony with nature.

## 📖 New words
- **Ahimsa** – non-violence
- **Enlightenment** – true understanding (for the Buddha)
- **Oral tradition** – knowledge passed by speaking and memorising

## 🎬 Let's begin
Take a train across India and every few hours the language, food, clothes and music change! Yet when India plays a cricket final or celebrates Independence Day, a billion hearts beat as one. This is the magic of **"unity in diversity"**. 🎉

## 🤔 What is diversity?
> 📌 **Diversity** means **variety** – differences in language, food, dress, festivals, religion and customs.
> 📌 **Unity** means feeling **one** – sharing values, history and belonging to one nation.

## 🍛 Diversity in food
Rice dominates in the east and south, wheat in the north-west; millets in dry regions. Yet **khichdi** is cooked almost everywhere in some form, and spices like turmeric and pepper are used across India.

## 👗 Diversity in clothing
The **saree** is draped in more than **80 different ways** across India – Nivi (Andhra), Nauvari (Maharashtra), Mekhela Chador (Assam), Coorgi style (Karnataka). Men wear dhoti, lungi, mundu, kurta-pyjama, with different names everywhere.

## 🎊 Diversity in festivals
Harvest festivals are celebrated all over India under different names: **Pongal** (Tamil Nadu), **Makar Sankranti**, **Lohri** (Punjab), **Bihu** (Assam), **Onam** (Kerala), **Baisakhi**. Same idea – thanking nature for the harvest!

## 🗣️ Diversity in languages
India has **22 scheduled languages** and hundreds of others. Many share words and grammar from common roots.

## 📖 The epics unite us
The **Ramayana** and **Mahabharata** are told in many versions – **Kamba Ramayanam** (Tamil), **Ramcharitmanas** (Awadhi, by Tulsidas), **Krittivasi Ramayan** (Bengali), and even in Southeast Asia (Thailand's Ramakien). The story is shared; each region adds its own colour.

## 🧵 What unites us?
- A shared **history** and freedom struggle.
- Common **values**: respect for elders, hospitality ("**Atithi Devo Bhava**" – the guest is like God), care for nature.
- Pilgrimages that connect all corners – from Badrinath to Rameswaram.
- The **Constitution**, the national flag, anthem and song.

## ✋ Try it yourself

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking diversity divides us. It actually makes India richer and stronger when we respect it.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Diversity: food, clothing, festivals, languages.
- Unity: shared history, values, epics, Constitution.
- India celebrates "many in the one".

## 📖 New words
- **Diversity** – variety
- **Epic** – a long story-poem about heroes
- **Hospitality** – welcoming guests warmly

## 🎬 Let's begin
When you fall sick, who brings you soup? When there's a wedding in your lane, who comes to help? **Family** and **community** – the people who make us feel we **belong**. 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦🏘️

## 🤔 What is a family?
> 📌 A **family** is a group of people related by birth, marriage or adoption, who live together and care for one another.
- **Joint (extended) family:** grandparents, parents, uncles, aunts and cousins live together.
- **Nuclear family:** parents and their children.
Many Indian families are joint; many are nuclear; all are special!

## 🧩 Roles in a family
- Elders guide, tell stories and pass on traditions.
- Parents earn, cook, care and teach.
- Children study, help with chores and care for younger siblings and elders.
Family members **share work** – cooking, cleaning, shopping – and **support** each other in good and bad times.

## 💛 Values we learn in a family

## 🎬 Let's begin
Imagine a cricket match with no rules – everyone bats at once, no one keeps score! Chaos! A country is like a giant team; it needs **rules** and people to make sure they are followed. That is the job of **government**. 🏏📜

## 🤔 What is governance?
> 📌 **Governance** is the process of making and carrying out decisions and rules for a group, a community or a country.
> 📌 The **government** is the group of people with the authority to make and enforce laws and run the country.

## 🧩 Why do we need a government?
- To make **laws** and keep **order** and peace.
- To **protect** the country's borders and citizens.
- To provide **public services**: schools, hospitals, roads, water, electricity, railways.
- To help in **disasters** and support the poor.

## 🏛️ Three organs of government
1. **Legislature** – **makes laws** (Parliament at the centre; Legislative Assemblies in states).
2. **Executive** – **carries out** laws and runs the government (Prime Minister, ministers, officials, police).
3. **Judiciary** – **interprets** laws and settles **disputes** (courts, with the Supreme Court at the top).
> 💡 This is called **separation of powers** – each organ checks the others so no one becomes too powerful.

## 🗺️ Levels of government
- **Central (Union) government** – for the whole country (defence, railways, currency).
- **State governments** – for each state (police, health, agriculture).
- **Local governments** – for villages and towns (water, sanitation, local roads).

## 🗳️ Democracy
> 📌 In a **democracy**, people **elect** their representatives through **voting**. If people are unhappy, they can vote for others in the next election.
India is the **world's largest democracy**. Every citizen aged **18 and above** can vote – this is **universal adult franchise**.

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Governance = making and carrying out decisions.
- Three organs: legislature (makes), executive (implements), judiciary (interprets).
- Three levels: central, state, local.
- Democracy: people choose leaders by voting; voting age 18.

## 📖 New words
- **Legislature** – law-making body
- **Executive** – law-implementing body
- **Judiciary** – courts

## 🎬 Let's begin
In a village, the hand-pump is broken and the road floods every monsoon. Who will fix them? Not the Prime Minister in Delhi – but the village's own elected body, the **Panchayat**! This is **democracy at the grassroots**. 🌾

## 🤔 What is Panchayati Raj?
> 📌 **Panchayati Raj** is the system of **local self-government** in rural India, where villagers elect their own representatives to manage local matters.
It got constitutional status through the **73rd Amendment (1992)**.

## 🧩 Three levels (tiers)
1. **Gram Panchayat** – village level
2. **Panchayat Samiti** (Block Panchayat) – block level, a group of villages
3. **Zila Parishad** – district level

## 🗣️ Gram Sabha
> 📌 The **Gram Sabha** is the meeting of **all adults (18+)** in a village who are voters.
- It discusses village problems and plans.
- It approves the Panchayat's work and budget and keeps it **accountable**.

## 🏛️ Gram Panchayat
- Headed by the **Sarpanch** (Pradhan, Mukhiya), elected by villagers.
- Members are called **Panchs** (ward members).
- A **Secretary** (appointed by the government) keeps records and calls meetings.
- Term: **5 years**.
**Work:** building and repairing roads, wells, drains, ponds and school buildings; street lighting; birth and death registration; running government schemes; collecting local taxes.

## 👩 Women and fairness
At least **one-third** of seats are reserved for **women**, and many states reserve **half**. Seats are also reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, so everyone has a voice.

- Panchayati Raj = rural local self-government (73rd Amendment, 1992).
- Three tiers: Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad.
- Gram Sabha = all adult voters; Sarpanch heads the Panchayat.
- One-third or more seats for women.

Who collects the garbage from your street every morning, fixes the streetlights, and keeps the water flowing in city taps? In towns and cities, it's the **municipality** – local government for urban areas! 🏙️🚮

## 🤔 What is urban local government?
> 📌 **Urban local bodies** manage services in **towns and cities**. They got constitutional status through the **74th Amendment (1992)**.

- The **Mayor** is the head of a Municipal Corporation (the Chairperson heads a Council).
- The **Municipal Commissioner** (a government officer) and staff carry out the decisions.
- Seats are reserved for women, SC and ST.

- Water supply and drainage
- **Garbage collection** and cleanliness
- Streetlights, roads, footpaths, parks
- Hospitals, dispensaries, schools
- Birth and death certificates, building permissions

**Property tax**, water tax, taxes on shops and advertisements, fees, and funds from state and central governments.

## 🙋 Our role as citizens
Pay taxes, separate wet and dry waste, report problems (many cities have apps and helplines), keep public places clean, and vote in municipal elections.

## 🎬 Let's begin
Your cotton shirt has a long story: a **farmer** grew the cotton, a **factory** turned it into cloth and stitched the shirt, and a **shopkeeper** sold it to you, delivered by a **truck driver**. Different kinds of work linked in a chain! 👕🚚

## 🤔 What are the sectors of the economy?
> 📌 Economic activities are grouped into **three sectors**:
1. **Primary sector** – getting things **directly from nature**: farming, fishing, forestry, mining, animal rearing.
2. **Secondary sector** – **making (manufacturing)** goods from raw materials: factories making cloth, sugar, cars, furniture; construction.
3. **Tertiary (service) sector** – providing **services** that support the other sectors and people: transport, shops, banks, schools, hospitals, IT, tourism.

## 🔗 How sectors depend on each other
**Example – sugar:**
- Primary: a farmer grows **sugarcane**.
- Secondary: a mill turns it into **sugar**.
- Tertiary: trucks transport it, a bank gives loans, a shop sells it.
If farmers stop growing cane, the mill closes; if trucks stop, sugar can't reach shops. Every sector **needs** the others.

## 🇮🇳 Sectors in India
- Many people still work in the **primary sector** (agriculture).
- The **tertiary sector** (services like IT, banking, tourism) produces the largest share of India's income.
- Manufacturing ("Make in India") is growing.

## 🌍 Around us
In a village: farming, dairy, fishing (primary); a rice mill, pottery (secondary); a shop, a school, a bus (tertiary).
In a city: factories (secondary); offices, hospitals, malls, delivery apps (tertiary).

## ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Thinking a teacher or doctor produces nothing. They provide **services** – the tertiary sector!

## 🧠 Quick recap
- Primary: from nature. Secondary: manufacturing. Tertiary: services.
- Sectors are interdependent.
- India: many work in agriculture; services give the biggest income.

## 📖 New words
- **Sector** – a part or group of the economy
- **Manufacturing** – making goods in factories
- **Interdependent** – depending on each other
