## ✏️ Grammar corner: Adjectives and degrees of comparison
> 📌 An **adjective** describes a noun – it tells **what kind, how many, which** or **whose**.
**Types:** quality (brave, clever), quantity (some, little), number (two, few), demonstrative (this, those), possessive (my, their).
**Degrees of comparison:**
- **Positive** – brave, big, good
- **Comparative** (compares two) – braver, bigger, better
- **Superlative** (compares three or more) – bravest, biggest, best
**Rules:**
- Short words: add **-er/-est** (tall → taller → tallest).
- Words ending in **e**: add **-r/-st** (brave → braver → bravest).
- Consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last letter (big → bigger → biggest).
- Long words: use **more/most** (more beautiful, most beautiful).
- Irregular: good–better–best, bad–worse–worst, many–more–most.

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Verbs
> 📌 A **verb** is a word that shows an **action** (run, eat) or a **state** (is, seem, belong).
**Types:**
- **Action verbs:** jump, write, help.
- **Linking verbs:** connect the subject to more information – is, am, are, was, seem, become. *They **are** friends.*

- **Transitive verbs** have an object: *He **kicked** the ball.*
- **Intransitive verbs** don't need one: *The baby **slept**.*

## 🎵 How to read a poem
1. Read it **aloud**, slowly, at least twice.
2. Notice the **rhyme** (words at line ends that sound alike) and **rhythm** (the beat).
3. Find the **speaker** – who is talking, and to whom?
4. Pick out lines that show **feelings**.
5. Ask: what is the **central idea**?

## 🧩 Parts of a poem
- **Line** – a single row of words.
- **Stanza** – a group of lines, like a paragraph in a poem.
- **Rhyme scheme** – the pattern of rhymes, shown by letters: aabb, abab.
- **Refrain** – a line or phrase that repeats.

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Adverbs
> 📌 An **adverb** tells us more about a **verb**, an **adjective** or another **adverb**.
**Types:**
- **Manner (how):** gently, kindly, happily – *She smiled **kindly**.*
- **Time (when):** now, soon, always, yesterday – *Come **soon**.*
- **Place (where):** here, there, everywhere – *Friends are **everywhere**.*
- **Frequency (how often):** always, often, never, sometimes.
- **Degree (how much):** very, too, quite, almost – *She is **very** kind.*
**Tip:** Many adverbs end in **-ly** (quick → quickly), but not all (fast, well, soon).

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Prepositions
> 📌 A **preposition** shows the **relationship** of a noun or pronoun to another word – often **place**, **time** or **direction**.
**Types:**
- **Place:** in, on, under, between, behind, beside – *The cat is **under** the chair.*

**Common mix-ups:**
- **between** (two) vs **among** (more than two).

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Conjunctions
> 📌 A **conjunction** is a **joining word**. It joins words, phrases or sentences.
**Types:**
- **Coordinating conjunctions** join equal parts – remember **FANBOYS**: **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o. *Neem is bitter **but** useful.*
- **Subordinating conjunctions** join a main idea to a less important one – because, although, when, if, since, unless, while. *We plant trees **because** they give us oxygen.*
- **Correlative conjunctions** come in pairs – either…or, neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also. ***Both** the leaves **and** the bark are useful.*

## 🧩 Spices and their benefits

- **Cinnamon (dalchini):** warm, sweet spice; used in kadha and sweets.
- **Fenugreek (methi)** and **fennel (saunf):** help digestion; saunf is chewed after meals.
> ⚠️ Spices help in small amounts, but they are **not a replacement for a doctor's medicine** when someone is really ill.

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Articles
> 📌 **Articles** are small words used before nouns: **a, an** (indefinite) and **the** (definite).
- **A** – before words starting with a **consonant sound**: a spice, a university (sounds like "you").
- **An** – before words starting with a **vowel sound**: an onion, an hour (h is silent).
- **The** – for something **specific** or already mentioned, and for unique things: the Sun, the Himalayas. *I bought **a** spice box. **The** box is red.*
- **No article** with general plural or uncountable nouns: Spices are healthy. Milk is white.

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Subject–verb agreement
> 📌 The **verb** must **agree** with its **subject** in number.
**Rules:**
- Singular subject → singular verb: *The runner **runs** fast.*
- Plural subject → plural verb: *The runners **run** fast.*
- Two subjects joined by **and** → plural: *Ravi and Meena **are** friends.*
- **Each, every, everyone, nobody** → singular: *Everyone **wants** to win.*
- With **either…or / neither…nor**, the verb agrees with the nearer subject: *Neither the coach nor the players **were** late.*
- Collective nouns usually take singular verbs: *The team **is** ready.*

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Present continuous tense
> 📌 The **present continuous** shows an action **happening now** or a fixed **plan** for the future.

## 🏛️ About the memorial
- Located near **India Gate, New Delhi**; inaugurated in **February 2019**.
- It honours soldiers of the Indian Armed Forces who gave their lives defending the nation after Independence.
- Its design has **four concentric circles (chakras)**:
  1. **Amar Chakra** – the circle of immortality, with the **eternal flame** (Amar Jawan Jyoti).
  2. **Veerta Chakra** – the circle of bravery, with murals of famous battles.
  3. **Tyag Chakra** – the circle of sacrifice, with granite tablets bearing soldiers' names.
  4. **Rakshak Chakra** – the circle of protection, lined with trees standing like soldiers guarding the nation.
- **Param Yodha Sthal** has busts of soldiers awarded the **Param Vir Chakra**, India's highest gallantry award.

## ✏️ Grammar corner: Active and passive voice (introduction)
> 📌 In the **active voice**, the subject **does** the action. In the **passive voice**, the subject **receives** the action.
- Active: *The Prime Minister **inaugurated** the memorial.*
- Passive: *The memorial **was inaugurated** by the Prime Minister.*
**Form of passive:** object becomes subject + **is/are/was/were** + **past participle** (+ by + doer).
Use passive when the doer is unknown or less important: *The names **are carved** on granite.*
